# Projecting a weight family gives new Hecke eigenvalues

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E$ be a CM field, $a\ge1$ and $G$ the standard totally definite unitary group in $a$ variables. Fix a prime $\ell$ and an isomorphism $\iota\colon\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\simeq\mathbb C$, and use the transported $\ell$-adic norm on $\mathbb C$. Let $w_0,v'\mid\ell$ be split in $E/E^+$ and lie above different places of $E^+$. Assume that $\iota^{-1}\tau_0$ induces $v'$ for the chosen embedding $\tau_0\colon E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$. Let $K$ be a level away from $w_0,\bar w_0$ containing the supported hyperspecial subgroup at $v',\bar v'$, let $K_v$ be open in $\mathrm{GL}_a(E_{v'})$ and set $K'=K\cap c_{v'}^{-1}(K_v)$.

Take an automorphic representation $U$ of $G$ of algebraic weight $\lambda$ with a nonzero $K'$-fixed vector. Consider any family $T_j$, $j\in J$, given by finite complex linear combinations of right translations. Assume the operators are locally $\ell$-integral for weight $\lambda$ in the sense of `IntL`, and that their supports are finite adeles with trivial coordinates at both pairs $w_0,\bar w_0$ and $v',\bar v'$. Assume $T_j$ acts by the scalar $\eta(V)_j$ on the $K'$-fixed vectors of each automorphic summand $V$. The local integrality input gives a common frame of coefficient vectors $b(g,i)$ with three properties. It detects the zero form, the evaluations of each algebraic form of weight $\lambda$ are bounded in the transported norm, and each $T_j$ is a contraction on the evaluations $F\mapsto F(b(g,i))(g)$. These properties apply to all linear maps from the coefficient space to functions, for the contraction, and to algebraic forms, for boundedness and detection. [Exact weight-changing statement](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/CongruenceOddLevel/WtDefs.lean#L13) · [Local integrality](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/SeparatingAlgebras/CAllFin.lean#L129) · [Frame construction](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/SeparatingAlgebras/FrameW.lean#L14)

For every $\varepsilon>0$ there are finitely many automorphic representations $V$ of weights $\mu_V$ with nonzero $K$-fixed vectors, and coefficients $c_V\in\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$, such that the following holds for every integer polynomial $P$ in the $J$-indexed variables:
$$
\left|P(\iota^{-1}\eta(U))-\sum_V c_V P(\iota^{-1}\eta(V))\right|_\ell
\le \varepsilon .
$$
The weights stay equal to $\lambda$ away from $\tau_0$ and its conjugate. At $\tau_0$, the weight satisfies `GapWt`. This means that its shifted infinitesimal parameters give $a$ distinct integral jumps $J_0<\cdots<J_{a-1}$ with $J_{i+1}-J_0>a(J_i-J_0)$ for each $i<a-1$. The same finite sum works for all $P$, even when the indexing set $J$ is infinite. [Lacunarity predicate](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/CongruenceOddLevel/WtDefs.lean#L27) · [Top-level proof](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/WeightCharacters/TopOf.lean#L16)

## Irreducible components of a family

Choose a nonzero algebraic form $f$ of weight $\lambda$ fixed by $K'$ whose coefficient functions belong to $U$. Since the evaluations detect zero forms, divide $f$ by one nonzero evaluation to make that evaluation $1$. Use the [coset-polynomial family construction](coset-polynomials-change-the-weight-at-one-infinite-place.md) with this $f$, the same frame and tolerance $\varepsilon$. It gives the degree error $d$ before the target weight $\nu$ is chosen. The resulting family $B$ is $K$-fixed and supported in a finite-dimensional subspace of the automorphic functions. [Choice and normalization of $f$](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/WeightCharacters/TopOf.lean#L19)

Here is why one can decompose the whole family at once. In this setting, the positive invariant Petersson pairing gives an algebraic sum of irreducible automorphic representations on the space of automorphic forms. Each of a finite basis of the span of the family lies in a finite sum of summands. Thus one finite sum contains every value $B(y_1,y_2)v$. One can take the sum direct by removing a summand whenever it meets the sum of previously chosen summands, since the intersection is invariant and the summand is irreducible. The projection $p_V$ to a member of the direct sum commutes with right translation there. Write $B_V=p_V B$. The archimedean and upper triangular covariances of $B$ then hold for $B_V$ as well. The Laurent degree bounds are retained because point evaluations span the dual of the finite-dimensional range of $B$: a vector annihilated by every evaluation is the zero function. Thus evaluation after $p_V$ is a finite linear combination of original evaluations on the range. [Finite direct sum](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/WeightCharacters/SumRep.lean#L12) · [Projection preserves the family properties](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/WeightCharacters/FamProj.lean#L11)

If $B_V$ is nonzero, $V^K\ne0$. Away from the archimedean place of $\tau_0$, the corresponding compact unitary factor acts trivially on the matrix arguments of $B_V$. By covariance, $B_V(y_1,y_2)$ is an intertwiner from the restriction of the original coefficient representation to this factor. Choose $y_1,y_2$ for which the intertwiner is nonzero and freeze tensor factors other than this place at a pure tensor on which it is nonzero. The remaining source is the irreducible highest-weight module for the original local weight. Since $V$ has a single weight there, it must be that original weight, including its conjugate at the other embedding. [Weights away from the chosen pair](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/WeightCharacters/FamOffHolds.lean#L15)

## Bounding the chosen component weight

Let $\kappa=\mu_V(\tau_0)$. For each $k$, the first $k$ diagonal coordinates at the chosen place define the circle $t_k(z)$. The largest exponent on the irreducible weight-$\kappa$ module is $s_\kappa(k)=\sum_{i<k}\kappa_i$, attained by the highest-weight vector. The upper bound on all exponents follows from the leading-minor realization of the module, whose highest-weight vector and translates satisfy the same exponent bound as the matrix coefficient in the family construction. Equivalently, a weight's exponent on this circle is bounded by the corresponding partial sum of the dominant weight. [Highest exponents](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/WeightCharacters/SqExpH.lean#L43) · [Detecting extreme exponents on a nonzero orbit](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/WeightCharacters/ExistsDual.lean#L43)

For the upper bound on $s_\kappa(k)$, project equivariantly from $V$ to a copy of the highest-weight module for the compact unitary factor, with nonzero value on some family vector. Such a projection exists by decomposition for this factor. The images of all family vectors form a stable set by the covariance, so their span is the whole irreducible module. All matrix coefficients of these images have exponents at most $s_\nu(k)+d$, by the family bound and the argument with evaluation functionals. Since the images span the module, the highest-weight vector gives $s_\kappa(k)\le s_\nu(k)+d$.

For the lower bound we first find a nonzero family vector with first parameter $1$. Write any $y_1$ giving a nonzero vector as $ub$, with $u$ unitary and $b$ upper triangular. The upper triangular factor multiplies by a nonzero scalar, and the archimedean covariance allows the factor $u$ to move out of the matrix arguments by an invertible right translation. Thus $B_V(1,y_2)v\ne0$ for suitable $y_2,v$. For this vector, right translation by $t_k(z)$ multiplies the family with first argument $1$ by $z^{s_\nu(k)}$, by upper triangular covariance. Its matrix coefficients have exponents at least $s_\nu(k)-d$, by the lower family bound. At least one of these coefficients is nonzero at $z=1$ after projection to a weight-$\kappa$ module. Since all exponents in that module are at most $s_\kappa(k)$, we obtain $s_\nu(k)-d\le s_\kappa(k)$. [Both partial-sum inequalities](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/WeightCharacters/FamSum.lean#L12)

Taking the difference of two consecutive partial sums gives $|\kappa_i-\nu_i|\le 2d$ for every $i$. Choose
$$
N=(a+1)(4d+a)+1,\qquad \nu_i=-N(a+1)^i\quad(0\le i<a).
$$
The [gap estimate](a-deep-weight-shift-forces-the-required-gap-and-generic-tests.md) shows that every $\kappa$ within $2d$ of $\nu$ has the required separated jumps. [Coordinate bounds](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/WeightCharacters/FamOffHolds.lean#L52) · [Choice of the target weight](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/WeightCharacters/TopOf.lean#L28)

## Returning to the Hecke eigenvalues

For each nonzero $B_V$, define $F_V(v)(g)=B_V(M(g),M(g))v(g)$, using the map supplied by the family construction. For a right translation by an element $h$ in the support of $T_j$, one has $M(gh)=M(g)$ because $h$ has trivial coordinate at $v'$. In the sum defining $(T_jF_V)(v)(g)$ all matrix arguments can therefore be held fixed at $M(g)$. The original family value in $V^K\subseteq V^{K'}$ is an eigenvector with eigenvalue $\eta(V)_j$, so $T_jF_V=\eta(V)_jF_V$. This deduction only uses the diagonalized functions as eigenvectors on the space with the frame evaluations. [Eigenvector after diagonal evaluation](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/WeightCharacters/TopOf.lean#L61)

The diagonal estimate now says that $f-\sum_V F_V$ is within $\varepsilon$ of zero on every frame evaluation. The [common contraction estimate](a-small-vector-error-gives-one-approximation-for-all-hecke-polynomials.md) bounds the evaluation after any integer polynomial in the operators by the same $\varepsilon$. Evaluate at the coordinate where $f=1$. With $c_V$ the transported evaluation there of $F_V$, this proves the desired inequality. If several $V$ have identical systems on $J$, add their coefficients. [Uniform polynomial estimate](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/WeightCharacters/TopOf.lean#L84)
