# A surviving cofactor forces a dual Selmer class

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $A$ be the finite local coefficient algebra of the [nilpotent line](a-unit-cofactor-survives-on-a-finite-nilpotent-line.md), with $I_0=(\ell^\nu)\mathfrak m_O$ acting by zero, $\nu>0$, and line parameter $t$. Fix the signature and admissible two-block auxiliary family at this specialization. Let $Q$ be all places in the auxiliary pairs. Let $T$ denote the fixed exceptional places where the ordinary local Selmer condition is also fully relaxed. Both sets are away from $\ell$. Let $\Psi$ be a square matrix over $A$ with $\det\Psi=0$ and such that, for each finite $A$-module $V$, $\ker(\Psi:V^a\to V^a)$ computes the ordinary specialized Selmer group $S(V)$. Suppose that an entry $C_{bj}$ of $C=\operatorname{adj}(\Psi)$ divides $t^h$ and that $\ell^\nu t^{2h+1}\ne0$, where $h=(e+1)w$ in the line specialization. Then there is a dual Selmer class on $A^r$ that is not killed by $c=\ell^\nu t^h$. The action on $A^r$ here is the Tate-dual matrix action with the inverse scalar twist.

The local and global inputs have the following precise roles. For finite $A$-modules $V,W$ with a perfect balanced additive pairing $\beta:V\times W\to\mathbb Z/\ell^{\nu+1}$, let $S^*(W;\beta)$ be the dual group: its classes are unramified off $\ell,T,Q$, are locally zero at $T\cup Q$, and at $\ell$ annihilate the ordinary finite local conditions on $V$. At the split auxiliary places and fixed exceptional places, $t$ kills the local invariants of the dual on $A^r$. Globally the dual has no invariants on any coefficient module. The ordinary finite local conditions at $\ell$ map onto their counterparts for surjective coefficient maps. Finally, the finite-coefficient Poitou–Tate count in this setup is
$$
|S(V)|=|S^*(W;\beta)|\prod_{v\in Q}|H^0(G_v,W^r)|,
$$
where the $H^0$ use the same dual action. The fixed places $T$ contribute a factor $1$, and the product of the ordinary local size-to-invariant ratios at $\ell$ cancels the complex-place factor. This is the count `OrderFormula`, with these hypotheses supplied by the finite local cardinality and duality results used in `orderFormula`.

Take $Y=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(A,\mathbb Z/\ell^{\nu+1})$, the additive character module with action $(a\lambda)(b)=\lambda(ab)$, and pair it with $A$ by evaluation. This pairing is perfect since $A$ is finite and killed by $\ell^{\nu+1}$. Put $s=ct$. The subgroup $A[s]=\ker(s:A\to A)$ has perfect pairing with $Y/sY$. Indeed its annihilator in $Y$ is $sY$, by dualizing multiplication by $s$ on the finite additive group. Invariants on $A[s]^r$ agree with those on $A^r$ at every $v\in Q$ since $t$, hence $s$, kills the latter.

Suppose every class in $S^*(A;\beta)$ is killed by $c$. Represent one by a cocycle $y$, so $cy=\delta m$. Modulo $cA^r$, the vector $m$ is invariant, and the global absence of invariants on $A/cA$ gives $m=cm'$. The cocycle $y_0=y-\delta m'$ takes values in $A[c]^r\subseteq A[s]^r$. Its class over $A[s]$ is still in the dual Selmer condition with respect to $Y/sY$. Off $\ell,T,Q$, inertia acts trivially, so subtracting the coboundary preserves vanishing there. At a place of $T\cup Q$, write $y=\delta m_v$ locally. Since $cy_0=0$, $c(m_v-m')$ is invariant and $t$ kills it. Thus $m_v-m'$ belongs to $A[s]^r$, giving a coboundary already over $A[s]$. At a place above $\ell$, every ordinary finite class over $Y/sY$ lifts to one over $Y$. Its cup product with $y_0$ is the original orthogonal cup product, by compatibility of the pairings and invariance under adding a coboundary. The class of $y_0$ maps to that of $y$ over $A$.

The image on dual Selmer classes is therefore onto. Applying the order formula to both coefficient pairs and using the equal $H^0$ factors gives
$$
|\ker(\Psi:Y^a\to Y^a)|\le|\ker(\Psi:(Y/sY)^a\to(Y/sY)^a)|.
$$
For each finite endomorphism, the kernel and cokernel have equal cardinality. The coefficient projection induces a surjection between the two cokernels, which the inequality forces to be an isomorphism. Hence $sY^a\subseteq \Psi Y^a$. Since $C\Psi=0$, row $b$ of $C$ annihilates this submodule, giving $s C_{bj}\,Y=0$. Evaluation on $Y$ separates elements of $A$, so $s C_{bj}=0$. But $t^h=C_{bj}u$ for some $u\in A$, and multiplication by $u$ gives $s t^h=\ell^\nu t^{2h+1}\ne0$. This contradiction supplies the required dual class. It is the same adjugate entry whose column gives the ordinary class, via $\Psi C=0$.
