# Self-dual Haar rescalings cancel in the adelic Gram mean

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $F$ be a totally real number field, $E/F$ a quadratic CM extension, $W$ a nondegenerate Hermitian $E$-space of rank $n$, and $j\ge1$ with $2j<n$. Choose rational coordinates over $F$ on $W^j$ and on the $F$-space of Hermitian $j$-by-$j$ matrices, as in the [coordinate Gram mean](unfolding-the-first-vector-inducts-on-the-gram-rank.md). The scalar coordinate measures give finite integer rings mass one and $\mathcal O_F$ covolume one at the real places taken together. Let $B_X$ be the trace of the Hermitian pairing on $W^j$, and $B_S(S,T)=\operatorname{tr}(ST)$ on the Hermitian target. Both are symmetric, nondegenerate $F$-bilinear forms. For the second, the diagonal coordinates pair by multiplication and each pair of off-diagonal coordinates pairs by the quadratic trace pairing, which is nondegenerate by separability.

Let $\psi$ be a nontrivial continuous character of $\mathbb A_F/F$. At every place replace both coordinate measures by the self-dual Haar measures for $\psi_v\circ B_X$ and $\psi_v\circ B_S$. Then for a fixed nondegenerate rational Gram target and any pure test, the product of the local fibre integrals is unchanged. In particular the coordinate mean gives the density statement with the actual self-dual measures of `SiegelDensities`. For nonnegative tests the same conclusion applies to the extended integral.

### A rational pairing and its self-dual measures

First take the standard additive character constructed from the rational local characters and the adelic trace. At finite $v$, if $\mathfrak D_{F,v}=\varpi_v^{d_v}\mathcal O_{F_v}$ is the local different, then the annihilator of the integer ring under the scalar multiplication pairing is $\mathfrak D_{F,v}^{-1}$. The [trace-dual calculation](the-different-and-euler-factors-give-the-product-of-local-densities.md) proves this directly. Haar Fourier inversion applied to the indicator of $\mathcal O_{F_v}$ then gives scalar self-dual measure $u_v=q_v^{-d_v/2}$ times the coordinate measure. At infinity the self-dual measure for the scalar pairing of the standard character is usual Lebesgue measure on the real product. The coordinate measure there is $|\operatorname{disc}F|^{-1/2}$ times Lebesgue measure, by the determinant of the real embedding matrix of any integral basis. Thus the infinite change factor is $u_\infty=|\operatorname{disc}F|^{1/2}$. Since $\prod_{v<\infty}q_v^{d_v}=|\operatorname{disc}F|$, these factors have product $1$, and all but finitely many finite factors are $1$.

Every nontrivial character of the additive adele quotient is a rational dilation of the standard character, by additive adelic duality: the annihilator of diagonal $F$ in the self-dual additive adeles is diagonal $F$. For the dilation by $a\in F^\times$, the self-dual measure factor has the additional $|a|_v^{1/2}$ at a finite place and $\prod_{w\mid\infty}|a|_w^{1/2}$ at infinity. Their product is $1$ by the product formula. Hence the scalar coordinate changes always have this product-one property.

For a nondegenerate bilinear form $B_0$ in $d$ coordinates, denote the rational determinant of its matrix by $\delta_0$. Locally the self-dual measure for $B_0$ has factor $u_v^d|\delta_0|_v^{1/2}$ relative to the product coordinate measure. Indeed, for the product of scalar self-dual measures the Fourier transform for $B_0$ is the ordinary product Fourier transform evaluated at the linear image under the $B_0$ matrix. A second transform then brings in the determinant Jacobian $|\delta_0|_v^{-1}$. Multiplying the measure by its inverse square root restores Fourier inversion. At infinity use the same argument with the product of all real absolute values. The new factors once again have product one and are $1$ at almost every finite place. Apply this calculation to $B_X$ and $B_S$ separately.

### Change both measures of the disintegration

Suppose the source and target additive measures $dX_v,dS_v$ change to $c_vdX_v,e_vdS_v$. The fibre disintegration changes from $\omega_{T,v}$ to $(c_v/e_v)\omega_{T,v}$: integrating that new family against $e_vdS_v$ gives exactly $c_vdX_v$. Continuity of the disintegration for compactly supported tests makes this equality valid at every nondegenerate $T$. Both $c_v$ and $e_v$ have product one, including their real factors. Consequently $\prod_v c_v/e_v=1$. The new fibre integrals differ from the old integrals only by these factors, so their convergent product is unchanged. The argument also covers a zero factor or an infinite nonnegative integral by grouping all finitely many exceptional places first.

For a smooth compactly supported archimedean test and a pure finite Schwartz–Bruhat test, the weighted pushforward Gram densities are the continuous local fibre integrals. Their product thus gives the theta mean with self-dual measures just as it did with coordinate measures. Integrability follows by applying the measure formula to the absolute value of the test: its finite integrals are finite by compact support and regularity, and the remaining tail product converges. Complex tests follow by splitting into real and imaginary parts. For $j=1$ this is the input to the [measurable norm mean](one-by-one-gram-fibres-give-the-measurable-norm-mean.md).
