# Analytic vectors close the lowest-type module and force unitary uniqueness

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $G=U(p,q)$ with $p,q\ge1$, $K=U(p)\times U(q)$, $n=p+q$, and take the sign data, parameter $\lambda$, gap $h>npq+1$, and compact weight $\Lambda$ from the [noncompact layer estimate](compact-casimir-growth-controls-the-noncompact-word-filtration.md). Let $\sigma$ be a strongly continuous unitary representation of $G$ on a Hilbert space $H$. Suppose its smooth vectors contain an irreducible compact module $V$ of highest weight $\Lambda$ on which the full quadratic Casimir acts by $c_\lambda=\|\lambda\|^2-\|\rho\|^2$. Put $M=U(\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C})V$ and $H_0=\overline M$. Then $H_0$ is an irreducible unitary representation; its compact-finite vectors are exactly $M$, with finite multiplicities and one copy of $\Lambda$. Any two models generated this way are unitarily equivalent.

## From the word filtration to the group action

Take orthonormal real bases of $\mathfrak k$ and $\mathfrak p$ for minus the trace form and the trace form, respectively. Write the corresponding derived operators as $A_i,B_j$. Denote the compact energy by $Q(v)=\sum_i\|A_i v\|^2$. A vector in noncompact layer at most $m$ satisfies
$$
 Q(v)\le (\sqrt{c_K(\Lambda)}+m\sqrt n)^2\|v\|^2 .
$$
Indeed the square root of the compact energy on a tensor product is the norm of the vector of compact derivatives. For the diagonal action, this vector is a sum of vectors of derivatives on the individual factors, with norms bounded by the square roots of the individual Casimirs. The initial compact factor contributes $\sqrt{c_K(\Lambda)}$ and each noncompact letter contributes $\sqrt n$. The same bound holds on an equivariant quotient, by lifting to the orthogonal complement of its kernel and passing the resulting eigenvalue bound to the quotient. Apply this to each new layer and sum their orthogonal compact energies.

The full Casimir is $-\sum_i A_i^2+\sum_j B_j^2$. All the real generators are skew adjoint in inner products of smooth vectors. Its scalar action on the generated module therefore gives
$$
 \sum_j\|B_j v\|^2=Q(v)-c_\lambda\|v\|^2 .
$$
It follows that for fixed $X\in\mathfrak g$ there is a constant $C_X\ge1$ such that $\|d\sigma(X)v\|\le C_X(m+1)\|v\|$ on layer $m$. The operator takes this layer into layer $m+1$. Iteration bounds its $a$-th power on $v$ by $C_X^a(m+a)!\|v\|/m!$. The Taylor remainder for the orbit $t\mapsto \sigma(\exp(tX))v$, after terms of orders below $a$, is bounded by $|t|^a\|d\sigma(X)^a v\|/a!$ by unitarity. This tends to zero when $C_X|t|<1$, as the factorial ratio after division by $a!$ is polynomial in $a$ for fixed $m$. Every Taylor term belongs to $M$. This proves invariance of $\overline M$ for small $t$, then for all $t$ by subdivision. Polar decomposition $G=K\exp\mathfrak p$ and the compact action now give group invariance.

## Irreducibility and comparison

The compact isotypic projection is integration against the corresponding irreducible character times its dimension. It sends each finite-dimensional layer into itself. Thus its range on $H_0$ is the closure of its range on $M$. The latter is finite-dimensional by the layer estimate, so it is already closed. This proves the assertion about compact-finite vectors, in particular that the whole $\Lambda$-isotypic space is $V$. The orthogonal projection onto any closed invariant subspace of $H_0$ is a group intertwiner. On $V$ it is a scalar by compact irreducibility, and being a projection it is zero or one there. It commutes with the derivatives on smooth vectors, so it is the same scalar on $M$ and on $H_0$.

For uniqueness take the Hilbert direct sum of two such generated representations. In their two lowest types choose a compact unitary identification and take its graph as the diagonal lowest type. The common Casimir scalar on this graph gives a generated irreducible closure by the argument above. Projection from it to either summand is a nonzero intertwiner $P$. The bounded positive operator $P^*P$ commutes with the group and is a positive scalar by irreducibility. Dividing $P$ by the square root of that scalar gives an isometry with closed image, and irreducibility of the target makes it onto. Composing the two isometries gives the desired equivalence.

## Applying this to a discrete series specified by the lowest type

Suppose an irreducible unitary representation contains a compact type $\Lambda$ of minimal Vogan norm $\|\mu+2\rho_c\|$. A smooth copy is available by applying a smooth compactly supported approximate identity averaged under conjugation by $K$. The resulting group convolution intertwines $K$ and converges strongly to the identity. On the chosen compact copy its restriction is nonzero for sufficiently small support, so its image is an isomorphic smooth copy. Smoothness of the convolution follows by moving derivatives onto the translated smooth kernel. For this copy the first layer has no highest weight $\Lambda-\alpha$ with $\alpha\in\mathcal P$, since $\Lambda+2\rho_c=\lambda+\rho$ and subtracting $\alpha$ strictly decreases the squared norm: its change is $2-2\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha\rangle<0$.

Once a smooth comparison model with scalar $c_\lambda$ is constructed, the gap and absence form of the layer estimate suffice to identify the scalar in this arbitrary representation. Form the direct sum with the comparison model and generate from a diagonal compact copy. The negative weights are absent in its first layer, as is seen by projecting to each summand. Therefore the generated module has just one highest line of weight $\Lambda$. The central Casimir sends a diagonal highest vector into this same line, and its projection into the comparison model has eigenvalue $c_\lambda$. The scalar must be $c_\lambda$ in both components. This gives the scalar on the whole compact copy by equivariance, so the unitary comparison now applies. The direct sum is the source's comparison argument under the gap hypothesis. The general route used by the source to establish the scalar from absence alone also has a Clifford-algebra proof, which is a separate dependency.
