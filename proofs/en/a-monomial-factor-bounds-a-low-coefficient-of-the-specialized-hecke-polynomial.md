# A monomial factor bounds a low coefficient of the specialized Hecke polynomial

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $O$ be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer $\varpi$ and residue characteristic $\ell$. Give its valuation $v_O$ the normalization $v_O(\varpi)=1$. Use $R$ variables $X_i$ and $R$ variables $Y_i$. Fix nonnegative bounds $s,n_{\max},V$, and put
$$
 B=Rn_{\max}\ell^s,\qquad W=(V+1)B,\qquad E=V+1+W .
$$
Let the exponents used in the substitution of the $X_i$ be $e_i\le s$ and those of the $Y_i$ be $e'_i\ge E$. Denote the substitution by $\mathcal S$, sending a variable of exponent $j$ to $(1+T)^{\ell^j}-1$. Let $M\ge E$ and let $f,D,g$ be polynomials in these variables over $O$, with the following three properties.

- $f-Dg$ belongs to the ideal generated by all $Y_i$ and all $(1+X_i)^{\ell^M}-1$.
- $v_O(g(0))\le v$ for some $0\le v\le V$.
- Modulo $\varpi$, $D$ equals $u\prod_iX_i^{n_i}$ modulo the ideal of all $\ell^M$-th powers of the individual variables, where $u(0)\ne 0$ and $n_i\le n_{\max}$.

Then $\mathcal S(f)$ has a coefficient of degree at most $W$ whose valuation is at most $v$.

For $1\le j\le W$, the identity
$$
 j\binom{\ell^a}{j}=\ell^a\binom{\ell^a-1}{j-1}
$$
shows that $\ell^{a-v_\ell(j)}$ divides $\binom{\ell^a}{j}$ when $j\le\ell^a$, with the coefficient zero at larger degrees. In this calculation $v_\ell(j)$ denotes the ordinary integer valuation. If $a\ge E$, then $a-v_\ell(j)\ge V+1$. Thus the coefficients through degree $W$ of $(1+T)^{\ell^a}-1$ are all divisible by $\varpi^{V+1}$. The same property holds after multiplying by an arbitrary integral power series and adding, since each low coefficient in the result uses only low coefficients of the given factor. After substitution the $Y_i$ have exponent $e'_i\ge E$ and the generators $(1+X_i)^{\ell^M}-1$ have exponent $M+e_i\ge E$ in this calculation. Hence through degree $W$, $\mathcal S(f)$ and $\mathcal S(D)\mathcal S(g)$ agree modulo $\varpi^{V+1}$.

Over the residue field each substitution is simply its leading monomial $T^{\ell^{e_i}}$ or $T^{\ell^{e'_i}}$. The prescribed monomial factor of $D$ therefore has degree $\sum_i n_i\ell^{e_i}\le B$. Its coefficient is $u(0)\ne0$, since terms from the ideal of $\ell^M$-th powers have degree at least $\ell^M>B$. Thus $\mathcal S(D)$ has a unit coefficient at some degree $m\le B$. Also $\mathcal S(g)(0)=g(0)$.

For completeness, let $m_1\le m$ be the first degree where the coefficient of $\mathcal S(D)$ is a unit. Suppose every coefficient of $\mathcal S(f)$ through degree $W$ were divisible by $\varpi^{v+1}$. The congruence then gives the same divisibility for $\mathcal S(D)\mathcal S(g)$ through $W$. Induct on $j\le v+1$ to show that the coefficient $\mathcal S(g)_i$ is divisible by $\varpi^j$ whenever $i+jm_1\le W$. For $j=0$ this holds by integrality. For the step $j\to j+1$, induct in increasing $i$. In the coefficient of degree $i+m_1$ of the product, terms using a coefficient of $\mathcal S(D)$ at degree $a<m_1$ have one factor $\varpi$, and their other factor has degree $i+m_1-a$, where the induction on $j$ applies. Terms with $a>m_1$ use a coefficient of $\mathcal S(g)$ of smaller degree than $i$, where the induction on $i$ applies. This leaves the coefficient at $m_1$ times $\mathcal S(g)_i$, which must also be divisible by $\varpi^{j+1}$. Cancel the unit at $m_1$. Taking $j=v+1$ and $i=0$ is allowed since $(v+1)m_1\le W$, and contradicts $v_O(g(0))\le v$. The desired low coefficient follows.
