# The type prime occurs on one side of the theta level

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

### Statement

Let $E/F$ be the project’s CM quadratic extension, with conjugation denoted by a bar. Fix a word prime $T$ and two type primes $g_0,g_1$, pairwise distinct rational primes. Choose $\pi_T,\pi_0,\pi_1\in\mathcal O_E$ with
$$
\pi_T\bar\pi_T=T,\qquad
\pi_i\bar\pi_i=g_i\quad(i=0,1),
$$
where each element and its conjugate generate coprime ideals. Write $S_T$ for the prime divisors of $\pi_T$ in $E$ and $S_i$ for those of $\bar\pi_i$. Assume each $S_i$ is nonempty and each $P\in S_i$ has residue cardinality $g_i$, as given by the rank and norm conditions on the type primes.

Let $\mathscr S$ be the excluded rational prime set for the twisting tuple, containing $T,g_0,g_1$. The remaining twist places form a finite set $Q_\mu$. Each member of $Q_\mu$ is the selected prime over a split prime of $F$ of residue characteristic outside $\mathscr S$, and the choice above each such prime of $F$ is fixed. Put $S_{\rm loc}=S_0\cup S_1\cup Q_\mu$, $S=S_T\cup S_{\rm loc}$ and $U=\prod_{P\in S_{\rm loc}}\#\kappa(P)$. For nonnegative depths $A_0,B_0$ with $B_0\ge1$, set $c_A=U^{A_0}$ and $c_B=TU^{B_0}$. Then

- $P\in S$ implies $\bar P\notin S$.
- $S_i\subseteq S$, and the places in $S$ containing $g_i$ are exactly $S_i$.
- $c_A,c_B>0$ and $g_i\mid c_B$ for $i=0,1$.

The theorem furnishing the split-prime jet setup also uses that the period fractional ideal is the unit ideal after localization at each $P\in S$. The final part of the proof below gives the arithmetic conditions that establish this.

### Conjugation and the levels

A place in $S_T$ lies over $T$, a place in $S_i$ lies over $g_i$, and a place in $Q_\mu$ has residue characteristic outside $\mathscr S$. Conjugation fixes the rational prime below a place. Thus it cannot move a place from one of these four sets into another. Within $S_T$, if $P$ and $\bar P$ both contained $\pi_T$, then $P$ would contain $\pi_T$ and $\bar\pi_T$, contradicting coprimality. The same reasoning within $S_i$ uses $\bar\pi_i$ and $\pi_i$. Within $Q_\mu$, conjugation exchanges the two distinct primes over a split prime of $F$, and only one was selected. This proves the first conclusion. The description of the rational primes below the four sets also proves the second.

Every residue cardinality in $U$ is positive, so $c_A,c_B>0$. Choose $P_0\in S_i$. Its contribution to $U$ is $g_i$. Hence $g_i\mid U$ and, since $B_0\ge1$, $g_i\mid c_B$. The nonemptiness used here follows in the formal setup from $\#S_i=[E:\mathbb Q]/2>0$.

### The period ideal at the selected places

Let $M$ be the prescribed level whose prime divisors include those of the host integers $Q_S$ and $q_*$ that can support the different. The project prime conditions give $g_i\equiv1\pmod M$ and $\pi_T\equiv1\pmod{M\mathcal O_E}$. Suppose that a rational prime $\ell\mid M$ belongs to $\mathscr S$. No $P\in S$ lies over $\ell$. At $P\in S_T$, being over $\ell$ would put $\pi_T$ and $\pi_T-1$ in $P$, which is impossible. At a type place, the rational prime below it is a $g_i$ that cannot divide $M$ because of $g_i\equiv1\pmod M$. At a twist place the residue characteristic lies outside $\mathscr S$. In the host setup the primes dividing $Q_S$ and the distinguished prime $q_*$ all belong to $\mathscr S$, so these exclusions apply to every selected place.

The fractional ideal of periods is generated by products of elements of an integral auxiliary ideal $\mathfrak c$ and of the inverse half-different $\mathfrak b^{-1}$, with the factors transported to $E$ by the field identification of the model. The integral ideal $\mathfrak b$ divides the relative different. At a selected $P$ the exclusion just proved and the different-support condition imply that the different is a unit ideal locally. Therefore $\mathfrak b\mathcal O_{E,P}=\mathcal O_{E,P}$ and its inverse also localizes to $\mathcal O_{E,P}$. This can also be seen by taking $z\in\mathfrak b\setminus P$ and observing that every $u\in\mathfrak b^{-1}$ has $zu\in\mathcal O_E$. The auxiliary ideal contributes a local unit when its norm is coprime to the rational prime below $P$, since the integer $\mathrm N(\mathfrak c)$ lies in $\mathfrak c$ and is then a unit at $P$. In the application $\mathfrak c$ is the unit ideal. The whole period ideal therefore localizes to $\mathcal O_{E,P}$ at every $P\in S$ and is nonzero.

These arithmetic facts supply the place set and the positive levels to [the coset-level comparison](changing-the-coset-level-isolates-the-auxiliary-prime.md). In particular, when applying that comparison with $p=g_i$, the rational integer $p$ is a unit at every place in $S\setminus S_i$.
