# Conjugate reference places give one odd-rank geometric seed

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E$ be CM with algebraic closure $\Omega$, let $\ell$ be a prime and $\iota\colon\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\simeq\mathbb C$, and let $a\ge1$ be odd. Suppose $d>0$, $x\in E$ satisfies $x^2=-d$, and $-d$ is a square in $\mathbb Q_\ell$. Assume $[E^+:\mathbb Q]$ is even, and every place of $E$ nonsplit over $E^+$ is prime to $\ell$ and unramified over $E^+$. Let $T$ be a finite set of nonsplit places. Take split places $w_0,v'$ above $\ell$ with distinct contractions to $E^+$, a reference automorphic datum $U$, and finite sets of places $S,Y$. Two places are called apart when their contractions to $E^+$ differ. A place is usable if it is split over $E^+$ and lies over a rational prime $q$ with $-d$ a square in $\mathbb Q_q$. [Field and place conditions](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/GaloisGeometry/LatticeDataThicknessBound.lean#L16) · [Exact seed statement](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/GaloisGeometry/LimWU4OfLimWU3.lean#L14)

Let $U'$ be a rank-$a$ definite-unitary automorphic representation, unramified at nonsplit places outside $T$. Assume it has the specified elliptic type at $w_0$: there is a torsion-free compact open subgroup of the local $\mathrm{GL}_a$, a surjection onto the cyclic group of order $\ell^m$ for some $m$, and a nontrivial character of this quotient. A nonzero vector of $U'$ transforms by that character, and any smooth irreducible local representation containing this character is cuspidal. Assume $U'$ is spherical at split places apart from $w_0$ and every place of $S$, and is also spherical at $v'$. Its weights agree with those of $U$ at all embeddings over coefficient-prime places apart from both $w_0,v'$. At some embedding above $v'$, the $a$ jumps attached to every weight of $U'$ there satisfy the [weight gap condition](a-gap-condition-separates-the-averages-of-disjoint-weight-sets.md). [Type definition](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/GenOf.lean#L14) · [Weight condition](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/GaloisGeometry/LimWPhi.lean#L24)

Then there is a continuous semisimple rank-$a$ Galois representation $r$ over $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ with the following properties.
- For all usable $w\nmid\ell$ apart from $S$ outside a finite exceptional set, $r$ is unramified at each spherical $w$. For Frobenius in a common open dense subset, its inverse Frobenius polynomial has roots $q_w^{(a-1)/2}B_w$, for every Satake multiset $B_w$ there.
- At each usable $w\nmid\ell$ in $Y$ apart from $S$, if $B_w$ satisfies the polynomial genericity condition below, the same assertion holds for every local Frobenius at $w$.
- At each $w\mid\ell$ in $Y$ apart from $w_0,v'$ and $S$, $r$ is crystalline and de Rham with the precise weight jumps of $U'$. For a spherical $B_w$ satisfying the same genericity condition, all crystalline Weil power sums of $r$ there are those of the normalized spherical Satake parameter. Quantification is over every compatible period bridge and induced embedding.

The genericity test is that the polynomial `DGen` is nonzero on each ordering of the Satake multiset. In particular, it excludes equality of the full product with another product of $a$ factors from those entries, allowing repetitions. The seed theorem permits auxiliary places at which all choices of $U'$ have to satisfy genericity. The construction here chooses that auxiliary set empty. ## Aligning the two reference places with the target

Write $K=\mathbb Q(x)\subset E$. It is imaginary quadratic, and complex conjugation on $E$ restricts to the nontrivial automorphism of $K$. All the places under consideration lie over the same rational prime $\ell$. Contract them to $K$. There is one prime of $K$ over $\ell$, or there are two which are exchanged by conjugation. Thus either a reference place or its conjugate has the same contraction to $K$ as any target place $w\mid\ell$. The project expresses equality of contraction by testing the integer elements of $E$ belonging to $K$ for membership in the two prime ideals. Conjugating a split place leaves its contraction to $E^+$ unchanged. Consequently it leaves all comparisons of apartness unchanged. [The contraction argument](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/ZetaCharacterDivisibility/PSameOr.lean#L107) · [Replacing a reference place](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/ZetaCharacterDivisibility/SeedFinOddOf.lean#L33)

The needed automorphic conditions survive this move. At a split place the same local unitary factor is identified with $\mathrm{GL}_a$ at both conjugate completions, and the identifications differ by the conjugation and contragredient isomorphism, i.e. inverse transpose in the standard bases. It transports hyperspecial subgroups, so a spherical vector at one place gives a spherical vector at the other. It transports the compact open subgroup and the type character by pullback, including the condition that any representation containing that type is cuspidal. At the conjugate embedding the dominant weight is dual; if the jumps at the original embedding are $J$, the dual jumps are $\{a-1-j:j\in J\}$. An affine map $j\mapsto a-1-j$ preserves inequality of the averages of disjoint nonempty subsets. Thus the lacunary weight property, deduced at $v'$ from the gap hypothesis, holds at its conjugate as well. ## Using different geometric models for the same representation

The geometric input `GeoOne` applies with a split reference pair whose places have the same contraction to $K$. For that pair and the automorphic conditions above it gives:
1. A geometric model in the sense of [rank extraction](a-common-rank-is-extracted-from-each-geometric-model.md).
2. At each usable spherical $w\nmid\ell$, a model with the unramified inverse-Frobenius annihilator at that place.
3. At each spherical $w\mid\ell$ with the same contraction to $K$ as the second reference place and apart from both references, one model crystalline at $w$ containing all prescribed jump values, and another crystalline model with the degree-$-1$ Weil polynomial dividing a power of the normalized Satake polynomial. The two models can differ because the required geometric comparisons are different.

Choose the original $v'$ as the bookkeeping target and align $w_0$ and $v'$ to it as above. Apply the geometric input and extract the [common representation](a-common-rank-is-extracted-from-each-geometric-model.md) from any model it supplies. This defines $r$ and proves the first assertion. The same application of `GeoOne` supplies a model for each usable spherical target away from $\ell$, so rank extraction and product-genericity give the second assertion.

For a target $w\mid\ell$ apart from both references and $S$, align the two references anew to $w$. Neither apartness comparison changes, and the spherical condition at $w$ is available by the stated hypotheses. All places over $\ell$ are split over $E^+$ by the field conditions. The geometric input can therefore be applied at $w$ to obtain the two models in its third clause. Each has the same rank-extracted constituent $r$, since the model definition uses the same automorphic datum and agreement on a dense set of usable Frobenius elements. The first model gives crystallinity and all jumps. The second gives the exact Weil polynomial when product-genericity is known. Taking powers of the matched eigenvalues gives each requested power sum. This proves the seed statement.
