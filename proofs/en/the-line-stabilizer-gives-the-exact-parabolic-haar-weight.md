# The line stabilizer gives the exact parabolic Haar weight

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Use the archimedean group $G=\prod_w U(H_w)$, majorant $M=(P_w)$, nonzero isotropic $x$, point stabilizer $H$, and compact majorant stabilizer $K$ of [the ellipsoid formula](the-isotropic-vector-quotient-gives-a-weighted-ellipsoid.md). Write $n\ge2$ for the Hermitian rank and $f$ for the number of complex places. For any Haar measure $\mu$ on $G$ there are a Haar measure $\nu$ on $H$ and a Borel function $\tau:\mathbb C^f\to G$ with $\tau(b)^{-1}x=(b_w x_w)_w$ whenever every $b_w\ne0$, such that for every nonnegative Borel $A$,
$$
 \int_G A(g)\,d\mu(g)
 =\int_{\mathbb C^f}\int_K\int_H A(h\tau(b)k)\,d\nu(h)\,dk\,
                  \prod_w |b_w|^{2(n-2)}\,db .
\tag{1}
$$
Here $dk$ is probability Haar measure and $db$ is Lebesgue measure. [Archimedean parabolic formula](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/GroundTheory/HeightsAndTheta/Heights/FiniteCovolume/PHaarParabolicCoordinates.lean#L21)

## Passing from the unitary group to the line stabilizer

Let $P$ be the subgroup of $G$ preserving the isotropic line at each place. In the coordinates for the ordinary Hermitian norm given by $P_w^{1/2}$, the indefinite form is the Hermitian involution $S_w=P_w^{-1/2}H_wP_w^{-1/2}$. A vector is isotropic exactly when its two components in the $+1$ and $-1$ eigenspaces have equal norms. The nonzero isotropic vectors have both components nonzero. The compact stabilizer $K_w$ is the product of the two ordinary unitary groups on these eigenspaces. For any two nonzero isotropic vectors, scale one by a positive real scalar to make the common component norms agree, and then apply two unitary maps between the corresponding equal-norm vectors. Hence $K_w$ acts transitively on the isotropic lines. It is compact as a closed subgroup of a group preserving a positive inner product. For $g\in G$, this transitivity provides $k\in K$ with $k^{-1}(Ex)_w=(g^{-1}Ex)_w$ at every place, so $gk^{-1}\in P$ and $G=PK$. [Compact group transitivity](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/GroundTheory/HeightsAndTheta/Heights/FiniteCovolume/PKTransitive.lean#L11) · [Parabolic decomposition](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/GroundTheory/HeightsAndTheta/Heights/FiniteCovolume/PParabolicDecomposition.lean#L14)

The group $G$ is unimodular. For example the Lie algebra of each unitary factor complexifies to all complex endomorphisms of its space, and conjugation by $g_w$ on those endomorphisms has determinant $\det(g_w)^n\det(g_w^{-1})^n=1$. Thus its real adjoint determinant on the unitary Lie algebra has absolute value $1$. The left Haar measure on $G$ is invariant under the transitive action $(p,k)\cdot g=p g k^{-1}$ of $P\times K$. The stabilizer of $1$ under this action is the diagonal copy of $P\cap K$, a compact subgroup. The pushforward of product Haar measure on $P\times K$ under $(p,k)\mapsto pk^{-1}$ is another invariant Radon measure. For local finiteness, the inverse image of a compact subset $C$ is closed in $(CK\cap P)\times K$, which is compact. Invariant Radon measure on a transitive quotient with compact stabilizer is unique up to a scalar: test it against the averages of continuous compactly supported functions over the stabilizer, thereby lifting it to a left invariant Radon measure on $P\times K$, and use Haar uniqueness. Since Haar measure on $K$ is invariant by inversion, this proves that integrating on $G$ amounts, up to a fixed positive scalar, to integrating $A(pk)$ against left Haar on $P$ and $dk$. ## Compute the density on the line scalars

Fix an isotropic partner $w_0$ and its orthogonal complement. For $a=(a_w)\in(\mathbb C^\times)^f$ let $t(a)$ act on $x_w,w_{0,w}$ by $a_w,\bar a_w^{-1}$, respectively, and fix the complement. This section of the line action gives the unique factorization $p=h\,t(a)$, $h\in H$. By [the point stabilizer calculation](the-point-stabilizer-inherits-finite-volume-from-the-complement.md), $H$ itself is the semidirect product of its unipotent group and the complementary unitary group, with product Haar measure. Conjugation by $t(a)$ fixes the complementary factor. Applying it to the unipotent formula gives $(y_w,c_w)\mapsto(a_w y_w,|a_w|^2c_w)$. The $n-2$ complex horizontal dimensions contribute $|a_w|^{2(n-2)}$ to the real Jacobian, and the one real center contributes $|a_w|^2$. Thus for $dh$ on $H$ the dilation factor of volume is
$$
 J(a)=\prod_w |a_w|^{2(n-1)}.
$$
In the coordinates $h\,t(a)$, left Haar on $P$ is $J(a)^{-1}dh\,d^\times a$, where $d^\times a=\prod_w |a_w|^{-2}\,da_w$. To check this, left multiplication by $h_0t(a_0)$ changes $(h,a)$ to $(h_0t(a_0)h t(a_0)^{-1},a_0a)$, and $J(a_0)^{-1}$ from the weight cancels the volume change in $h$. The multiplicative measure is invariant under multiplication and inversion. Set $b=a^{-1}$. Then $J(b^{-1})^{-1}\,d^\times b=\prod_w |b_w|^{2(n-2)}\,db$. Define $\tau(b)=t(b^{-1})$ on the nonzero coordinates and set it equal to $1$ on the coordinate hyperplanes, which have Lebesgue measure zero. The normalization scalar from $PK$ can be absorbed in $dh=d\nu$. This yields (1) with the claimed inverse action on $x$.
