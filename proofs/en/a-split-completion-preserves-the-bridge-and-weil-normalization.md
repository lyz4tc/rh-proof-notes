# A split completion preserves the bridge and Weil normalization

**Status: Source-based reconstruction draft.** This expands the place and bridge inputs to the spherical Frobenius comparison. No new Lean build.

Draft reconstructed from the pinned Lean sources with AI assistance from Dot and checked against the cited source by Codex.

## Setting

Let $F\subset K$ be number fields in a common algebraic closure $\Omega$. Let $A$ be a valuation ring of $\Omega$ over a finite place $v$ of $F$; write $w$ for its restriction to $K$. Denote by $D_F,D_K$ the respective decomposition groups preserving $A$. Suppose the completion map induces a continuous isomorphism $i:F_v\to K_w$ compatible with the inclusion $F\subset K$ and that $D_F$ is contained in the image of $G_K\to G_F$. These are the data supplied at the place chosen by the good quadratic split-family theorem. Here we treat the local comparison, not existence of that family.

Fix a prime $\ell$ and a local bridge $B$ from $D_F$ to the absolute Galois group $G_M$ of its finite local field $M\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$. Thus $B$ has an embedding $j:\Omega\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ whose image on $F$ is dense in $M$, a topological group identification $e:D_F\to G_M$ equivariant for $j$, its finite coefficient field $L_0$ containing all the stipulated conjugates of $M$, and the prescribed topological $\mathbb Z_\ell$-algebra identification. If it is inducing, this means its period realization induces the given $j$. We show:
1. there is a bridge from $D_K$ with exactly the same $M,j,L_0$ and period-side algebra identification;
2. the Weil groups identify by retaining the very same action on $\Omega$, with the same signed degree and residue norm;
3. that identification pulls back the spherical normalized Weil–Deligne parameter over $K_w$ to the one over $F_v$, for every rank $a$ and multiset of nonzero Satake parameters $B_v$ used on both sides.

## Keeping the same bridge

Each automorphism $\sigma\in G_M$ comes from $g=e^{-1}(\sigma)\in D_F$. By hypothesis $g$ lies in the image of $G_K$ and so fixes $K$. For $x\in K$, equivariance gives
$$
\sigma(j(x))=j(gx)=j(x).
$$

The fixed field of $G_M$ inside $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ is $M$, so $j(K)\subset M$. Its closure is $M$ since it contains $j(F)$, whose closure is $M$.

Restriction of scalars gives an injective continuous map $G_K\to G_F$, with closed image. An element $h\in G_K$ belongs to $D_K$ exactly when its image preserves $A$, since this tests the same underlying automorphism. Conversely each $g\in D_F$ fixes $K$ and hence has a unique preimage in $D_K$. Thus restriction of scalars is a topological group isomorphism $D_K\to D_F$: it is a continuous bijection of compact Hausdorff groups, or equivalently the restriction of the closed embedding above. Compose it with $e$. Keeping all other bridge data, the defining equivariance holds and density has just been checked. Because the embedding $j$ and the period-side algebra identification were not changed, the inducing property is preserved.

This proves the bridge construction, not merely an abstract isomorphism between abstractly chosen local Galois groups. It ensures that the companion finite-model comparison can conjugate neither the coefficient field nor the local period realization.

## Why the Weil degree is unchanged

The residue action can be defined on the residue field of $A$. If $q_v=|\mathcal O_F/v|$, a Weil element over $F$ is a decomposition element whose residue action is an integral power of the $q_v$-Frobenius. Equivalently it satisfies an equality of two nonnegative powers on residues, written with exponent pair $(u,t)$; its degree is $u-t$ in the project's convention. The analogous convention over $K$ uses $q_w$.

Here is a residue-field proof of $q_v=q_w$ following the source. In either direction, if a decomposition element acting on the residue field of $A$ by the $q$-power map fixes the number field, then the embedded residue field of that number field at the place consists of roots of $X^q-X$. The embedding follows by reducing the ring of integers in $A$; the kernel is the given prime since $A$ lies over that place. Since $q>1$, the polynomial is nonzero, so the residue field has at most $q$ elements. Lifting the $q_v$-Frobenius to $D_F$ and viewing the same element in $D_K$ gives $q_w\le q_v$. Lifting the $q_w$-Frobenius to $D_K$ and viewing it in $D_F$ gives the reverse inequality. The argument in the general Lean place-isomorphism theorem transports these residue identities by conjugation; here the conjugating map on $\Omega$ is the identity. Alternatively, equality of residue cardinalities here follows directly from the continuous completion identification and its inverse.

Now a given pair $(u,t)$ describes the same residue equality on either side, because the actions preserve the same ring $A$ and the cardinalities agree. The correspondence $c:W_v\to W_w$ is therefore a group isomorphism preserving degree. The two elements are related in the Lean sense of `WeilRel` because their actions on every element of $\Omega$ are the same. Smoothness of any monoid representation of the Weil group is preserved under precomposition with $c$: the identity neighborhoods on inertia correspond, since inertia is the degree-zero subgroup and the underlying decomposition groups have the same Krull topology under the identification.

## Transporting a Weil–Deligne parameter

A representative of a Weil–Deligne parameter over $K_w$ has a smooth Weil action $R_w$ and a nilpotent monodromy $N$. On the same complex vector space use action $g\mapsto R_w(c(g))$ and leave $N$ unchanged. The defining relation between action and monodromy involves only $q_w^{\deg c(g)}=q_v^{\deg g}$, and is therefore unchanged; smoothness and the semisimplicity conditions on the action are preserved. The inverse transport uses $c^{-1}$. Passing to isomorphism classes gives the transport in `place_over_transport_ring_iso_symm` (that theorem writes the corresponding forward equivalence). The transport commutes with direct sums and real norm twists because it preserves $q$ and the degree, and it preserves pointwise characteristic polynomials at paired elements.

For an unramified diagonal parameter whose list is $b^{-1}$ for $b\in B_v$, each diagonal entry at $g$ is the same as the corresponding entry at $c(g)$, since the list and degree have not changed. The normalization is the real norm twist $(1-a)/2$, hence it is preserved as well. This gives exactly the spherical parameter comparison used in `holdsS_all_phi`. No assertion about compatibility of arbitrary ramified automorphic local components is needed for this step.
