# Two Chebotarev blocks realize inertia and Frobenius coordinates

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix the CM host $F$, a prime $\ell$, an integer $M\ge1$, and $n=2\ell^M$. Write $G_F$ for the absolute Galois group. The auxiliary character input gives an open normal subgroup $H$, a finite exceptional set, and a character $d_P:G_F\to\mathbb Z/\ell^M\mathbb Z$ at each usable split degree-one place $P$ whose decomposition group lies in $H$. Usability includes $q_P=\mathrm NP\equiv1\pmod n$. The character is surjective on $I_P$, has the prescribed conjugation behavior, and is unramified outside the auxiliary pair over the real place under $P$. Let $z\in H$ be the designated tame element, and assume it fixes $\mu_n$. Let $U$ be any open normal subgroup and let $h_1,\ldots,h_R\in H$ fix $\mu_n$. A finite exclusion list is given.

**Proposition.** One can choose two blocks $P_{1,a},P_{2,a}$, $1\le a\le R$, of usable places, all distinct up to conjugation and outside the exclusions, with all their inertia groups contained in $U$. Every Frobenius at $P_{1,a}$ is conjugate to $z$ modulo $U$. Every Frobenius at $P_{2,a}$ is conjugate to $h_a$ modulo $U$ and satisfies $d_{P_{1,b}}(\operatorname{Frob}_{P_{2,a}})=\delta_{ab}$ for every $b$.

**Proof.** Let $C_n$ be the kernel of the action on $\mu_n$, an open normal subgroup since $\mu_n$ is finite. Put $W=U\cap H\cap C_n$. Enlarge the excluded list by its conjugate places, the bad places of the character input, the finitely many places ramified in the quotients by $U,H$, those dividing $n$, and the finite exceptions to using split degree-one places. For the last point, over a quadratic CM extension a degree-one prime of the host outside ramification has relative residue degree one, hence is split. The degree-one form of Chebotarev chooses primes outside this enlarged finite set for any given conjugacy class in a finite quotient. It may be applied successively with previously selected pairs excluded. In an unramified quotient the image of a Frobenius lift is independent of the lift, and conjugating the chosen prime above a place changes its image only up to conjugacy.

First use Chebotarev modulo $W$ with target $z$ to choose the first block. For every such prime the Frobenius lies in $H\cap C_n$, since $z$ does and these groups are normal. All its inertia lies in $H\cap U$ by the exclusions and in $C_n$ because its residue characteristic does not divide $n$. Inertia together with a Frobenius topologically generates the decomposition group, so that group lies in $H$. Fixing $\mu_n$ at Frobenius gives $q_P\equiv1\pmod n$: reduction is injective on roots of order prime to the residue characteristic, and the induced action on the reduced roots raises to $q_P$. Thus the auxiliary characters are available at all first-block places.

At $P_{1,b}$, inertia lies inside $W$ and realizes any value of the $b$-th character. Because the selected pairs are distinct, all the other first-block characters are trivial on that inertia. It follows by taking products of inertia elements, one for each $b$, that the tuple of first-block characters maps $W$ onto $(\mathbb Z/\ell^M\mathbb Z)^R$. In particular choose $\eta_a\in W$ so that $d_{P_{1,b}}(h_a\eta_a)=\delta_{ab}$.

For the second block apply Chebotarev modulo $W\cap\bigcap_b\ker d_{P_{1,b}}$, with target $h_a\eta_a$. Exclude the first block and its conjugates in addition to the previous exclusions. The target still lies in $H\cap C_n$, so all the same usability and inertia properties hold. Conjugation does not affect any cyclic character. The additional kernels therefore force the exact Kronecker values at every second-block Frobenius. Modulo $U$ the adjustment $\eta_a$ is trivial, so the prescribed class remains that of $h_a$.
