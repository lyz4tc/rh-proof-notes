# Unfolding the first vector inducts on the Gram rank

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E/F$ be a quadratic CM extension, $W$ a nondegenerate Hermitian space of rank $n$ over $E$, and $T$ a nondegenerate Hermitian $j$-by-$j$ matrix over $E$. Assume $j=0$ or $n\ge j+2$. Work with the coordinate measures used for the [one-vector mean](a-dilation-fixes-the-scalar-in-the-one-vector-siegel-mean.md): choose an $F$-basis of $E$, use scalar measures of mass one on local integer rings and of covolume one for $\mathcal O_F$ at infinity, and take product measures in coordinates. Use these measures both on tuples of $W$ and, in diagonal and upper triangular coordinates, on Hermitian matrices. Let $\omega_{W,T,v}$ be the continuous fibre measure at $T$ for the Gram map at $v$.

**Claim.** The probability-normalized mean of the sum of a nonnegative pure adelic test over rational tuples of Gram matrix $T$, evaluated at their unitary translates, is the product of the integrals of that test on the local Gram fibres at $T$. The tests away from a finite set are indicators of the standard coordinate lattice. The statement includes the archimedean product as one factor, so no separation among the real places is required. The products are meaningful in the extended nonnegative sense. Off a sufficiently large finite set the untested masses have summable deviations from $1$, by the [integral tail count](an-orthogonal-first-vector-splits-the-local-gram-fibre-measure.md#the-integral-tail).

### Changes of rational coordinates

For tuples written as the columns of a matrix $X$, a basis change $A\in\mathrm{GL}_j(E)$ on the tuple positions has $X\mapsto XA$ and $\operatorname{Gram}(XA)=A^*\operatorname{Gram}(X)A$. It gives a bijection of rational summation sets and commutes with the unitary action. The local source Jacobian for this change is $|\mathrm N_{E/F}\det A|_v^{\,n}$, one determinant factor for each of the $n$ rows. The local target Jacobian is $|\mathrm N_{E/F}\det A|_v^{\,j}$. To see the latter, a diagonal change with entries $a_i$ scales the diagonal $i$-coordinate by $\mathrm N(a_i)$, and each off-diagonal $ij$-coordinate, a copy of the quadratic algebra, by a scalar of norm $\mathrm N(a_i)\mathrm N(a_j)$. Thus each $|\mathrm N(a_i)|_v$ occurs $j$ times. Permutation matrices have Jacobian one, and elementary shears have Jacobian one as their congruence operation on matrices is unipotent. For the last fact, left and right multiplication by the elementary shear and its adjoint are commuting unipotent maps on all matrices, so their product and its restriction to Hermitian matrices are unipotent as real or local-field linear maps. These operations generate the full linear group. The ratio of the two Jacobians cancels in the adelic product. The changes preserve the coordinate lattices at almost all places since their matrices and inverses are rational.

A rational change of coordinates on $W$ likewise transports the unitary group and its quotient probability measure, and has product of local Jacobians equal to one. In rank $j$ the tuple measure scales locally by the $j$-th power of the absolute norm of its determinant, while the Gram target coordinates stay the same for the transported form. This also cancels globally. It is therefore legitimate to choose rational coordinates adapted to an orthogonal splitting of the form or of the target.

### The induction step

For $j=0$ each untested fibre measure is the unit point mass on the empty tuple, and both sides evaluate the pure test there. Let $j=s+1$. A nondegenerate Hermitian form over $E/F$ represents a nonzero scalar: if every diagonal norm were zero, polarization over $F$ and over a nonreal element of $E$ would make the form vanish identically. Split such a line from the target and take $T=(t)\perp T'$ with $t\in F^\times$ and $T'$ nondegenerate of size $s$.

If $W$ has no rational vector of norm $t$, the sum on the left is empty. At least one local norm-$t$ fibre is also empty. Indeed, if all are nonempty, the one-vector formula and the positive tail density prove the existence of a rational vector: at the finitely many exceptional places take compactly supported local tests of positive fibre mass, and include an archimedean bump with positive mass. All remaining unit-coordinate masses are positive and their product is positive. The mean is then positive, so the rational sum cannot be empty. An empty norm-$t$ fibre at a place forces the target Gram fibre to be empty there, so the right hand side vanishes too.

Otherwise choose a rational vector $e$ of norm $t$, and write $W=Ee\perp W'$, with rational coordinates adapted to the splitting. Put $G=U(W)$ and $H=U(W')$. By the unitary quotient finiteness input both rational quotient volumes are finite and positive. They may use any Haar normalizations in this step. All rational norm-$t$ vectors are one $G(F)$-orbit, with stabilizer $H(F)$. Rational tuples whose first vector is $e$ and whose mixed Gram entries vanish have their remaining $s$ vectors in $W'$. Their remaining Gram condition is $T'$. Therefore the global sum can first be opened along the first rational orbit. This is the [same Tonelli unfolding as for one vector](unfolding-a-unit-norm-orbit-leaves-the-complement-volume.md), with the remaining sum kept inside the integral.

Next integrate over $H(\mathbb A_F)\backslash G(\mathbb A_F)$, and over $H(F)\backslash H(\mathbb A_F)$ in its fibres. For a fixed coset with representative $g$, the inner integral is the $H$-quotient mean of the test on $s$-tuples of Gram matrix $T'$, after inserting $e$ and acting by $g^{-1}$. It is multiplied by $V_H/V_G$ when the outer measure is the Haar quotient measure of $G$ by $H$. The induction hypothesis applies since $\operatorname{rank}W'=n-1\ge s+2$ when $s>0$. It expresses the inner mean as the product of the local complementary Gram integrals.

The outer measure, with the factor $V_H/V_G$, is the normalized first-vector mean viewed on the adelic norm-$t$ orbit, by applying the same unfolding with only the first-vector test. The one-vector theorem therefore expresses this outer integration as product measure of the norm-$t$ fibres. To check the restricted-product issue, choose a finite set containing the places with nonstandard tests and those with nonunit $t$ or nonstandard local coordinates and lattices. At any place outside it with first vector in the coordinate lattice, the representative can be taken in its stabilizer, since the line and its perpendicular integral complement split off and their self-dual frames match. The complementary test there is the ordinary coordinate lattice indicator, independently of the first vector. If the first vector is outside the lattice, the test vanishes. Consequently the inner mean yields a pure first-vector test at the finitely many special places and the standard first-vector indicator elsewhere, times the complementary tail product. Measurable local orbit sections give the indicated test functions, and invariance under the complement group makes them independent of the section. These assertions also follow by evaluating nonnegative cylinders and then using Tonelli.

Finally, at each place the [two-stage Gram Jacobian](an-orthogonal-first-vector-splits-the-local-gram-fibre-measure.md) identifies the two local integrals with one Gram-fibre integral, times the reciprocal of $|t|_v^{-2s}$. The rational product formula makes the product of these factors one. Thus the outer and inner means combine to the claimed product of the local Gram integrals at $T$. This proves the induction and includes the full one-row convergence case $2j<n$ used in the volume calculation.
