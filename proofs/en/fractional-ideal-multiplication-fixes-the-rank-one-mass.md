# Fractional ideal multiplication fixes the rank-one mass

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E/F$ be CM and unramified at every finite place, and let $H$ be a totally positive Hermitian form on a one-dimensional $E$-space. For every full integral lattice $L$, the correction $\lambda(L)$ in [the successive mass formula](two-limits-of-one-lattice-count-give-the-siegel-mass-ratio.md) is $1$. The genus mass $\mu(L)$ equals the positive genus mass $c_1$ of $\mathcal O_E$ with the form $(x,y)\mapsto\bar x y$. It depends on $E$. It is common to all rank-one forms and lattices over that field. [The rank-one constant](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/GL3MeanSquares/GenusMeanAsymptotics/UnimodularClassRepresentatives/ExistsMassConstOne.lean#L58)

## The ideals give a common genus mass

Choose a nonzero vector in the line to identify the form with $h(x,y)=b\,\bar x y$ for a fixed $b\in F^\times$, and the lattice with a fractional ideal $I$ of $E$. The isometries are the multiplications by $u\in E^\times$ with $u\bar u=1$, since the scalar $b$ cancels from the isometry condition. The same comparison works for local isometries. For any other fractional ideal $J$ in the same field, multiplication of ideals $M\mapsto J I^{-1}M$ is a bijection on the set of full lattices, with inverse multiplication by $I J^{-1}$. It takes $I$ to $J$, commutes with multiplication by $u$ and commutes with localization. Thus it takes the genus of $I$ to the genus of $J$ even when the latter is given a different nonzero form scale. It gives a bijection on rational isometry classes with the same stabilizer sizes. Hence their masses are equal. For the principal ideal and the form $b=1$ the genus has a finite nonempty set of classes and finite stabilizers by definiteness. Its mass is positive. ## Compute the local correction

Fix a prime $\mathfrak p\subset\mathcal O_F$ with $q=\mathrm N\mathfrak p$, work over the local integers and take a local generator of $L$ over the quadratic algebra, which may be split. The one-dimensional Gram entry $b_{\mathfrak p}$ lies in the fixed local ring and is integral. Let $d=v_{\mathfrak p}(b_{\mathfrak p})$, the local discriminant valuation. A scalar endomorphism $z$ preserves the form modulo $\mathfrak p^k$ when $b_{\mathfrak p}(\bar z z-1)\equiv0\bmod\mathfrak p^k$. For $k>d$ this is the unit norm congruence modulo $\mathfrak p^{k-d}$, and each solution there has $q^{2d}$ lifts modulo $\mathfrak p^k$.

At the split residue place, a scalar is a pair and the unit norm equation determines its second component as the inverse of the first. It has $q-1$ solutions modulo $\mathfrak p$. At an inert place, the residue norm $\mathbb F_{q^2}^\times\to \mathbb F_q^\times$ has kernel of size $q+1$, by cyclicity. In either case the number of lifts of one unit-norm solution from modulus $\mathfrak p^j$ to $\mathfrak p^{j+1}$ is $q$. Indeed the possible shifts are parametrized by the two-dimensional quadratic residue algebra, and the derivative at a unit equals a nonzero scalar multiple of the trace on that algebra after identifying the shift with a multiplier of $z$. The trace is surjective, including at residue characteristic two where the extension is étale, so there are $q$ solutions of the required linear congruence. This gives $q^{k-d}(1-\eta(\mathfrak p)/q)$ solutions modulo $\mathfrak p^{k-d}$, where $\eta$ is $+1$ for the split case and $-1$ for the inert case. Hence the normalized count $\alpha_{\mathfrak p}(L)$ is
$$
 q^d(1-\eta(\mathfrak p)/q).
$$
The numerator of the local correction is $A_{\mathfrak p}(1)q^d=(1-\eta(\mathfrak p)/q)q^d$, so each local correction is one. This proves $\mu(L)=c_1\lambda(L)$ in rank one, the initial condition for the mass induction.
