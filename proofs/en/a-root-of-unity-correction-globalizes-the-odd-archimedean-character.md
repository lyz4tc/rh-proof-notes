# A root-of-unity correction globalizes the odd archimedean character

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E/F$ be a CM quadratic extension unramified at every finite place, and denote its involution by $c$. Let $\varphi_i$ be one complex embedding of $E$ above each real place of $F$. Let $n_i$ be odd integers. Write $\eta$ for the quadratic idele class character of $F$ of the extension. Its infinite components are signs, and its finite components are unramified, with value $+1$ on a split uniformizer and $-1$ on an inert uniformizer. Suppose that for every root of unity $\zeta\in E$ there is $\delta\in E^\times$ with
$$
 \delta/c(\delta)=\zeta,\qquad
 v_{\mathfrak P}(\delta)\in2\mathbb Z\text{ at every inert prime},\qquad
 \prod_i\left(\frac{\varphi_i(\delta)}{|\varphi_i(\delta)|}\right)^{n_i}=1.
 \tag{1}
$$
Then there exists an everywhere finitely unramified unitary Hecke character $\widetilde\eta$ of $E$ whose restriction to $\mathbb A_F^\times$ is $\eta$, and whose complex components have exponents $n_i$. The source uses the case $n_i=1$ for the frame.

On the open subgroup $U=E_\infty^\times\prod_{\mathfrak P}\mathcal O_{\mathfrak P}^\times$ prescribe the character $\psi$ given by the archimedean phases to powers $n_i$, with trivial finite part. For each finite prime $v$ of $F$, write $b_v$ for the idele of $E$ obtained from a local uniformizer of $F_v$ in the components above $v$, with other components $1$. Prescribe $\widetilde\eta(b_v)=\eta_v(\varpi_v)$ and $\widetilde\eta(k)=1$ for diagonal $k\in E^\times$. To extend consistently it is enough to check that when $k\prod_v b_v^{m_v}\in U$, with finitely supported integer exponents $m_v$, we have
$$
 \psi(k)=\prod_v\eta_v(\varpi_v)^{m_v}.
 \tag{2}
$$
Here $\psi(k)$ denotes the archimedean phase on the given diagonal element. This condition also handles every relation among the prescribed generators.

In the stated intersection, for every prime $\mathfrak P$ above $v$ we have $v_{\mathfrak P}(k)=-m_v$, since the extension is unramified. Thus all finite valuations of $k/c(k)$ are zero, and its relative norm to $F$ is $1$. The [norm-one unit argument](a-norm-one-unit-test-globalizes-tame-cm-characters.md) shows $k/c(k)$ is a root of unity. Apply (1) to this root. The relation $\delta/c(\delta)=k/c(k)$ implies $k/\delta=a\in F^\times$. At an inert prime $v$ the valuation of $\delta$ is even, so $\eta_v(\varpi_v)^{m_v}=\eta_v(a)^{-1}$, while at a split prime both sides are $1$. The product of these finite factors is the archimedean factor of $\eta(a)$ by its principal-idele product formula. Oddness of $n_i$ makes this product equal to the archimedean phase to powers $n_i$ on $a$. Equation (1) makes that phase unchanged on multiplication by $\delta$, giving (2).

The prescribed character on the subgroup generated by $U,E^\times$ and the $b_v$ therefore extends to the whole idele group by the [open-subgroup extension argument](a-norm-one-unit-test-globalizes-tame-cm-characters.md). Agreement on $U$ makes it continuous. It descends through $E^\times$, is unramified at finite places and has the specified exponents. On $F$-ideles the finite units are trivial, the local uniformizers have the specified values, and each real component gives its sign because the exponent is odd. These local identities prove the restriction.

## The correction on the host

For $n_i=1$, the host provides the following properties. It is unramified quadratic over the real field at every finite place; the rational primes in its conductor split in the quadratic extension over the real field. The conductor is divisible by $2$, by $d$, and by the order of the roots of unity of $E$. The chosen CM embeddings all send the distinguished $\sqrt{-d}$ to $i\sqrt d$, and their number is divisible by $4$. Also there is a fixed-point-free pairing of this CM type that sends the value of any root of unity to its inverse. This pairing is multiplication by the order-two element in the chosen subgroup of the host Galois group, whose action on roots of unity is inversion.

If $\zeta=-1$, take $\delta=\sqrt{-d}$. Its quotient by its conjugate is $-1$, and it is a unit outside the primes dividing $d$. Its phase is $i$ at every chosen complex embedding, so the product of phases is $1$. If $\zeta\ne-1$, take $\delta=1+\zeta$. Then $c(\zeta)=\zeta^{-1}$ proves the quotient identity. Outside the conductor the roots of unity of order dividing the conductor reduce injectively, since their order is prime to the residue characteristic. For example, a prime-to-residue-characteristic root reducing to $1$ must be $1$ by expanding its power equation at $1$ and comparing valuations. Hence $1+\zeta$ is a unit at all those primes. Pairing the chosen embeddings sends $1+\varphi_i(\zeta)$ to its complex conjugate, so the corresponding normalized phases cancel. Since every inert place lies away from the conductor, these two choices establish (1).
