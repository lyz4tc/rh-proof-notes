# An invertible character period controls the Weil–Deligne twist

**Status: Source-based reconstruction draft.** This proves the local, rank-one Weil–Deligne tensor identity used in the good-scope spherical comparison. The final application specifies the normalization on both sides. It is not the common-twist argument elsewhere in the proof.

Draft reconstructed from the pinned Lean sources with AI assistance from Dot and checked against the cited source by Codex.

## Statement

Fix a prime $\ell$, an algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$, and a field isomorphism $\iota:\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\simeq\mathbb C$. Fix a local period package $P$, its $A_{\inf}$ package, and the existence of a compatible tie between the two period realizations. Let $E$ be a number field, $\Omega$ an algebraic closure of $E$, $w$ a finite place of $E$, and $A$ a valuation ring of $\Omega$ above $w$. Write $D$ for the decomposition group at $A$ and $W_w\subseteq D$ for the Weil group at this place. Use a single local bridge $\mathcal B$ satisfying the pointed embedding condition `Induces` in Lean. In particular its prescribed isomorphism $D\simeq G_K$ to the absolute Galois group of a finite extension $K/\mathbb Q_\ell$ is compatible with the embedding used in the period constructions. Use the same place, bridge and $\iota$ for all three parameters.

Let $r:D\to\mathrm{GL}_a(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell)$ and $\mu:D\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell^\times$ be representations with continuous matrix entries, the latter regarded as a representation on a line. Assume that $r$, $\mu$, and the entrywise scalar twist $r\otimes\mu$ are each potentially semistable at $\mathcal B$. More precisely, each admits a finite coefficient model that is potentially semistable after transport by $\mathcal B$. Then
$$
\operatorname{WD}_{\mathcal B}(r\otimes\mu)=
 \operatorname{WD}_{\mathcal B}(r)\otimes\operatorname{WD}_{\mathcal B}(\mu).
$$
This equality concerns Frobenius-semisimplified Weil–Deligne isomorphism classes, including the monodromy operator. The rank-one parameter has zero monodromy, so tensoring by it scales the Weil action and carries the old monodromy to its tensor with the identity of the line. The tensor identity alone neither proves potential semistability of the three representations nor identifies the rank-one character by reciprocity.

## The common finite coefficient model

Choose finite coefficient models over $L_1,L_2,L_3\subseteq\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ for $r,\mu,r\otimes\mu$ respectively, and put $L=L_1L_2L_3$. The [coefficient comparison](enlarging-coefficients-preserves-the-local-weil-deligne-parameter.md) gives two needed facts: each enlarged model remains potentially semistable, and its specialized complex parameter is unchanged. The scalar twist of the first two enlarged models is an $L$-model of $r\otimes\mu$: its matrix entries agree with those of $r\otimes\mu$ after inclusion into $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$. The enlargement of the third model and this scalar twist therefore agree entry by entry, because the inclusion of $L$ into $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ is injective. This is why the argument needs no simultaneous model at the outset and no identification of independently chosen complex specializations. Transport the common-field models along $\mathcal B$ and write them as $V$, $L$ with character $\mu$, and $V$ with the twisted action.

## An invertible period, not merely a nonzero vector

Set $B_{\mathrm{st}}=B_{\mathrm{st}}(P)$ and $F_{\mathrm{ur}}=\mathbb Q_\ell^{\mathrm{ur}}$. The precise local input is a period $y$ of the rank-one model, fixed under the diagonal action over some finite extension of $K$, and an inverse $y^{-1}$ in $L\otimes_{\mathbb Q_\ell}B_{\mathrm{st}}$. The inverse need not belong to the same period space. Nor does mere nonvanishing of $y$ establish invertibility in that tensor algebra.

The [companion construction, “The fixed vectors give enough coefficients”](a-potentially-semistable-character-has-an-invertible-period.md#the-fixed-vectors-give-enough-coefficients), starts with an $F_{\mathrm{ur}}$-basis of the rank-one period space, of size $[L:\mathbb Q_\ell]$. Its image in $B_{\mathrm{st}}\otimes L$ is independent over $B_{\mathrm{st}}$: choose a single finite-extension subgroup that fixes the basis for the twisted action; in a shortest relation, compare the relation with its Galois transforms to show the ratios of the coefficients are fixed. Semistable period-ring regularity puts those ratios in $F_{\mathrm{ur}}$, contradicting the original independence.

In [“Descend a nonzero norm to period scalars”](a-potentially-semistable-character-has-an-invertible-period.md#descend-a-nonzero-norm-to-period-scalars), the adjugate of the coordinate matrix produces a combination of these fixed vectors over $B_{\mathrm{st}}$ with nonzero algebra norm. The norm is a polynomial in the coefficients, so some tuple from the infinite field $F_{\mathrm{ur}}$ also gives nonzero norm. This second choice is important: it keeps the combination in the space of potentially semistable periods. Finally, [“Turn the norm into a unit”](a-potentially-semistable-character-has-an-invertible-period.md#turn-the-norm-into-a-unit) shows that the selected combination has norm $n$ transforming, on a finite-extension subgroup, by the $\mathbb Q_\ell$-scalar $\mathrm N_{L/\mathbb Q_\ell}(\mu(g))^{-1}$. Thus $n$ is a nonzero stable-line element of $B_{\mathrm{st}}$, which makes it a unit by the period-ring stable-line property. As $n$ is the determinant of multiplication by $y$, that multiplication is invertible and its preimage of $1$ is the required inverse. The companion proof reconstructs these steps from the actual `exists_unit_Dpst` body, including the passage from crystalline to semistable stable lines.

## Multiplication and division before complex specialization

On the tensor spaces over $\mathbb Q_\ell$, the period product is
$$
(v\otimes b,\ c\otimes d)\longmapsto cv\otimes bd
 \quad(v\in V,\ c\in L,\ b,d\in B_{\mathrm{st}}).
$$
It is equivariant for the diagonal Galois actions: the character factor on the line is precisely the extra factor on the product. Thus two periods fixed over different finite extensions yield a period of the twisted representation over the compositum. The product is balanced for both scalar actions. In more detail, multiplying either input by a coefficient in $L$ multiplies the product by that coefficient; multiplying either input by an unramified scalar in $F_{\mathrm{ur}}$ multiplies the product by its image in the period ring. The specialized complex space $W(s)$ is spanned by the symbols $[x]$ for periods $x$, with linearity and scalar relations $[\lambda x]=\iota(\lambda)[x]$ for $L$ and $[c x]=\iota(c)[x]$ for $F_{\mathrm{ur}}$. Both scalar actions on each period are used here, through the respective inclusions into $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$. The balanced product induces
$$
 \Phi: W(r)\otimes_{\mathbb C}W(\mu)\longrightarrow W(r\otimes\mu),
 \qquad [x]\otimes [y]\longmapsto [xy].
$$

For every period $z$ of the twisted representation, choose a single finite-extension subgroup over which $z$ and the invertible $y$ above are fixed for their respective actions. Without the factor $\mu(g)$, both $z$ and $y$ transform by $\mu(g)^{-1}$ on that subgroup. The ordinary action on $y^{-1}$ transforms it by $\mu(g)$. Thus $z\,y^{-1}$ belongs to the untwisted period space and has product $z$ with $y$. Since the symbols $[z]$ span the target, $\Phi$ is surjective. The potentially semistable rank formula for the specialized spaces gives dimensions $a$, $1$ and $a$ for $W(r)$, $W(\mu)$ and $W(r\otimes\mu)$ respectively, so $\Phi$ is an isomorphism.

## Compatibility with the Weil action and monodromy

Before specialization the source's Weil operator for $g\in W_w$ is the diagonal action of its image in $G_K$ composed with the period Frobenius to the power $-\alpha(g)$, where $\alpha(g)=\operatorname{resDeg}(\ell,w)\deg(g)$ and $\operatorname{resDeg}(\ell,w)$ is the residue degree of $w$ over $\ell$. The Galois action and Frobenius commute with the period product in the sense that acting on the product is the same as acting on the two factors. For the negative Frobenius powers this follows from the same identity and the invertibility of Frobenius on the period spaces. Thus the Weil operators intertwine under $\Phi$. The period monodromy is a derivation, so the monodromy intertwined with the target's operator is $N_r\otimes1+1\otimes N_\mu$. Both checks on the spanning pure period symbols suffice for the specialized spaces.

The line has nilpotent monodromy and dimension one, hence $N_\mu=0$. For a Frobenius lift, multiplying the original Frobenius operator by a nonzero line scalar scales the semisimple factor of its multiplicative Jordan decomposition and leaves its unipotent factor unchanged. In particular the line scalar commutes with taking the semisimple factor. The Lean proof uses the general tensorial Frobenius-semisimplification theorem to pass from the intertwiner of the raw pairs to the isomorphism of Frobenius-semisimplified Weil–Deligne pairs. This completes the local identity.

## The same scalar on both sides of the spherical comparison

At the split place used in `holdsS_good_phi`, the proof has an algebraic Hecke character $\kappa$, unramified at $w$, and the $\ell$-adic character $\mu$ attached to it by the global reciprocity input. The twist $\pi'=\pi\otimes(\kappa\circ\det)$ is in the conjugate-self-dual scope, and the unramified twist preserves sphericality at $w$. The crystalline clause applied to $\pi'$ gives a crystalline, hence semistable, model for $r\otimes\mu$ at the bridge. The inverse line is crystalline by the rank-one Hecke-character comparison, so twisting by that inverse line gives semistability of $r$ via the semistable-by-crystalline tensor result. The forward line is de Rham by the same Hecke-character input, and hence potentially semistable. Thus all the hypotheses of the local tensor identity hold. Separately, the rank-one Weil–Deligne identification `AlgebraicCharacterWD` supplies the character of the line as the composite of the local $\kappa_w$ with inverse local reciprocity.

Here the direction and sign of reciprocity matter. Lean uses a map $\operatorname{art}_w:W_w\to E_w^\times$ whose defining condition is
$$
 \operatorname{ord}_w(\operatorname{art}_w(g))=-\deg(g),
$$
where $\operatorname{ord}_w$ takes a uniformizer to $1$. Thus a Weil element of degree $-1$ maps to a uniformizer times a unit, whereas degree $+1$ gives valuation $-1$. If $c=\kappa(w)$ denotes Lean's `κ.val w`, then `loc_eq_unrChar` says $\kappa_w(x)=c^{\operatorname{ord}_w(x)}$. Consequently the character multiplying the Galois Weil operator at $g$ is
$$
 (\kappa_w\circ\operatorname{art}_w)(g)=c^{-\deg(g)}.
$$
This is a statement about the normalized Weil–Deligne line, not direct evaluation of $\iota\mu(g)$ when the local line need not be unramified as an $\ell$-adic Galois representation.

On the spherical side, twisting by $\kappa$ multiplies each member of the Satake multiset $B$ by $c$. The definition of `satWD` first inverts each member of $B$ to form the unramified Weil parameter and then applies the fixed twist with exponent $(1-a)/2$. Replacing $b$ by $bc$ replaces its inverse by $b^{-1}c^{-1}$; the fixed normalization has not changed. This corresponds to tensoring by the unramified Weil character with base $c^{-1}$, namely $g\mapsto(c^{-1})^{\deg(g)}$, by `satWD_map_mul`. Thus **both** Galois and spherical operators at $g$ have been multiplied by the same scalar $s_g=c^{-\deg(g)}$, not by its inverse on one side.

The conjugate-self-dual comparison gives equality of the two twisted characteristic polynomials. Scaling a rank-$a$ operator by $s_g$ changes its characteristic polynomial $P(X)$ into $s_g^aP(X/s_g)$. Substituting $s_gX$ and canceling $s_g^a\ne0$ gives equality of the untwisted polynomials. The Lean proof applies `charPol_lpTwist_iff` to precisely this common Weil character. This application concludes equality of characteristic polynomials at each $g$; no stronger identification of the original spherical and Galois parameters is required in this step.
