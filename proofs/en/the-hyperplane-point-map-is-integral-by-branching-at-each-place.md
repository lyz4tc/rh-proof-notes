# The hyperplane point map is integral by branching at each place

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

## Statement

Let $K$ be the common coefficient number field, embedded in $\mathbb C$, and $E$ the host field. Fix a word and a tuple of the built column family. Let $M\subset V_M$ be the coefficient-field lattice of last column forms and $P\subset V_P$ the coefficient-field lattice of hyperplane forms after restricting the last column. Let $j_M,j_P$ be their injective semilinear embeddings into the complex form spaces $F_M,F_P$. These spaces are the complex spans of the corresponding coefficient-field forms. The hyperplane map $L:F_M\to F_P$ is evaluation at the chosen class representative followed by branching. For this statement assume $L(F_M)\subseteq F_P$, the terminal and hyperplane weights at each embedding are interlaced, and their prescribed determinant shifts agree. Use the compatible local frames and coordinate tests described below. Then $L$ has a unique $K$-linear realization $L_K:V_M\to V_P$, meaning $j_P L_K=L j_M$, and $L_K(M)\subseteq P$. For a twisted tuple this proves the field and integral-lattice conditions `PRat` and `PtilIntegral`. The same local argument is used for the base tuple.

## Local coordinate calculation

Write $n+1$ for the terminal dimension and $n$ for the hyperplane dimension. At each finite place $w$ and hyperplane class $c$, use an $E$-basis $B_P$ of the localized hyperplane lattice and a basis $B_M=\operatorname{diag}(B_P,t^{-1})$ of the corresponding terminal lattice in the coordinates of the restricted class. Here $t\in E^\times$ is the scalar of the distinguished line. These are the frame conditions checked for `bP` and `bM`. The terminal form value is first transported to this representative of the restricted class. It lies in the transported lattice by equivariance of the terminal form. The two local polynomial coordinate maps take values in the dual frames of these bases. In the source they are `coM` and `coP`.

For a family of weights $\lambda_\tau$, denote by $I_\lambda(w)$ the $\mathcal O_w$-span of tensors of integral Weyl polynomials, viewed over $\mathbb C$ by the embedding of $K$. Once a local frame is fixed, this describes precisely the localized lattice of polynomial values at the class. To see the coordinate rule, transport the localized primal lattice to the standard one in its basis, which transports the localized dual lattice in the inverse transpose. Forming the Weyl lattice in each factor sends a frame change to the polynomial action. It respects localization since its standard generators are integral polynomials and the module they span extends by allowing coefficients in $\mathcal O_w$. Thus the transported tensor coordinates are exactly $I_\lambda(w)$. The natural weight, obtained by subtracting the determinant shift, defines the standard lattice; adding a common shift to all weight components preserves this lattice. This is the local form of `mem_locW_delAtr_iff`.

The restriction in physical coordinates is the standard tensor branching map multiplied in each embedding by the weight character of the last coordinate $t$. In the compatible frames that scalar disappears: $B_M^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(B_P^{-1},t)$. The standard branching intertwines the upper-left block action with the corresponding action on the hyperplane, and the last block acts on the output by the last-coordinate character. Applying these two identities for each embedding yields
$$
\mathrm{coP}_{w,c}(L\phi)=
\left(\bigotimes_\tau \mathrm{br}_{\lambda_\tau,\gamma_\tau}\right)
\mathrm{coM}_{w,c}\bigl(\phi\ \text{at the matching terminal class}\bigr).
\tag{1}
$$
These are the equivariance properties of `brOne` in `IsBr`. The additional property used here is its integral restriction: for all interlaced weights with the same shift it maps the integral Weyl lattice for $\lambda_\tau$ into that for $\gamma_\tau$. Therefore (1) takes $I_\lambda(w)$ into $I_\gamma(w)$: apply the maps to each integral tensor generator and extend by linearity over $\mathcal O_w$.

We explain why the integral coordinates on the hyperplane give a coefficient-field form. For each class, write its coordinate polynomial as a sum of integral tensors with coefficients in $\mathcal O_w$. This is an actual polynomial over $K$, since all coefficient maps into $\mathbb C$ are injective. Undo the coordinate change using $B_P$ and the embeddings of $E$ in $K$; this gives the corresponding hyperplane polynomial over $K$. The complex class function is in $F_P$, so its values obey the stabilizer equivariance equations. The recovered polynomials obey the same equations over $K$, since their embeddings do and the polynomial-to-function maps at each class are injective. Hence they form one coefficient-field row. The coordinate test places each component in its local value lattice. The integrality test is simultaneously valid for the whole row: clear the local denominators separately at all its finitely many classes by scalars outside $w$, then use their product to clear all of them. Conversely any local integral row passes the test in each class.

## Global integrality

Let $M_w,P_w$ be the localizations of $M,P$ at $w$. For $x\in M_w$, evaluation and transport put its terminal coordinates in $I_\lambda(w)$. By (1) its image has coordinate polynomials in $I_\gamma(w)$. The row descent just proved supplies $y_w\in P_w$ with $j_P(y_w)=Lj_M(x)$. For a single fixed $w$, $M_w$ spans $V_M$ over $K$. Linearity and semilinearity now give a $K$-linear map $L_K$ on all of $V_M$ by taking the same $K$-linear combinations of the $y_w$'s. It is independent of those combinations by injectivity of $j_P$. This proves coefficient-field rationality and uniqueness.

For $x\in M$, the preimages $y_w$ at all places are equal to $L_K(x)$ by injectivity again. If $L_K(x)\notin P$, the ideal $\{b\in\mathcal O_K:b L_K(x)\in P\}$ is proper. At a maximal ideal containing it, no scalar outside the ideal of the place clears the denominator, contrary to local membership there. This proves $L_K(x)\in P$. The conclusion applies to all twists for which the local construction and the interlacing hold, so in particular to the used nonbase tuples.
