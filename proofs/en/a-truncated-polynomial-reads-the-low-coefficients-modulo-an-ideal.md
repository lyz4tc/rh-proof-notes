# A truncated polynomial reads the low coefficients modulo an ideal

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $O$ be a commutative ring, $\ell$ a prime, $\nu\ge0$, $M\ge\nu+1$, and $I\subset O$ an ideal containing $\ell^{\nu+1}$. For a finite index set $J$, put $\Lambda_J=O[(\mathbb Z/\ell^M)^J]$ and $H=\ell^{M-\nu-1}$. Write $s_j$ for the group element in coordinate $j$ that corresponds to $1$ in $\mathbb Z/\ell^M$. The map $\lambda:O[X_j:j\in J]\to\Lambda_J$ sends $X_j$ to $s_j-1$. If $\lambda(F)\in I\Lambda_J$, then every coefficient of $F$ of total degree $<H$ lies in $I$. In the Selmer application $I$ contains $(\ell^\nu)\mathfrak m_O$, which contains $\ell^{\nu+1}$ because $\ell\in\mathfrak m_O$.

Set $B=(O/I)[X_j:j\in J]/(X_j:j\in J)^H$. Write $x_j$ for the image of $X_j$. Each $1+x_j$ is a unit, with inverse given by a finite geometric sum. For $1\le a<H$, the identity
$$
a\binom{\ell^M}{a}=\ell^M\binom{\ell^M-1}{a-1}
$$
gives $v_\ell\bigl(\binom{\ell^M}{a}\bigr)\ge M-v_\ell(a)\ge \nu+1$. The terms of degree $\ge H$ vanish by truncation, so $(1+x_j)^{\ell^M}=1$. When $H=1$, all the $x_j$ are zero and the same conclusion holds. The rule $s_j\mapsto1+x_j$ therefore defines an $O$-algebra homomorphism $\Lambda_J\to B$. It sends $I\Lambda_J$ to zero, and its composite with $\lambda$ is ordinary reduction and truncation. The coefficients of the monomials of degree $<H$ in this quotient are independent over $O/I$.

One consequence used for the induction is the following. Fix excluded coordinates $K\subseteq J$ and $c\le H$. The polynomials whose $K$-free coefficients of total degree $<c$ all lie in $I$ form an ideal. In a coefficient of a product, each contributing exponent of one factor is bounded coordinatewise by the target exponent. If $I_0\subseteq I$ also contains $\ell^{\nu+1}$, and the image of $F$ in $\Lambda_J/I_0\Lambda_J$ is in the ideal generated by images of such polynomials, then $F$ itself belongs to this ideal of polynomials. Indeed lift each multiplier to the polynomial ring, which is possible because the group ring is generated by the $s_j=1+X_j$ and $s_j^{-1}=s_j^{\ell^M-1}$. Subtract the lifted products and apply the coefficient test with $I_0$.
