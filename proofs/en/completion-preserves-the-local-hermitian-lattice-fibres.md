# Completion preserves the local Hermitian lattice fibres

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E/F$ be a quadratic extension of number fields, with the conjugation on $E$, and $V$ a nondegenerate Hermitian space. Fix a finite place $\mathfrak p$ of $F$, a rational vector $x_0\in V$ with nonzero norm, and full lattices $L\subset V$ and $T\subset x_0^\perp$. Localizing a lattice here means allowing denominators from $\mathcal O_F\setminus\mathfrak p$ inside its rational vector space. Completing means extending the resulting module to $\mathcal O_{F_\mathfrak p}$. In the formal local fibre, the lattices counted are rationally isometric translates of the localized $L$ that contain $x_0$ and cut in $x_0^\perp$ to the localized $T$. Completion gives a bijection from this set to the corresponding fibre of completed lattices using the local unitary group. It also gives the same isometry classes for the complementary lattices and preserves the congruence counts used in [the mass ratio argument](two-limits-of-one-lattice-count-give-the-siegel-mass-ratio.md). The comparison below explains the use of completed coordinates in the companion proofs. ## Recover a localized lattice by intersection

A full lattice over a number field can be compared with a fixed free $\mathcal O_E$-lattice by a positive integer multiple in both directions. After localizing at $\mathfrak p$, and then after completing, the lattices lie between the same multiples of the local reference lattice. The two finite quotient modules between the bounding multiples are identified by completion, because each quotient is killed by a power of $\mathfrak p$, and $\mathcal O_{F,\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak p^k=\mathcal O_{F_\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak p^k$. Thus the completed lattice determines the localized one by rational intersection. Every completed lattice in the space arises this way, using local commensurability to choose the multiples and then patching with the reference lattice away from $\mathfrak p$. Rational intersection shows that the membership of $x_0$ and the equality of the cuts correspond. For the cut, the completed intersection with $x_0^\perp$ is the completion of the localized cut, since a rational basis of $x_0^\perp$ spans the completed complementary space, and its rational points are dense there. Every point of the completed intersection can be approximated arbitrarily closely by rational points in the same intersection, which is open in the completed complement. The finite quotients of the resulting integral lattices, and reductions of the Hermitian pairings on them, are unchanged by completion. ## Approximate a local unitary isometry by rational ones

On the unitary group consider matrices $g$ with $g+1$ invertible. They are parametrized by the Cayley map $g=(1-X)(1+X)^{-1}$, where $X$ is skew-adjoint for the Hermitian form and $1+X$ is invertible. Indeed $X=(1-g)(1+g)^{-1}$ has adjoint $-X$ if $g^*g=1$ for the Hermitian adjoint, and the converse follows by multiplication. The space of skew-adjoint endomorphisms is defined by a linear equation over $F$, so it has a rational basis. Its rational points are dense in that space over $F_\mathfrak p$, as $F$ is dense in its completion. The rational Cayley parameters therefore approximate every local isometry in this open part.

This open part is dense in the local unitary group. For any fixed local $g$, multiply $g$ by central norm-one scalars tending to $1$, avoiding the finitely many values that make $\lambda g+1$ singular. There are infinitely many such scalars in every neighborhood of $1$. For instance choose a nonzero skew element $b$ of the quadratic local algebra and use $(1-tb)/(1+tb)$ for small $t\in F_\mathfrak p$. In the split case $b$ can be taken with nonzero entries in both factors, and the determinant condition excludes finitely many $t$ in each factor. This proves density of the rational unitary group. The same argument applies to $x_0^\perp$ and hence, by extending its isometries, to the rational point stabilizer of $x_0$.

For a completed isometry sending one lattice to another, all isometries in a sufficiently small open neighborhood of it have that same effect, since the lattice stabilizer is open. Approximate by a rational isometry in that neighborhood. Its effect on the localized lattices is then the desired one by rational intersection. This proves both correspondences of isometry classes, with and without the vector fixed. The fibre correspondence follows, including its count when it is finite. The formal proof works directly with the localizations and relative indices, whose definitions are linked above.
