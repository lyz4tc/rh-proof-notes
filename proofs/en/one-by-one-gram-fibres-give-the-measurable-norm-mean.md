# One-by-one Gram fibres give the measurable norm mean

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E/F$ be any quadratic CM extension of number fields, $W$ a nondegenerate Hermitian $E$-space of rank $n\ge3$, and $\beta\in F^\times$. Let $G=U(W)$, and assume the finite positive Haar volume of $G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A_F)$. Let $\Phi_v$ be nonnegative Schwartz–Bruhat functions at finite places, equal to a coordinate-lattice indicator at almost every place for some fixed rational coordinates. Put $\Phi_f=\prod_{v<\infty}\Phi_v$ on the finite adeles. For the one-vector Gram map use the disintegrations $\omega_{\infty,\beta}$ and $\omega_{v,\beta}$ of trace-self-dual additive measures relative to multiplication-self-dual measures on the norm line. The additive character is any nontrivial continuous character of $\mathbb A_F/F$. Assume that the convergent Gram-form density formula holds for smooth compactly supported archimedean tests and this pure finite tensor. This formula is stated below in the exact special case needed for its application to the norm map.

**Claim.** For every nonnegative Borel function $g$ on $W_\infty$, with extended integrals, one has
$$
 \operatorname{mean}_{G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A_F)}
 \sum_{Q(x)=\beta}
  g((a^{-1}x)_\infty)\Phi_f((a^{-1}x)_f)
 =
 \left(\int g\,d\omega_{\infty,\beta}\right)
 \prod_{v<\infty}\int\Phi_v\,d\omega_{v,\beta},
 \qquad Q(x)=h(x,x).
$$
The product is a convergent product of nonnegative real numbers. The equality uses the nonnegative measure convention $0\cdot\infty=0$. In particular taking $\Phi_v=\mathbf1_{L_v}$ gives the hypothesis used by the [unitary volume comparison](two-evaluations-of-the-one-vector-mean-give-the-volume-ratio.md).

### The rank-one input and its measures

In the convergent Gram-form formula, the target is the space of Hermitian $j$-by-$j$ matrices over the scalar extension of $E/F$, the source is $j$ vectors of $W$, and $2j<n$. The formula identifies the mean sum over rational tuples of prescribed nondegenerate Gram matrix $T$ with the product of the continuous local Gram densities at $T$. The tests are smooth compactly supported at infinity and pure Schwartz–Bruhat at the finite places. The source measure uses the trace of the Hermitian form on the tuple space and the target measure uses the Hermitian matrix trace pairing, both self-dual for the chosen additive character. The tested local complex densities are the continuous densities of the pushforward of the test times the corresponding source measure, relative to the target measure, on the nonsingular target locus. The formula includes convergence of the product and integrability of the mean for these compactly supported tests. Use $j=1$ and $T=(\beta)$, which is nonsingular.

Over every completion $R=F_v$, or over the product of the real completions, a Hermitian $1$-by-$1$ matrix is a fixed scalar in $R\otimes_F E$. The map $t\mapsto[t]$ from $R$ is an additive isomorphism onto this target, and its inverse is half the trace to $R$. Indeed the involution and the trace satisfy $z+\bar z=\operatorname{Tr}(z)$. The target pairing of two such matrices reduces to $st$, and the single Gram entry of $x$ is $Q(x)$. Consequently the pushforward of the self-dual measure on $R$ is self-dual on the Hermitian target, and transport of a continuous Gram density is exactly a continuous density for $Q$. No Jacobian factor remains in this identification because both measures are transported with their pairings. Smooth compact support implies the rapid decay required in the source.

The disintegration of $Q$ on the nonzero levels is continuous on compactly supported continuous tests. One can see this directly: at a nonzero norm vector $x$ the differential maps $x/(2Q(x))$ to $1$. On a coordinate chart in which one coordinate is $Q$, the fibre measure is given by change of variables in the remaining coordinates. For an archimedean compact support, join finitely many such charts with a partition of unity. At a nonarchimedean place, a compact open partition suffices within local inverse-function charts. Integrating the continuous density for each chart yields continuity on the nonzero norm levels. Two continuous densities equal almost everywhere relative to Haar are equal everywhere on those levels. Therefore the smooth Gram identity reads exactly as the displayed norm-mean equality for smooth nonnegative $g$ of compact support.

### The measure extension

Define a measure $m_1$ on $W_\infty$ by integrating over the unitary quotient the weighted point measures at $(a^{-1}x)_\infty$ for the rational norm-$\beta$ vectors. Their weights are $\Phi_f((a^{-1}x)_f)$, and use quotient probability measure. The rational vectors are countable. Tonelli, or the sum of the weighted pushforward measures on a fundamental domain, gives the formula for $m_1$ against every nonnegative Borel test. Define $m_2=C_f\omega_{\infty,\beta}$, where $C_f=\prod_v\int\Phi_v\,d\omega_{v,\beta}$ is finite. The preceding smooth identity says that $m_1,m_2$ agree on all smooth nonnegative compactly supported functions. In that argument the real test lies in Euclidean coordinates on $W_\infty$; since changes of additive coordinates are continuous linear maps over $\mathbb R$, the smoothness condition is independent of the choice.

For each compact set there is a nonnegative smooth compactly supported function at least $1$ there. Its $m_2$ integral is finite by local finiteness of $\omega_{\infty,\beta}$, so the same is true for $m_1$; both measures are locally finite. Continuous functions of compact support can be uniformly approximated with common compact support by smoothing with a compactly supported approximate identity. Their integrals therefore agree too. Local finiteness and regularity of Borel measures on Euclidean space imply equality of the two measures, for example by approximating the indicators of compact sets from above by continuous compactly supported tests and then applying inner regularity. Now integrating an arbitrary nonnegative Borel $g$ gives the conclusion.
