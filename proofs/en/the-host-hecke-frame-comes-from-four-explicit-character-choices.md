# The host Hecke frame comes from four explicit character choices

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E/F$ be the host's CM quadratic extension with involution $c$, and $E/K$ its extension over the auxiliary imaginary quadratic subfield $K$. Use the host CM type $(\varphi_\tau)$, which consists of the embeddings extending its chosen embedding of $K$. Assume the host properties used below: complete splitting in $E$ of the two generator primes $g_0,g_1$ and of the two distinguished primes $p_d,p_d'$, with $p_d,p_d'\equiv1\bmod15$; the norm identity $N_{E/K}\zeta=1$ for roots of unity; and the quadratic restriction and correction conditions from [the odd-type construction](a-root-of-unity-correction-globalizes-the-odd-archimedean-character.md). At each layer indexed by $j=0,\ldots,m-1$, prescribe integral exponents $e_{j,\tau}$ with
$$
 \prod_\tau\varphi_\tau(\zeta)^{e_{j,\tau}}=1
 \qquad\text{for every root of unity }\zeta\in E.
 \tag{1}
$$
This is the weight congruence used in the source. Then a frame exists with the following characters and residue data.

First choose one prime of $K$ over each $g_i$ and an injective residue character $\omega_i:\mathbb F_{g_i}^\times\to S^1$. A choice is possible because these primes are split and each residue-unit group is cyclic. On the $E$-primes over the chosen $K$-prime prescribe the residue character $\omega_i^{-2(j+1)}$, via their canonical identification of residue fields with $\mathbb F_{g_i}$. The conjugate set of primes is disjoint because $c$ exchanges the two underlying $K$-primes. For a root of unity $\zeta$, the product of its residues over this chosen fibre is the residue of $N_{E/K}\zeta$, since the relative local extensions are of degree one. Thus the product of the corresponding residue character values is $1$ for each $i$. The infinite contribution on roots of unity is $1$ by (1). Apply the [norm-one extension](a-norm-one-unit-test-globalizes-tame-cm-characters.md) to both fibres together with exponents $e_{j,\tau}$. This gives $\mu_{0,j}$ trivial on the ideles of $F$, of angular type $-2e_{j,\tau}$, tame and unramified away from $g_0,g_1$, with exactly those residue prescriptions.

The unramified angular-type-$2$ character $\chi_\Psi$ is the same norm-one extension with $S=\varnothing$ and $e_\tau=-1$ for every $\tau$. Here the root-of-unity condition follows directly from $N_{E/K}\zeta=1$: the product over the chosen CM embeddings is the chosen embedding of that norm. The character is trivial on $F$-ideles. The character $\eta$ in the frame has angular exponent $1$, is unramified at finite places, and restricts to the quadratic character of $E/F$. It is the character obtained from the odd-type argument under the stated host conditions.

For each of $p=p_d,p_d'$, repeat the fibre construction with a single $K$-prime above $p$ and a residue character of exact order $15$. Such a character exists by cyclicity and $15\mid p-1$. Take zero infinite exponents. The same residue norm identity verifies the root-of-unity condition, so the resulting character is trivial on $F$-ideles and unramified away from $p$. The two $K$-prime fibres exhaust the $E$-primes over $p$. The residue characters on the chosen fibre have order $15$ and those on the opposite fibre are conjugate inverses, hence have the same order. Because the infinite component is trivial and the finite conductor is supported on this finite tame modulus, the character factors through the finite ray class group of that modulus. In particular it has finite global order. These two choices are the determinant characters $\delta,\delta'$. All choices can be made in one frame, since the two generator residue characters are fixed first and all remaining choices are finite or indexed by the finitely many layers.
