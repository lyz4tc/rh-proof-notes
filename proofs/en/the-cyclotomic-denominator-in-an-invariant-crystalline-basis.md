# The cyclotomic denominator in an invariant crystalline basis

**Status: Source-based reconstruction draft.** This expands the exact unit criterion called by `invU_holds`, then explains why it permits a basis over the rational crystalline period ring. It does not assume that the Teichmüller difference has already been inverted, or that Fontaine's map extends to the full localized ring.

Draft reconstructed from the pinned Lean sources with AI assistance from Dot. This prose was subsequently checked against the cited source by Codex.

## 1. Period rings and statement

Let $p$ be any prime, $R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}$, and $R^\flat$ its tilt. Use $A_{\inf}=W(R^\flat)$, Teichmüller representatives $[e]$, and Fontaine's map $\theta:A_{\inf}\to R$, with $\theta([e])=e^\sharp$ (the untilt of $e$). Write $A_0$ for the archive's uncompleted divided-power ring `AcrisZero`: the $A_{\inf}$-subalgebra of $A_{\inf}[1/p]$ generated by the divided powers of $\ker\theta$. Write $A=A_{\rm cris}$ for its $p$-adic completion, $B^+=A[1/p]$, and $B=B_{\rm cris}$ for the localization of $B^+$ at the multiplicative set generated by the logarithms of the primitive cyclotomic systems. In the source, such a system $e$ has $e^\sharp=1$ and its first inverse-Frobenius coordinate is not $1$. Write $a_B:A_{\inf}\to B$ for the composite of the period-ring maps.

**Claim (`CrisLoc.unit_of_root_aB`).** For every $e\in R^\flat$ with $e^\sharp=1$ and $e\ne1$, the scalar $a_B([e]-1)$ is a unit **in $B$**. The proof uses two stages of localization: $p$ is already a unit in $B^+$, and the primitive logarithms become units by the definition of $B$. It asserts neither invertibility of $[e]-1$ in $A_{\inf}$ nor invertibility in $A$. Indeed the respective Fontaine maps send it to zero; the same obstruction applies in $B^+$ using the map to $\mathbb C_p$ after inverting $p$.

## 2. Divisibility before localization

We first show the integral identity used in the actual route. For any $u\in A_{\inf}$ with $\theta(u)=1$, set $z=u-1$, and denote images in $A_0$ and $A$ by the same letters. Then
$$
z\mid p\log(u)\quad\text{in } A.
$$
In particular, no inverse of $z$ is used to prove this divisibility.

Here is the finite logarithm calculation. Put $M=p^n$. In $A_0$, the element
$$
L_n=\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}(M-1)(M-2)\cdots(M-j)\,\gamma_{j+1}(z)
$$
has $p^nL_n=u^{p^n}-1$. For $j=0$ the empty product is $1$. Multiply each term by $M$ and use $M(M-1)\cdots(M-j)=(j+1)!\binom{M}{j+1}$, giving the binomial expansion. Moreover, each coefficient is congruent to $(-1)^j j!$ modulo $p^n$, by reducing the factors modulo $M$. These latter coefficients are those of the crystalline logarithm on divided powers. The reduction of the logarithm modulo $p^n$ is represented by its partial sum through $j=M-1$ (`Acris.eval_log`); equivalently, $\log(u)$ and $L_n$ have the same image at level $n$. This is the exact two-part assertion of `Acris.exists_quot`; at $n=0$ we have $L_0=z$, whose residue is zero in $A_0/(p^0)$.

Construct $y_n\in A_0$ satisfying
$$
p^n z y_n=u^{p^{n+1}}-1,\qquad y_{n+1}-y_n\in p^n A_0.
$$
For $n=0$, take $y_0=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}u^i$. For the step put $v=u^{p^{n+1}}$. The quotient just proved gives $p^{n+1}\mid v-1$. Thus $p^{n+1}$ divides $\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(v^i-1)$, since $v-1$ divides each summand. Write $\sum_i v^i=p+p^{n+1}c_n$ with $c_n\in A_0$. Then $y_{n+1}=y_n(1+p^n c_n)$ has both required properties: multiply $p^n z y_n=v-1$ by the geometric sum to get $v^p-1$. In particular the reduction of $y_n$ modulo $p^n$ gives compatible residues and hence an element $y\in A$.

At level $n$, comparing $p^n z y_n$ with $p^{n+1}L_{n+1}$ gives $z y_n=p L_{n+1}$ in $A_0$. Cancellation of $p^n$ is legitimate here because $A_0$ is a subalgebra of a ring in which $p$ is a unit. Reducing $L_{n+1}$ to level $n$ gives the reduction of $\log(u)$ by its level $n+1$ identity and the transition map. Consequently $z y=p\log(u)$ at every level, hence in the completion. This is the divisibility argument in `UnitOfRootLog`, not the separate proof through a directly summed quotient of the logarithm series.

## 3. A nontrivial cyclotomic logarithm is a unit in $B$

A cyclotomic system is an element of the multiplicative kernel of the untilt map. Its coordinates $e_n=(\varphi^{-n}e)^\sharp$ satisfy $e_0=1$ and $e_{n+1}^p=e_n$. Here $\varphi$ is Frobenius on the perfect tilt. If $e\ne1$, some coordinate is not $1$, since the coordinates determine the tilt element. Take the least such index $n\ge1$. The system $e'=\varphi^{-(n-1)}e$ satisfies $(e')^\sharp=1$ and has first coordinate $e_n\ne1$, so it is primitive. Moreover $e=(e')^{p^{n-1}}$.

For such Teichmüller logarithms the power rule is $\log([v^m])=m\log([v])$. To see the additivity behind it, if $u^{p^k}=1+p^k L_k$ and $v^{p^k}=1+p^k M_k$, the quotient for $uv$ is $L_k+M_k+p^k L_kM_k$, which reduces to the sum at level $k$. Equality at every level gives $\log(uv)=\log(u)+\log(v)$ for $\theta(u)=\theta(v)=1$, and the power rule follows by induction. Therefore in $B$ the logarithm of $[e]$ is $p^{n-1}$ times the logarithm of $[e']$, a unit because $p$ and every primitive logarithm have been inverted. This shortest-coordinate proof is the same argument as Lean's induction on a nontrivial coordinate: when the first coordinate is $1$, shift once and multiply back by $p$.

Apply Section 2 to $u=[e]$, since $\theta([e])=1$. After mapping to $B$, the equation $([e]-1)y=p\log([e])$ has a unit on the right. A factor of a unit in a commutative ring is a unit; explicitly the inverse of $a_B([e]-1)$ is the image of $y$ multiplied by $(p\log([e]))^{-1}$. No use of the later `bcris_away` identification or of a local-ring criterion in $B_{\rm dR}^+$ is needed.

## 4. The precise use in `InvU`

Fix $K/\mathbb Q_p$, a Breuil–Kisin datum $\mathrm{bk}$ and a period $\tau\in A_{\inf}$ satisfying $\mathrm{bk.IsPer}(\tau)$. This condition includes the frame Frobenius equation $\varphi(\tau)=\mathrm{ev}(E)\tau$ for the datum's Eisenstein element $E$, and a factorization $\varphi(\tau)=\eta([e]-1)$ with $\eta\in A_{\inf}^\times$, $e^\sharp=1$, and a $p$th root of $e$ in the tilt whose untilt is not $1$. The last condition implies $e\ne1$ by uniqueness of $p$th roots in the perfect tilt. Put $f_I=i_A\circ\varphi:A_{\inf}\to A$ and $f=a_B\circ\varphi=j\circ f_I$, where $j:A\to B$. The unit needed is
$$
 w=f(\tau)=a_B(\eta)\,a_B([e]-1)\in B^\times.
$$
It is **not** an assertion about $\tau$ or $f_I(\tau)$ being a unit in the integral rings. The same $w$ works for every frame in the specified datum.

For a representation $\rho:G_K\to GL_d(\mathbb Z_p)$ and the frame at height $N$, take $Y,Y'\in M_d(A_{\inf})$ with $YY'=\tau^{2N}I$ and with the cleared lift condition for the frame in `InvU`: under the natural embedding from $A_{\inf}$ into the frame's Witt coefficient ring, the image of $Y$ is the image of $\tau^N$ times the frame matrix $T$. This condition yields $\tau^N\rho(g)g(Y)=g(\tau)^NY\varepsilon_g$, with $\varepsilon_g$ the frame cocycle. Take $Q,Q'\in M_d(A)$ with $QQ'=p^{2k}I$ and $f_I(\varepsilon_g)g(Q)=Q$ for every $g\in G_K$. Applying ring maps entrywise, define
$$
 U=w^{-N}f(Y)j(Q).
$$
It satisfies $w^NU=j(f_I(Y)Q)$. Multiplying $U$ on the right by $j(Q')f(Y')$ gives $w^N p^{2k}I$. That scalar is a unit in $B$, so $w^{-N}p^{-2k}j(Q')f(Y')$ is a right inverse. Over the commutative ring $B$ a right inverse of a square matrix is also its left inverse, as seen by taking determinants and using the adjugate. This is the matrix invertibility in `IsPerU`.

Finally, using Galois compatibility of $f$ and $j$, the cleared identity becomes $w^N\rho(g)g(f(Y))=g(w)^N f(Y)f(\varepsilon_g)$. Since $w$ and $g(w)$ are units, substitution into $\rho(g)g(U)$ cancels the scalar factors and leaves $w^{-N}f(Y)f(\varepsilon_g)g(j(Q))=U$. Thus the columns of $U$ are invariant under the diagonal semilinear action and form a basis after extension to $B$. The smaller invariant coefficient field used to view them as a basis of crystalline periods is a separate comparison input. Nothing here requires moving an inverse back to $A$; the integral comparison retains the cleared matrix $f_I(Y)Q$.
