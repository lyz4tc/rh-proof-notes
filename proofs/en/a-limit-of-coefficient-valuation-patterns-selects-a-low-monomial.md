# A limit of coefficient valuation patterns selects a low monomial

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $O$ be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer $\varpi$ and residue characteristic $\ell$, a prime. Let $v$ be the integral valuation. Use $r$ variables $X_1,\ldots,X_r$, and let $I$ be a finite index set. Given integers $D,M_0,\mu_x\ge0$ for $x\in I$, let $F_{M,x}$ be a family of multivariate $O$-polynomials for $M\ge M_0$, such that for every $M,x$ some monomial of total degree at most $D$ has coefficient valuation at most $\mu_x$. Then there is a spacing $a\ge0$ with the following property. For any nonnegative integers $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ with $e_{j+1}-e_j\ge a$ there is $W$ such that, for arbitrarily large $M$, every polynomial
$$
 H_{M,x}(T)=F_{M,x}\bigl((1+T)^{\ell^{e_1}}-1,\ldots,(1+T)^{\ell^{e_r}}-1\bigr)
$$
has some coefficient of degree at most $W$ with valuation at most $\mu_x$.

Put $B_0=1+\max_x\mu_x$, taking the maximum to be zero if the family is empty. For each degree bound $d$, record for every $x$ and every monomial of degree at most $d$ the valuation of its coefficient, truncated at $B_0$. For fixed $d$ this record has only finitely many possible values. By successive infinite pigeonhole choices there are nested infinite sets of $M$'s on which the records for $d=0,1,2,\ldots$ stabilize. The stabilized records are compatible and define one truncated coefficient pattern for each $x$. Every finite portion of the pattern occurs at arbitrarily large $M$. In particular the degree-$D$ hypothesis gives a monomial with pattern value at most $\mu_x$. Let $\nu_x\le\mu_x$ be the least pattern value over all monomials at index $x$.

For each $x$, among exponents whose coefficient has pattern value $\nu_x$, take the least $m(x)=(m_1,\ldots,m_r)$ in lexicographic order comparing the largest variable index first. This order on $\mathbb N^r$ has a least element on every nonempty subset, by minimizing one coordinate at a time. Choose $B$ bounding all entries of these finitely many exponents and choose $a$ with $\ell^a>rB$. Then for weights $c_i=\ell^{e_i}$, the contribution $\sum_{i<j}m_i(x)c_i$ is strictly less than $c_j$. Thus $m(x)$ is the unique exponent of least weighted degree among those with pattern value $\nu_x$. In fact, at the largest index $j$ where another exponent differs, it has increased by at least one, gaining $c_j$, and its loss in smaller coordinates is at most $\sum_{i<j}m_i(x)c_i$.

Let $W=\max_x\sum_i m_i(x)c_i$. Choose arbitrarily large $M$ matching the pattern through ordinary degree $W$. Terms of larger ordinary degree cannot enter coefficients at degrees at most $W$ after substitution, since all substitutions have zero constant term. The coefficients of the remaining terms at index $x$ are divisible by $\varpi^{\nu_x}$. Divide that truncated input by $\varpi^{\nu_x}$ and reduce modulo $\varpi$. In residue characteristic $\ell$ the substitutions reduce to $T^{c_i}$. The coefficient at degree $\sum_i m_i(x)c_i$ is the residue of the coefficient of $m(x)$ after division. It is nonzero, since no other exponent with pattern value $\nu_x$ has the same weighted degree. Therefore the corresponding coefficient in $H_{M,x}$ has valuation exactly $\nu_x\le\mu_x$. This argument works simultaneously for the finite family because $W$ and the chosen finite record were common.

In the application the polynomials are determinant lifts that depend on the auxiliary level $M$. This argument permits $M$ to be chosen arbitrarily large after the spacings and the degree bound have been set, even though the determinant lifts themselves depend on $M$.
