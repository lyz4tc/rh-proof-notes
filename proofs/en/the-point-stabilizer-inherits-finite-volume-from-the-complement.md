# The point stabilizer inherits finite volume from the complement

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E/F$, $W,h$, $G$, and $\Lambda$ be as in [the arithmetic covolume proof](the-thick-part-and-short-vector-count-give-finite-covolume.md), with $n=\dim_E W$, and take a nonzero isotropic $x\in\Lambda$. Write $H=\{g\in G: gx=x\}$ and let $\Gamma_x$ consist of the rational lattice isometries fixing $x$, embedded in $H$. Assume the finite arithmetic covolume theorem for integral lattices in every Hermitian space of rank less than $n$. Then $\Gamma_x$ has finite positive covolume in $H$ with respect to every Haar measure on $H$. This is the inductive input used in [the isotropic-vector unfolding](the-isotropic-vector-quotient-gives-a-weighted-ellipsoid.md). ## The lower-rank projection and its section

Choose a rational isotropic partner $w$ of $x$ with $h(x,w)=1$, and put $W_0=(Ex+Ew)^\perp$. Then at each place $x^\perp$ is the direct sum of $Ex$ with $W_0$ after scalar extension, so the action on $x^\perp/Ex$ defines a homomorphism $H\to L_\infty=U(W_0)(F_\infty)$. Its section extends a complementary isometry by the identity on the hyperbolic plane. The kernel is precisely $N_\infty$ described by [the two unipotent coordinates](horizontal-and-central-reduction-bound-the-unipotent-quotient.md). The projection and the section are continuous in the matrix entries. Every element has a unique form $nm$ with $n\in N_\infty$, $m\in L_\infty$, obtained by multiplying by the inverse of the section of its projection. This gives a homeomorphism from $N_\infty\times L_\infty$ to $H$. ## Lift a finite-index subgroup of the complementary stabilizer

Let $\Lambda_0=\Lambda\cap W_0$ and choose a positive integer $d$ with $dw\in\Lambda$, possible by fullness. For $z\in\Lambda$, its two hyperbolic projections after multiplication by $d$ belong to $\Lambda$: they are $h(dw,z)x$ and $h(x,z)dw$, with integral coefficients. It follows that $dP_0z\in\Lambda_0$, where $P_0$ is the projection to $W_0$. The index argument of [lattice-line reduction](a-finite-lattice-sandwich-controls-isotropic-line-orbits.md#bound-the-dual-index-on-the-complement) then gives a full integral lattice $\Lambda_0$ with finite dual index.

The kernel of the complementary lattice stabilizer acting on the finite additive group $\Lambda_0/d\Lambda_0$ has finite index. For each member $m$ of this kernel, let $\widetilde m$ act by $m$ on $W_0$ and fix the hyperbolic plane. For $z\in\Lambda$, $m(dP_0z)-dP_0z\in d\Lambda_0$, so $mP_0z-P_0z\in\Lambda_0$. Therefore $\widetilde m z-z\in\Lambda_0\subseteq\Lambda$. The same holds for its inverse, and $\widetilde m$ fixes $x$. Thus the projection of $\Gamma_x$ contains the archimedean image of this finite-index subgroup. Since $W_0$ has rank $n-2$, the inductive hypothesis gives a finite-volume domain for its full lattice stabilizer. Finitely many translates of that domain give a finite-volume almost-everywhere cover for the kernel, or equivalently for the reduction in $L_\infty$ used here. ## Cover the semidirect product by a set of finite measure

Let $C\subset N_\infty$ be the compact reduction set from the two unipotent coordinates. Let $T\subset L_\infty$ be the finite-measure cover for the projection of $\Gamma_x$. One can add a measure-zero exceptional set to $T$ if using a fundamental domain defined almost everywhere. Under $H=N_\infty L_\infty$, the product of Haar measures $dn\,dm$ is left Haar measure. Indeed multiplication by $n_0m_0$ sends $(n,m)$ to $(n_0m_0nm_0^{-1},m_0m)$, and conjugation by $m_0$ preserves $dn$. Haar uniqueness gives the same product up to a constant for any Haar measure on $H$.

For an element $g\in H$, first use $\Gamma_x$ to move its $L_\infty$-coordinate into $T$. Once it is there, use $\Gamma_x\cap N_\infty$ to move its $N_\infty$-coordinate into $C$ while leaving its $L_\infty$-coordinate fixed. The product $CT$ therefore covers the quotient and has finite Haar measure. The discrete subgroup $\Gamma_x$ admits a measurable fundamental domain. As in the thick-part cover, its volume is at most that of $CT$, because its own translates partition the group up to null sets. Its volume is positive because countably many of its translates cover a group of nonzero Haar measure.
