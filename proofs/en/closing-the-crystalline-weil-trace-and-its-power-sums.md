## Local setting and the two closure statements

Let $E$ be a number field, $\Omega/E$ an algebraic closure, $\ell$ a rational prime, and $\iota:\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\simeq\mathbb C$ a **field** isomorphism. All approximation bounds below are in $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$, after pulling back complex values through $\iota$; no continuity of $\iota$ is assumed. Fix a rank $a\ge1$. Choose a finite place $w$ of $E$ and a valuation ring $A$ of $\Omega$ over $w$, with decomposition group $D$. Use the source's period package $P$, its compatible $A_{\inf}$ package $AI$, and the nonempty space of ties between the two realizations; choose a tie $T$ in the proof. Let $B_w$ be a local bridge satisfying the source condition `Induces`, over a commutative $\mathbb Z_\ell$-algebra $O$ equipped with a topology. It provides a finite local field $K/\mathbb Q_\ell$, a topological group identification $e:D\simeq G_K$, the induced embedding $j_E:E\hookrightarrow K$, and a finite field $L_0\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ containing every image of $K$ under a $\mathbb Q_\ell$-embedding. These are bridge data, not further estimates on the representations.

Let $r:G_E\to\mathrm{GL}_a(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell)$ be continuous and semisimple, and let $C$ be a set of homomorphisms of the same rank. Fix a function $J$ giving an integer multiset $J(\tau)$ for every embedding $\tau:E\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$. The common seed hypotheses are that every $\rho\in C$ is continuous with `IsCrysAt` at the specified bridge, and that for **every** finite field $L'\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$, every $\mathbb Q_\ell$-embedding $\tau':K\to L'$, and every $\tau:E\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ with `IsInduced` for $\tau,\tau'$, the restriction $\rho|_D$ satisfies `IsJumpsAt` at $\tau$ with multiset $J(\tau)$. Here $\tau$ is the composition of $j_E,\tau'$ and the inclusion into $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$. This quantifies over all induced embedding data, not one selected place or one chosen coefficient model. The source's `ClosedTr` **also** assumes `IsSstAt` for every seed restriction; the power-sum statement does not assume this separately.

For $\rho$ (either a seed or the target) let $W(\rho)=\operatorname{wdAt}(\rho|_D)$ be the complex parameter at the same bridge and inducing data. For a fixed Weil element $g$ of **arbitrary signed degree**, and a fixed $\theta\in\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$, put
$$
p_g(\rho)=\iota^{-1}\!\left(-\operatorname{nextCoeff}(\operatorname{charPol}(W(\rho)(g)))\right).
$$
This is the pullback of the trace. Suppose that for every real $\epsilon>0$ there exist a finite set $I\subseteq C$ and coefficients $c_\rho\in\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ for which
$$
\left|\operatorname{tr}r(x)-\sum_{\rho\in I}c_\rho\operatorname{tr}\rho(x)\right|_\ell\le\epsilon
\quad\text{for every }x\in G_E,\qquad
\left|\theta-\sum_{\rho\in I}c_\rho p_g(\rho)\right|_\ell\le\epsilon .
$$
The uniform global approximation and the local approximation use the **same** $I,c_\rho$ at that precision. Then $p_g(r)=\theta$; this is `ClosedTr` with the semistable and crystalline seed hypotheses above. The target is not assumed crystalline.

For the separate statement `GlobalTheory.CongPhi.ClosedPhi`, fix **any** Weil element $\sigma$ and a sequence $(S_j)_{j\ge0}$ in $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$. Write $s_j(\rho)$ for the sum of the $j$th powers of the roots of $\operatorname{charPol}(W(\rho)(\sigma))$, counted with multiplicities and pulled back through $\iota$. Assume that for every integer $N\ge0$ and every real $\epsilon>0$ there exist $I\subseteq C$ finite and coefficients $c_\rho$ that satisfy the uniform global inequality above and, **simultaneously** for $0\le j\le N$,
$$
\left|S_j-\sum_{\rho\in I}c_\rho s_j(\rho)\right|_\ell\le\epsilon .
$$
Then $s_j(r)=S_j$ for all $j\ge0$ (in Lean the equality is in $\mathbb C$, with right side $\iota(S_j)$). The family and coefficient choices may depend on $(N,\epsilon)$; the target, the Weil element and the sequence may not. The proof derives semistability of the crystalline seed restrictions via `CrysSst.isSstAt_of_isCrysAt` before applying `ClosedTr`. Newton identities and recovery of a full characteristic polynomial from the first $a$ positive power sums are **not** part of this closure statement.

## The same data on the global group and the local group

### The target crystal is established on a separate branch

The trace proof first applies `LimitTop.closedAtL` to `JointApprox.lin`, which forgets the local scalar approximation. With continuous semisimple target and continuous semistable seeds of prescribed jumps it gives a de Rham target with those jumps, and separately an implication from crystallinity of all the seeds to crystallinity of the target. The trace proof uses only that last conclusion. The lattice target frame later supplied by `FrEx` is not the justification used here for `IsCrysAt` of $r|_D$.

Here is the precise boundary of this step:

1. The caller constructs a **plain-trace** cover using `TraceConjugationCover.cover_exists` and pulls it back along $\jmath:G_K\to D\hookrightarrow G_E$. It is not the joint cover constructed later to remember $p_g$. The model transport in lines 38–71 checks the matrix action and semistability, multiplicities, and conditional crystallinity of the seeds after pullback.
2. Given any finite coefficient field $L_*$, the caller separately uses compactness of the global representation's source to find a finite field $L$ containing $L_*L_0$ and all entries of $r$, and then constructs an $L$-valued model. This matters for jumps: for given $\tau':K\to L_*$ the caller enlarges to that $L$, composes $\tau'$ with the inclusion, and compares the degree counts against the original $\tau:E\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$. For existence of a de Rham or crystalline model it may instead take $L_*=L_0$.
3. On each such model `CovClose.closed_of_cover` supplies de Rhamness, degree counts and the crystalline implication. The [fixed-embedding Hodge-jump draft](how-linked-frames-recover-the-hodge-jumps-at-one-embedding.md) expands how that theorem reads the de Rham and crystalline conditions from the lattice and identifies jump multiplicities. The present note does not redo that argument, the plain-trace cover construction, the compact finite-field theorem, or the lower period comparisons furnishing the frame and adapted bases.

### The joint cover also remembers the local scalar

Apply `CoverJ` on the compact group $G_E$ to the **joint** approximation. The cover is indexed by integers $m\ge0$. Its fixed target piece is a continuous integral representation $\lambda$ on $\mathbb Z_\ell^d$ and matrices $(b_i)$ with a unit vector $e_0$ such that left multiplication by $r(x)$ acts on these matrices through $\lambda(x)$ and $\sum_i(e_0)_i b_i=I_a$. Each layer has continuous integral representation $\lambda_m$, reading matrices $b'_{jk}$ for its selected seeds $\rho_{mk}\in C$, and one unit vector $e_m$ reading the identity in all of them. The layer also has an integral map $\Pi_m$ to the target, equivariant and split surjective modulo $\ell^m$, and equivariant transitions from the next layer compatible with $\Pi_m$ modulo $\ell^m$. Its coefficients $c_{mk}$ satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Pi_m e_m-e_0&\equiv0\pmod{\ell^m},\\
\left|\sum_i(\Pi_m)_{ij}\operatorname{tr}b_i-\sum_k c_{mk}\operatorname{tr}b'_{jk}\right|_\ell&\le\ell^{-m},\\
\left|\theta-\sum_k c_{mk}p_g(\rho_{mk})\right|_\ell&\le\ell^{-m}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These are the two coordinate controls on a `Cover` and the extra control from `CoverJ`. In particular, there is no claim that any individual sequence of seed matrices converges to $r$. The [joint approximation and integral lattice tower draft](joint-trace-approximation-produces-an-integral-lattice-tower.md) explains the `CoverJ` construction. It samples the joint approximation at sufficient precision for the trace loss, keeps the supports nested by cumulative union, and gives older seeds coefficient zero when they are not used in a layer. Hence the third bound uses exactly the coefficients that governed the global trace, not a new fit to the local scalar.

Pull the cover back along $\jmath:G_K\xrightarrow{e^{-1}}D\hookrightarrow G_E$. Its reading matrices, coefficients and transition maps do not change, so the global coordinate bounds still hold. Use $J_K(\tau')=J(\tau'\circ j_E)$ for embeddings $\tau'$ of $K$. To verify the period hypotheses of `FrEx`, take *any* finite coefficient field $L\supseteq L_0$ and any $G_K$ matrix model of one pulled-back seed, with its indicated matrix action on $L^a$. Compose that model with $e$. Evaluating the entrywise model equality at $e(h)$ for $h\in D$ proves it is a finite model on $D$. Bringing it back along $e^{-1}$ gives exactly the original linear action on $L^a$. Thus the seed semistability and crystallinity pass to this model. For any embedding $K\to L$, the induced embedding of $E$ is its composition with $j_E$. The seed jump predicate therefore gives multiplicity $\#\{j\in J_K(\tau'):j=n\}$ for every filtration index $n\in\mathbb Z$, which is the condition on this finite model. This verification of the universally quantified finite models is the local step in `closedTr_of`, not a change of the original cover.

## Fixed coefficients and two nonnegative Frobenius powers

Choose the local Breuil–Kisin datum and its compatible period element. The frame theorem `FrEx`, applied to the whole pulled-back cover, gives one pole bound and crystalline frames for its target and every layer, together with a fixed denominator exponent $r_0$ for the links. Precisely, for every $n'$ some $m\ge n'$ admits rectangular linker matrices $R,G$ over the Breuil–Kisin power-series ring such that, with $U_0,U_m$ the frame matrices denoted `T` in the source, the evaluated product $\Pi_mU_m$ agrees with $U_0 R$ modulo $\ell^{n'}$, and $RG\equiv\ell^{r_0}I$ modulo $\ell^{n'}$. The first congruence uses the evaluation from the power-series ring to the Witt-vector realization and the coefficient map on $\Pi_m$; the second is over the power-series ring. The frame condition is that the matrix of the induced Galois action is congruent to the identity modulo the evaluated formal variable in the integral period ring. The [uniform linked crystalline frames draft](closed-phi-frex-linked-crystalline-frames.md) traces `FrEx` through semistable layers, one Hodge interval, and the crystalline link condition. Here it is applied only to the actual pulled-back cover and with the universal finite-model inputs checked above.

### Signed Weil degree and the order of the two powers

Write $\alpha(g)=\operatorname{resDeg}_{\ell}(w)\deg(g)$, using the source's degree and residue-degree factor. Set
$$
n_1=\max(-\alpha(g),0),\qquad n_2=\max(\alpha(g),0),\qquad \alpha(g)=n_2-n_1.
$$
For a coefficient $c$ in the intersection of $K$ with the maximal unramified subfield of $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$, `FixOf` says $\varphi^{|\alpha(g)|}(c)=c$. Indeed, the residue action is represented by a difference of two nonnegative Frobenius powers; after multiplying by the residue-degree factor let their exponents be $x,y$ with $\alpha(g)=x-y$. Choose $c'$ with $\varphi^y(c')=c$. The bridge's compatibility with the Weil element says the associated local Galois element carries $\varphi^y(c')$ to $\varphi^x(c')$. It fixes $c$ because $c\in K$. Thus $\varphi^y(c')=\varphi^x(c')$, and applying $\varphi^{|x-y|}$ to the expression with the smaller exponent proves the assertion. Both $n_1,n_2$ therefore satisfy the coefficient-fixing condition: each is either zero or $|\alpha(g)|$. These are hypotheses on the unramified coefficients, not an assertion that $\varphi$ fixes $K$ pointwise.

The relevant Frobenius in the **Weil action** occurs with the opposite sign $-\alpha(g)$. The source gives the representative identity on vectors coming from a semistable invariant $m$:
$$
\operatorname{weilD}(g)(\varphi^{n_2}m)=\varphi^{n_1}m.
$$
It uses the group-action factor on the potentially semistable module: this factor fixes $m$ and commutes with Frobenius. On that module the exponent sum is $-\alpha(g)+n_2=n_1$. For degree giving $\alpha(g)>0$, the identity reads $\operatorname{weilD}(g)(\varphi^{\alpha(g)}m)=m$; for $\alpha(g)<0$, it reads $\operatorname{weilD}(g)m=\varphi^{-\alpha(g)}m$. If $\alpha(g)=0$, both exponents are zero. This is why the integral matrix argument never calls for a negative iterate. It does not claim that the group-action factor is trivial on the whole potentially semistable space.

The similarly named `UpperTheory.Gal.ClosedPhiFromClosedTrace.ClosedPhi` in the single-trace helper file **does** assume $\deg(g)\le0$. It is not the `ClosedTr` proved by `closedTr_of`, nor the `GlobalTheory.CongPhi.ClosedPhi` power-sum property proved by `congPhi_holds`. Neither of those two conclusions has a sign restriction. [`FixUr`](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ClosedPhiFunctoriality/JointApproxOfMem.lean#L83) · [The degree calculation and fixed coefficients](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ClosedPhiFunctoriality/FixOfHolds.lean#L8-L26) · [The source picks the two exponents](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ClosedPhiFunctoriality/ClosedPhiOfClosedTr.lean#L54-L65) · [The opposite sign in the Weil action](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ClosedPhiFunctoriality/WeilPhi.lean#L8-L51) · [The distinct degree-restricted helper](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ClosedPhiFunctoriality/JointApproxOfMem.lean#L28-L45).

### The integral operator comparison has its own precision

Apply `OpEx2`. Fix the frames and their common pole bound, the link exponent $r_0$, and the chosen powers $n_1,n_2$. It gives one denominator $s_{\rm int}\in A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ whose image $s$ is a unit in $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$, a target integral matrix $V$, and integral matrices $V_m$ for **all** layers, chosen before any requested accuracy. Each then has an invariant period basis $U$ with
$$
V\,\varphi^{n_2}(U)=s\,\varphi^{n_1}(U)
$$
in $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$, and the analogous relation holds for $V_m$. All matrices and coefficients occurring in that equality are mapped to $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$. For every $M\ge0$ there is a layer $m\ge M$ for which
$$
V\,z_C(\Pi_m)-z_C(\Pi_m)V_m\equiv0\pmod{\ell^M}
$$
entrywise in $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, with $z_C:\mathbb Z_\ell\to A_{\mathrm{cris}}$. For such $M$ the construction first obtains a frame-link threshold $n(M)$ from `LinkZ` and then requests a frame link at precision $\max(n(M),M)$; this gives a single $m$ late enough for both this comparison and the cover's errors. The first congruence of the link is used in the operator comparison, after restricting it to the precision $n(M)$. This need not hold at every $m$ or follow directly from the raw trace approximation.

## Passing the period identities to the limit

Apply `Tie2` to the target and to every seed in each layer. In the target, use its now-proved crystalline restriction, its reading matrices and the two-power operator for $\lambda$; in the layer, use the crystallinity of the *particular* seed $\rho_{mk}$, the reading of that seed by $\lambda_m$, and the same scaled layer operator. This produces the functional trace identities of [the period-basis note](https://github.com/lyz4tc/rh-proof-notes/blob/main/proofs/en/period-basis-matrices-recover-the-weil-trace.md) with trace values $p_g(r)$ and $p_g(\rho_{mk})$, respectively. For example the target identity is
$$
\sum_i(Ve_0)_i\,u\bigl(\Lambda(\operatorname{tr} b_i)\bigr)
=s\,u\bigl(\Lambda(p_g(r))\bigr)
$$
in $B_{\rm cris}$ for every $F_{\rm ur}$-linear functional $\Lambda:\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\to F_{\rm ur}$, with $F_{\rm ur}$ the maximal unramified subfield of the coefficient algebraic closure and $u$ its period-ring embedding. In the displayed identity $V$ and $e_0$ are evaluated in $B_{\rm cris}$ and $s$ is the image of $s_{\rm int}$. This identity is not an application of continuity of crystalline Frobenius as a function of a global trace.

Given $M$, take the layer from the operator congruence. Because $m\ge M$, all three cover errors at this same layer have precision at least $M$. The [trace-error lemma](https://github.com/lyz4tc/rh-proof-notes/blob/main/proofs/en/arbitrarily-small-crystalline-trace-errors-vanish.md), which balances finite coefficients through the functionals, implies $p_g(r)=\theta$: any nonzero error would force one fixed nonzero multiple of $s_{\rm int}$ to be divisible by every power of $\ell$ in $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$. Thus the local fields, the operators, the reading matrices, and the approximation coefficients are used in *one* argument, with a common denominator and arbitrarily precise jointly chosen layers.

### Why the finite-model trace identity applies to the cover matrices

The period-basis proof is stated for matrices over a fixed finite coefficient field. In this application the entries of the reading matrices may initially be anywhere in $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$. To use that proof, start with a finite crystalline model of the local representation and adjoin all entries of the finitely many $b_i$ to its coefficient field. This is a finite extension. The model stays crystalline: the project compares the bridge predicate with the crystalline condition in any finite model above the bridge's coefficient field. It is therefore also potentially semistable. By injectivity of the coefficient-field inclusion, both the equivariance of the reading matrices (checked column by column on $L^a$) and $\sum_i(e_0)_i b_i=I$ remain identities over that larger field. The same reasoning applies separately to each seed's reading matrices.

The characteristic polynomial of the raw Weil operator in that finite model is the characteristic polynomial of the chosen complex parameter: the model comparison identifies its Frobenius semisimplification with the parameter, and removing a commuting nilpotent part does not change a characteristic polynomial. Its polynomial degree is $a$, which gives the dimension hypothesis of the period-basis trace identity. Its negative next-to-leading coefficient is the trace of the raw operator. The inclusion back to $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ takes the traces of the finite reading matrices to their original traces, so the functional identity obtained in the finite model is literally the one displayed above. This is [the model step in `tie_holds`](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ClosedPhiFunctoriality/TraceEqNextCoeff.lean#L15-L108). No common finite field for every seed in the infinite cover was needed.


## Recovering the power sums

Let $n\ge0$. The Weil representation acts by powers: $W(\rho)(\sigma^n)=W(\rho)(\sigma)^n$. Over $\mathbb C$ triangularize the latter operator before taking powers. Its diagonal entries, counted with multiplicity, become the $n$th powers of the original entries, so
$$
\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{roots}(\operatorname{charpol} W(\rho)(\sigma))}\lambda^n
=\operatorname{tr} W(\rho)(\sigma^n)
=-\operatorname{nextCoeff}(\operatorname{charpol} W(\rho)(\sigma^n)).
$$
The monic characteristic polynomial gives the last sign; $n=0$ gives the dimension. This is `PowTr`, following `eig_pow_of_rep` and the coefficient identity; triangularization is an equivalent linear-algebra presentation.

Use the joint approximation for $N=n$ and retain its $j=n$ inequality. The preceding identity turns that inequality into joint approximation for the single trace at $g=\sigma^n$, with $\theta=S_n$. The common jump and crystalline hypotheses are unchanged; `CrysSst.isSstAt_of_isCrysAt` supplies the extra semistable hypothesis of `ClosedTr`. Closedness gives $p_{\sigma^n}(r)=S_n$, and converting once more through $\iota$ proves the claimed $n$th power-sum equality. The approximation choices can depend on $n$ and $\epsilon$; $r$ and $\sigma$ stay fixed. Moreover, this proof only uses the $j=n$ component at each cutoff $N=n$; the simultaneous prefix approximation is the source statement's stated hypothesis.
