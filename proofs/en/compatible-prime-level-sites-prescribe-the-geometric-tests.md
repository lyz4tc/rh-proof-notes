# Compatible prime-level sites prescribe the geometric tests

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Take the data of the [odd-rank seed](conjugate-reference-places-give-one-odd-rank-geometric-seed.md) after aligning $w_0,v'$ to the same prime ideal on the imaginary quadratic subfield $K=\mathbb Q(x)$. Let $H,C$ be disjoint finite sets of places of $E$. Every place in their union is required to be usable, apart from $w_0$, and spherical for the fixed $U$. Require that each place in the union above $\ell$ has the same contraction to $K$ as $w_0$. More generally require that any two places in the union above the same rational prime have the same contraction to $K$. Then the construction produces a single geometric datum as in the [twist argument](an-algebraic-twist-turns-a-hecke-tower-into-a-geometric-seed.md). It passes the Hodge test at every place in $H$ and the Frobenius test for every spherical Satake multiset at every place in $C$. It also passes the Frobenius tests at usable spherical places away from $\ell$ outside a finite set. ## Keeping consistent local orientations

At each rational prime used by the construction, $-d$ is a square in the rational local field, so the two choices of prime of $K$ are distinct. An eligible place $u$ is allowed as a support place of the chosen automorphic vector if its conjugate has no contraction in common with an element of $H\cup C$. At a split pair, at least one of the places or its conjugate is allowed. If both were forbidden, two target places over the same rational prime would have opposite contractions to $K$. The hypotheses rule this out. The distinguished place $w_0$ is allowed because each target over $\ell$ contracts to its own prime of $K$, which differs from its conjugate prime. This is the combination of conditions called `Up` in the choice of an automorphic vector. [Orientation choice](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/GeometricFlagBases/PrimeLevelLocalization/SameKTrans.lean#L103)

Take the automorphic vector of the prescribed elliptic type at $w_0$ and of the required shape, with compact open level that is maximal at the split spherical targets and whose other support places use the allowed sides. The construction also provides the algebraic central character $\kappa$ and nonnegative geometric weights after a weight shift, by passing to the similitude group. The restrictions on support and level make each chosen target free for geometric level selection. This means neither it nor its conjugate is in the vector's exceptional support, no conjugate of a support place has the target's contraction, and the target has different contraction from its own conjugate. [Vector and freeness conditions](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/GeometricFlagBases/PrimeLevelLocalization/PrimeProj.lean#L119) · [Deducing freeness at each target](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/GeometricFlagBases/PrimeLevelLocalization/SameKTrans.lean#L116)

## Constructing the common spectral system

Choose a rational prime $p$ outside the target primes and the finite obstruction from the vector and level. Over a suitable Harris–Taylor unitary datum of rank $a$ at $p$, the frame identifies the away-$p$ adelic action with the specified local coordinates and integral lattices. A separate auxiliary prime $r_0$ is chosen away from all targets and $p$. The spectral construction gives a nonzero Hecke function once the level $N$ is divisible by $r_0$ and its principal congruence group fixes the vector with trivial type character. The simultaneous local level selection then also makes $N$ divisible by each target rational prime. It supplies a local congruence group $K_w$ for each target, contained in the same kernel of the type and stabilizer. At places over the same rational prime the two groups use the same distinguished root in the imaginary quadratic subfield and the same level ideal. These are the compatibility relations in `SiteEx`. [Choosing a compatible level](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/GeometricFlagBases/PrimeLevelLocalization/PrimeProj.lean#L220) · [Spectral construction](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ScalarCommutation/SpecTopG.lean#L8)

The Hecke system includes every required deck correspondence and the Hecke correspondences for the Frobenius tests. A deck correspondence is attached to an appropriate level-group element and has eigenvalue $1$. For a non-Hodge place $w$, its $i$-th Hecke correspondence uses a diagonal matrix with $i$ entries equal to a uniformizer, multiplied by a central correction. The correction makes the adelic element integral in the allowed sense and puts its central character into the Hecke eigenvalue. This eigenvalue is the Hecke index times
$$
\iota^{-1}\!\left(\operatorname{SatEig}_i(B_w)\,\kappa(\varpi_w)^{-i}\right).
$$
Here $\operatorname{SatEig}_i$ is the eigenvalue of the $i$-th spherical Hecke operator in the project normalization. Use all such Hecke data at sites compatible with every site in $H$, omitting a Frobenius-test operator at any place of $H$. The spectral construction supplies the common Hecke function with this entire system of eigenvalues. [Deck and Hecke operators](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/GeometricFlagBases/PrimeLevelLocalization/PrimeProj.lean#L112) · [Specifying the whole system in Lean](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/GeometricFlagBases/PrimeLevelLocalization/SameKTrans.lean#L137)

At $w\in C$, the selected deck operators and every needed $i$-th Hecke operator are present, since $w\notin H$ and the chosen sites are compatible. The local congruence theorem identifies the Hecke polynomial, and the elementary symmetric coefficient formula gives its root multiset $q_w^{(a-1)/2}B_w\cdot\kappa(\varpi_w)^{-1}$. This proves the Frobenius test. At $v\in H$, the deck operators are also present, and every other operator comes from a different place with compatible local level data. The good-cover theorem uses exactly these conditions to supply the geometry at $v$ needed for the Hodge comparison. The exponent computation puts each desired shifted jump into the geometric list. Finally, outside the rational primes dividing the level, the geometric bad-prime integer, or lying below the vector's exceptional set, and outside $p$, the ordinary principal local level is a compatible site for every usable spherical place. It differs in rational prime from each $H$-site. The same construction gives the Frobenius test at all these places. ## Moving from the plan to a Hecke tower

Choose the $k$-fold fiber power of the universal abelian scheme with $k$ given by the Hodge trace calculation. The project uses a standard generic-fiber model of the chosen unitary datum, with the same field $E$ after the prescribed identification. The congruence rule transports the group-level identities at each place to the corresponding geometric Hecke tower. The good-cover rule supplies the covers at the places in $H$, and the Hodge calculation realizes each shifted required exponent in the common eigenspace, after the allowed simultaneous conjugation of the embedding and eigenvalues. This gives the required geometric datum. [Assembling a tower from the plan](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/GeometricFlagBases/PackExOf.lean#L101)

For the four applications in the odd-rank seed, choose respectively $(H,C)=(\{v'\},\varnothing)$, $(\{v'\},\{w\})$ for $w\nmid\ell$, $(\{v',w\},\varnothing)$ for the jump comparison at $w\mid\ell$, and $(\{v'\},\{w\})$ for its crystalline Frobenius comparison. The aligned-reference hypotheses give the same contraction wherever two requested places lie over $\ell$, and a place away from $\ell$ is automatically apart from $w_0$. These choices give `PackEx` and hence all geometric inputs needed by the seed.
