# The adjugate column detects a pair on the common kernel

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix the finite local specialization of the dyadic Selmer construction, with coefficient algebra $A$, residual prime $\ell$, exponent $\nu>0$, and two continuous twisted actions $T_1,T_2$ on $A^r$, the second the Tate dual of the first. Write $\bar g=\iota(g)$ for the automorphism of the absolute Galois group $G$ induced by CM conjugation. Let $L_0$ be the common small open normal subgroup intersected with the kernels of all active ray coordinates, that is, all coordinates except the unused first-block ones. The group $L_0$ is stable under $\iota$ and acts trivially on both modules. Let $z$ be the fixed tame inertia element that acts by scalars $d$ and $d^{-1}$, with $d-1$ and $d^{-1}-1$ units in $A$. Assume the `Setup` data and the small-subgroup and tame conditions in `exists_bform_ne_zero`. They give the following coordinate-product implication, the content of `two_mul_values_eq_zero` in this setting:
$$
\begin{gathered}
x(g)\cdot y(g)-x(\bar g)\cdot y(\bar g)=0\quad(g\in L_0)\\
\Longrightarrow\quad
\ell^\nu x(g)_a\,y(g')_b=0
\quad(g,g'\in L_0,\ 1\le a,b\le r)
\end{gathered}
$$
for any continuous cocycles $x,y$ for $T_1,T_2$. Here the dot denotes the coordinate dot product. The coefficient ring kills $(\ell^\nu)\mathfrak m_O$; the twist is anticyclotomic and trivial on $L_0$ and on $z$. The implication uses comparison of two matrix spans and the cyclotomic determinant, and is the separate input to the deduction below.

Suppose the square presentation $\Psi$ with universal cocycle matrix $Z(g)$ computes the specialized ordinary Selmer condition on every coefficient module. Let $v\in\ker\Psi$ and fix a coordinate $i$ and an element $b\in A$ with $v_i\mid b$. Let $y$ be a dual cocycle whose cohomology class survives multiplication by $\ell^\nu b$. With $x(g)=Z(g)v$, there exists $h\in L_0$ with
$$
x(h)\cdot y(h)-x(\bar h)\cdot y(\bar h)\ne0 .
$$
In the new-prime step, if the selected entry is $C_{ij}$, take $v$ to be column $j$ and use its coordinate $i$, with $b=t^{(e+1)w}$.

For either action, a cocycle $u$ vanishing on $L_0$ is a coboundary. The small-subgroup condition puts $[z,g]$ in $L_0$ for every $g\in G$, so $z$ is central in $G/L_0$. Thus $u(zg)=u(gz)$. If the scalar of $z$ is $a_z$, the cocycle rule gives
$$
(a_z-1)u(g)=(T(g)-1)u(z),
$$
where $T$ is the relevant action. The unit $a_z-1$ lets one divide to obtain a coboundary formula. This applies also over a quotient ring of $A$.

Assume the signed evaluation vanishes throughout $L_0$, and let $J\subseteq A$ be generated by all coordinates of $x(g)$ for $g\in L_0$. The ordinary universal cocycle for the reduction of $v$ over $A/J$ restricts to zero on $L_0$. It is therefore a coboundary. The computation of the Selmer functor by $\Psi$ identifies the zero class with the zero kernel vector, so every coordinate of $v$ lies in $J$. In particular $b\in J$ by divisibility. The coordinate-product implication says that $\ell^\nu a\,y(g')_k=0$ for every generator $a$ of $J$, all $g'\in L_0$, and every $k$, and linearity gives the same for every $a\in J$. Hence the cocycle $\ell^\nu b y$ restricts to zero on $L_0$. It too is a coboundary by the scalar argument, contrary to the choice of $y$. This proves the claimed nonzero signed evaluation.
