# The local norm orbit compares representation and stabilizer densities

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix a finite place $\mathfrak p$ of $F$ and use the rank-$m$ Hermitian space and norm $a=h(x_0,x_0)\ne0$ of [the local mass factor](a-unit-vector-pins-the-local-representation-mass-factor.md). Work over the completed quadratic algebra at $\mathfrak p$, with $q=\mathrm N\mathfrak p$. Let $L,L'$ be full integral lattices in the same space, and $\psi(L),\psi(L')$ their sums over complementary lattice classes defined in [the representation mass product](orthogonal-complements-factor-the-weighted-representation-count.md). Define $R(L)=q^{(m-1)\Delta(L)}\alpha(a;L)/\alpha(L)$ as in the local mass factor. Then
$$
 \psi(L)R(L')=\psi(L')R(L).
\tag{1}
$$
The Lean proof compares normalized finite tubes near exact vectors of norm $a$, whose common factor is the same for both lattices. The argument below obtains the proportionality by the corresponding measures on the local orbit. ## Measure the norm fibre on an integral lattice

Let $G$ be the unitary group of the full space and $H$ the point stabilizer of $x_0$, isomorphic to the unitary group of its orthogonal complement. The set $X_a=\{x:h(x,x)=a\}$ is one orbit of $G$. Over the quadratic field this uses the rank-one isometries of [the equal-norm vector proof](two-reflections-align-the-two-sides-of-a-hyperbolic-plane.md). Its proof also works over a split quadratic algebra: if its scalar $\beta$ is a zero divisor, one may instead match the two vectors in the first factor by a general linear map carrying the given vector and the kernel of its dual vector to the corresponding pair in the target. The pairing with the dual vector is the common nonzero norm, so the two choices are compatible, and the inverse transpose carries the dual in the other factor. This identifies the orbit with $G/H$. The identification is a homeomorphism because the orbit map is a submersion. At $x_0$, its differential from the unitary Lie algebra has kernel the Lie algebra on the complement, of dimension $(m-1)^2$. Its image thus has dimension $2m-1$, which is the dimension of the norm fibre by surjectivity of the norm differential. The inverse function argument then gives an open orbit map.

Choose additive Haar measure $dx$ on the ambient space. The function $Q(x)=h(x,x)$ has nonzero differential along $X_a$. For instance the direction $x$ has derivative $2a\ne0$. Using the local coordinate argument of [the isometry density proof](normalized-isometry-counts-measure-the-local-stabilizer.md#a-measure-on-the-exact-equations) gives a measure $\mu_a$ on $X_a$ computed on $X_a\cap L$ by the limit of $q^k$ times the $dx$-volume of vectors in $L$ with $Q(x)\equiv a\bmod\varpi^k$. As the norm is constant modulo $\varpi^k$ on each vector class, this is
$$
 \mu_a(X_a\cap L)=dx(L)\,\alpha(a;L).
\tag{2}
$$
The same construction gives measure zero when $X_a\cap L$ is empty, because there are no approximate solutions in $L$ at sufficiently high levels by compactness. The ambient unitary action preserves $dx$ and $Q$, so it preserves $\mu_a$. The change of basis from a fixed basis to a lattice basis of $L$ scales $dx$ by $|\mathrm N(\det S)|$. The Gram determinant in the lattice basis is $\det H_{\mathrm{fixed}}\,\mathrm N(\det S)$. Thus $dx(L)$ is a fixed positive constant times $q^{-\Delta(L)}$.

Choose Haar measures $dg,dh$ on $G,H$ and the invariant quotient measure on $G/H$ normalized by integrating first by $dh$ on the right $H$-coset and then on the quotient. Both groups are unimodular, as in the unitary volume argument for the isometry densities. This quotient measure is well-defined and unique up to scale. In local analytic sections of the quotient map it is obtained from the fibre product of the two measures, and independence of the section follows from left Haar invariance in $H$. Alternatively any invariant measure on the quotient lifts by the fibre integral to a left Haar measure on $G$, proving uniqueness. Hence there is a positive scalar, independent of $L$, relating $\mu_a$ to this normalized quotient measure. ## Decompose by stabilizers of the complementary lattices

For $K_L\subset G$ the lattice stabilizer, every $K_L$-orbit in $X_a$ is open because $K_L$ is open and the map $G\to X_a$ is open. The set $X_a\cap L$ is compact, so it is a finite union of such orbits. An orbit represented by $g x_0\in L$ has normalized quotient measure
$$
 \frac{dg(K_L)}{dh(H\cap g^{-1}K_Lg)}.
$$
To see this, apply quotient integration to the indicator of $K_Lg$ in $G$. This set has Haar measure $dg(K_L)$ by unimodularity. On the quotient its inner fibre integral is $dh(H\cap g^{-1}K_Lg)$ on the orbit and zero off it.

Send this orbit to the $H$-orbit of $M=g^{-1}L$. These are exactly the lattices isometric to $L$ and containing $x_0$, up to isometries fixing $x_0$. The stabilizer of $M$ in $H$ is $H\cap g^{-1}K_L g$. To group the lattices by $T=M\cap x_0^\perp$, fix one representative $T$ of each complementary class. The finite fibre over $T$ carries an action of $K_T\subset H$. The measure of an orbit contribution after dividing by $dg(K_L)$ is $1/dh(\operatorname{Stab}_{K_T}M)$. This equals the number of lattices in that $K_T$-orbit divided by $dh(K_T)$, since the stabilizer has finite index. Summing all the orbits over $T$ gives $b_{\mathfrak p}(T)/dh(K_T)$. Summing all the complements gives the normalized quotient volume divided by $dg(K_L)$. [Restriction of the point stabilizer to the complement](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/GL3MeanSquares/GenusMeanAsymptotics/UnimodularClassRepresentatives/ExtHomInjective.lean#L90) · [Regrouping by complementary lattices](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/GL3MeanSquares/GenusMeanAsymptotics/UnimodularClassRepresentatives/NuGmapLocF.lean#L34)

By [the stabilizer density normalization](normalized-isometry-counts-measure-the-local-stabilizer.md), $dg(K_L)$ is a fixed multiple of $q^{-m\Delta(L)}\alpha(L)$. By (2) and the covolume factor, $\mu_a(X_a\cap L)/dg(K_L)$ is a fixed multiple of $R(L)$. For the complement $dh(K_T)$ is a fixed multiple of $q^{-(m-1)\Delta(T)}\alpha(T)$, so the sum of $b_{\mathfrak p}(T)/dh(K_T)$ is another fixed multiple of $\psi(L)$, using the definition of $\lambda_{\mathfrak p}(T)$. All constants are determined by the forms, the chosen norm vector, and the Haar normalizations. This gives a single proportionality between $R(L)$ and $\psi(L)$ for every $L$, including empty orbits. Applying it twice proves (1). Its constant is fixed by the norm-one comparison lattice in the linked local mass factor proof.
