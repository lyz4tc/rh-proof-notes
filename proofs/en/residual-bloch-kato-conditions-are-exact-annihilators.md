# Residual Bloch–Kato conditions are exact annihilators

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $F_v/\mathbb Q_\ell$ be a finite local field extension. Let $O$ be a coefficient valuation ring with fraction field $E$, uniformizer $\pi$, and finite residue field $k$. Let $T,T'$ be stable dual lattices with a perfect $O$-pairing into $O(1)$, so $T'=\operatorname{Hom}_O(T,O)(1)$ in dual coordinates. Write $V,V'$ for their rational representations. Assume $V$ is de Rham. Define $Z_f(T)$ and $Z_f(T')$ as integral cocycles which have rational Bloch–Kato finite classes. The finite local conditions on the two residual representations are the images in cohomology of reductions of these lattices of cocycles. Since the reduction $O\to k$ is onto, their images are already $k$-subspaces.

Use the integral Bloch–Kato local duality in the following form. A class in $H^1(F_v,V'/T')$ comes from $H^1_f(F_v,V')$ if and only if its mixed cup with every element of $Z_f(T)$ has zero class in $H^2(F_v,(E/O)(1))$. This is the integral duality input called `bkBCore` in the source.

**Proposition.** The reduced finite conditions are exact annihilators of one another under the residual cup pairing. After extension of the residual coefficient field, the forward orthogonality continues to hold.

**Proof.** Embed $T'/\pi T'$ in $V'/T'$ by $\bar y\mapsto y/\pi+T'$. This is equivariant and independent of integral lift. A residual cocycle $\bar y(g)$ thus becomes a torsion cocycle $c(g)$. Its class belongs to the image of rational finite cohomology exactly when $\bar y$ itself is the reduction of an integral finite cocycle. Indeed, if $c$ comes from a rational finite class, choose a rational finite cocycle $z$ representing it. The reduction of $z$ agrees with $c$ up to a coboundary in $V'/T'$. Lift the element giving that coboundary to $V'$ and modify $z$ by its coboundary. The new $z$ reduces to $c$ as a cocycle. Then $\pi z(g)$ is integral and has reduction $\bar y(g)$, while its rational class remains finite. In the other direction, divide any integral finite lift by $\pi$ to obtain a rational finite lift of $c$.

For $x\in Z_f(T)$, the mixed cup of $x$ and $c$ is the image of the residual cup of $\bar x,\bar y$ under $k(1)\hookrightarrow (E/O)(1)$, $\bar a\mapsto a/\pi+O$. This follows pointwise from the cup formula $(g,h)\mapsto \langle x(g),g y(h)\rangle$. The resulting map in degree two is injective. To see this, an image which is a coboundary can be witnessed in a finite subgroup of $E/O$: the witnessing cochain has finite image by compactness, and a common finite subgroup killed by a power of $\ell$ contains its values and the embedded $k$. For finite cyclotomic coefficient modules, local Tate duality identifies the dual of the map on $H^2$ with restriction on the invariant groups of the dual coefficient modules. The twists cancel there. Every additive character of the smaller finite subgroup into $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ extends to the larger one, so restriction is onto. Hence the map on $H^2$ is injective even after passing to $E/O$.

It follows that a residual class $[\bar y]$ annihilates the reduced finite condition for $T$ exactly when its torsion image pairs to zero with all of $Z_f(T)$. Integral duality and the lift criterion identify this with the reduced finite condition for $T'$. Local Tate duality is perfect on the residual pair after using a nonzero trace functional on $k$; testing all $k$-multiples under that trace is equivalent to requiring zero for the $k$-valued cup product. This gives exact annihilation on both sides. The forward implication persists after scalar extension, since each extended finite condition is spanned by scalar multiples of reduced integral cocycles and the cup product is bilinear.

**Lean correspondence.** The lattice lift criterion is `lift_iff`. The source uses `h2_inj` for injectivity via local Tate cohomology generators. `bkOn_orthogonal` proves forward orthogonality including coefficient extensions, and `mem_bkOn_of_orthogonal` obtains the finite integral lift in the reverse direction. The trace interpretation attaches the same local annihilator to the ordinary finite-module pairing. Source-based reconstruction.
