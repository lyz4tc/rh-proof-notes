# A Casimir identity and cutoffs preserve weighted L² under differentiation

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $G=U(p,q)$, with $K=U(p)\times U(q)$, and let $D_X f(g)=\frac{d}{dt}|_{0} f(g\exp(tX))$ for $X\in\mathfrak g$. Put $w(g)=\|g\|_{\mathrm F}^\beta$ for a fixed $\beta\ge0$. Suppose $W$ is a finite-dimensional space of smooth complex functions on $G$ that is stable under the compact derivatives and on which the full quadratic Casimir $\Omega$ is a fixed scalar. If $wf\in L^2(G)$ for every $f\in W$, then $wD_{X_1}\cdots D_{X_m} f\in L^2(G)$ for every $m\ge0$, all $X_j$ in the complexified Lie algebra and $f\in W$. In particular the same conclusion with $w=1$ makes the original functions smooth vectors of the right regular representation.

Take real trace-orthonormal bases of the compact and noncompact directions with signs negative and positive, respectively. Write $A_i$ and $B_j$ for the associated derivatives, and $\Delta=\sum_i A_i^2+\sum_j B_j^2$. The identity $\Delta=\Omega+2\sum_i A_i^2$ identifies $\Delta f$ on $W$ with another element of $W$, so $w\Delta f\in L^2$.

Write $\ell(g)=\log\|g\|_{\mathrm F}$. The estimate $\|g e^{tX}\|_{\mathrm F}\le e^{|t|\|X\|_{\rm op}}\|g\|_{\mathrm F}$, applied also with $e^{-tX}$, gives $|D_X\ell|\le\|X\|_{\rm op}$. Also $\ell$ has compact sublevel sets, since $G$ is the closed matrix locus $g^*Jg=J$, which implies invertibility. Choose a smooth cutoff $\chi$ equal to $1$ on $[0,1]$, supported in $(-1,2)$, and between $0$ and $1$. For $R\ge1$ put $u_R=w\chi(\ell/R)$. Then $u_R$ has compact support, $u_R(g)\to w(g)$, and $|D_X u_R|\le C_X w$ uniformly in $R$. Here $\ell(g)\ge0$, by $|\det g|=1$ and the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality on singular values.

Use right invariance of Haar measure to integrate $D$ by parts for each real basis derivative in the set $\mathcal D=\{A_i,B_j\}$. Differentiating $u_R^2\bar f Df$ gives
$$
 \sum_{D\in\mathcal D}\|u_R Df\|_2^2
 =-\operatorname{Re}\int_G u_R^2\bar f\,\Delta f
  -2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{D\in\mathcal D}\int_G u_R(Du_R)\bar f\,Df.
$$
The integration is valid because these products have compact support. The first term is bounded by $\|wf\|_2\|w\Delta f\|_2$. For the second, Cauchy–Schwarz followed by $2ab\le a^2/2+2b^2$ absorbs half of the left-hand sum and leaves a constant times $\|wf\|_2^2$. The resulting bound is independent of $R$. Fatou's lemma now gives $w Df\in L^2$ for each basis derivative.

For a nonnegative integer $m$, let $W_m$ be the finite span of all words of length at most $m$ applied to $W$. Compact derivatives preserve $W_m$ by commuting to the right, since the commutator with each generator is a linear combination of generators. The Casimir retains its scalar value on $W_m$ by centrality. Thus the argument applies to $W_m$ whenever all its elements are in weighted $L^2$. Induction proves the assertion for all words. To recognize the $L^2$ derivatives as derivatives of the regular action, integrate the pointwise derivative along $g\exp(tX)$; this agrees in $L^2$ with the integral of the translated $L^2$ derivative. The latter integrand is continuous in $L^2$ by strong continuity, so the derivative agrees in that norm. Iteration along products of one-parameter subgroups gives smoothness.

## Application to matrix coefficients

Let a unitary representation have a smooth finite-dimensional compact subspace $V$ with scalar Casimir, and suppose all its coefficients $g\mapsto\langle v',\sigma(g)v\rangle$ for $v,v'\in V$ are in $L^2$ after multiplication by $w$. For a fixed $v'\in V$, the functions obtained by varying $v\in V$ satisfy the hypotheses just proved. Differentiation of the second vector gives the same weighted integrability for any second vector in the Lie module generated by $V$. Inversion and complex conjugation exchange the two vectors and preserve the measure and the weight. In fact $g^{-1}=Jg^*J$ has the same Frobenius norm as $g$. Now keep any generated first vector fixed and apply the derivative argument in the second vector again. This proves weighted $L^2$ for two arbitrary generated vectors. The use in the [spectral projection construction](weighted-l2-coefficients-give-real-spectral-projections-in-the-tensor-module.md) is $\beta=1$, to absorb an ordinary finite-dimensional matrix factor in a coefficient.
