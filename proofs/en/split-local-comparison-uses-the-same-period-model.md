# Split local comparison uses the same period model

**Status: Source-based reconstruction draft.** Read against the pinned Lean source. No new Lean build or independent audit. This note expands the split transport step left implicit in [the existing Core-to-CoreAll draft](https://github.com/lyz4tc/rh-proof-notes/blob/main/proofs/en/twisting-and-split-base-change-give-the-spherical-comparison-for-every-polarized-form.md).

## Statement and scope

Fix a prime $\ell$, a coefficient-field identification $\iota:\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\simeq\mathbb C$, and a finite extension $F\subset K$ of number fields inside a common algebraic closure $\Omega$. Let a valuation ring $A$ of $\Omega$ lie above finite places $v$ of $F$ and $w$ of $K$. Write $D_F,D_K$ for the subgroups that preserve $A$ in $G_F,G_K$. The place identification in this note is a continuous completion isomorphism $F_v\simeq K_w$ compatible with the given inclusions; the algebraic closure and its valuation are not changed.

Use one project local period package $P$ at $\ell$ and a topological $\mathbb Z_\ell$-algebra $O$ for the two local bridges. A bridge identifies its decomposition group continuously with $G_M$ for a finite extension $M/\mathbb Q_\ell$ inside the coefficient algebraic closure; it is supplied with an embedding $j:\Omega\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ compatible with the action, and a finite coefficient field $L_0$ containing the required embeddings of $M$. Both bridges are assumed inducing in the sense that their period-side realization induces the given embedding. Assume the two bridges have the **same** $M,j,L_0$. Denote the group identifications by $e_F:D_F\simeq G_M$ and $e_K:D_K\simeq G_M$.

For any rank $a$ and $r:G_F\to\mathrm{GL}_a(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell)$, form the restrictions on $D_F$ and $D_K$ using the given inclusions. Write $W_F(r)$ and $W_K(r|_{G_K})$ for Lean's local parameters `wdAt`, which use Frobenius semisimplification of the potentially semistable realization if it exists, and the rank-$a$ trivial parameter otherwise. Then for every pair of corresponding Weil elements $g,c(g)$ the two parameters have the same characteristic polynomial. More strongly, they are related by `WDRel`: in suitable complex bases their monodromy matrices are equal and their Weil operators agree on corresponding elements. With the same bridges, the predicates de Rham, semistable, crystalline and potentially semistable descend from $K$ to $F$. Any jump multiset at an embedding $\tau':K\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ descends unchanged at $\tau'|_F$. No regularity, cusp form or attachment hypothesis is required for these local transport assertions.

## The same finite coefficient model

Let $h\in D_K$ and write $g=e_F^{-1}(e_K(h))$. Equivariance of the **same** embedding $j$ gives, for every $x\in\Omega$,
$$
j(gx)=e_F(g)j(x)=e_K(h)j(x)=j(hx).
$$
By injectivity, $g$ and $h$ act by the very same automorphism of $\Omega$, with the latter viewed over $F$. Thus if the matrices of the first restriction are realized over a finite $L\supseteq L_0$ by $r_L$, the second is realized over that same $L$ by $r_L\circ e_F^{-1}\circ e_K$. Both have identical matrices as representations of $G_M$; there is no scalar extension on the local field. The reverse construction uses $e_K^{-1}\circ e_F$. It proves the descent of all four period properties by reusing the corresponding invariant-space witness. For jumps it also reuses the same embedding of $M$ into the coefficient field: the compatibility square with $\tau'$ restricts to the square with $\tau'|_F$. The integer-indexed filtration multiplicities are unchanged because the linear representation in that square did not change.

The project definition of `wdAt` may select a *different* finite coefficient model. In the potentially semistable case this choice is immaterial: enlarge the selected model and the specified model to their finite compositum in $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$. The enlarged matrices agree entry by entry because both realize the same original matrices. Coefficient enlargement leaves the complex Weil–Deligne parameter unchanged. This is the actual argument in the private helper `wdAt_of_model`; it does not identify distinct coefficient models before enlargement.

## Transport of the Weil operator

In the potentially semistable case use the common finite model above. The completion identification gives a Weil-group isomorphism $c:W_v\to W_w$ with equal degrees and residue cardinalities. In Lean, this means that $c(g)$ is the unique element acting exactly like $g$ on $\Omega$ after restricting scalars. Hence the two transported elements of $G_M$ coincide. The signed Frobenius exponent $\alpha$ is the Weil degree times the project's residue-degree factor over $\ell$, so it is also unchanged. On the common potentially semistable module the two actions are therefore both the action of this same $G_M$ element composed with $\varphi^{-\alpha}$, with the same monodromy. This gives equality before Frobenius semisimplification.

To pass to the parameters used by Lean, put both Frobenius-semisimplified realizations on the same complex vector space. For each related pair of Weil elements their semisimple operators are the semisimple parts of the identical unsimplified endomorphism: the defining differences are nilpotent and commute with that endomorphism. Uniqueness of the additive Jordan decomposition proves equality of the semisimple operators. The monodromy was not changed. Choosing a basis now gives `WDRel`. In the other case, potential semistability fails on both sides by the model descent just proved; the definitions yield two rank-$a$ trivial parameters. Their operators are identities and their monodromy is zero, so they are again related. Equality of the characteristic polynomials is now just equality of the matrices in the chosen coordinates.

## Application to the spherical clause in CoreAll

Let $F$ be CM, $a\ge1$, and $\pi$ be a regular algebraic cuspidal representation of $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb A_F)$ polarized by a Hecke character of $F^+$. Let $r$ be any weakly attached representation, and fix any finite $v$ with a Satake multiset $B$. Choose a good quadratic CM base change $K/F$ split at $v$, supplying a good cuspidal lift $P_K$, a polarization, and a place $w$ split over $K^+$ above the selected valuation. These global supplies and weak attachment of the restricted representation are covered in [the Core-to-CoreAll draft](https://github.com/lyz4tc/rh-proof-notes/blob/main/proofs/en/twisting-and-split-base-change-give-the-spherical-comparison-for-every-polarized-form.md#removing-the-good-field-restrictions-by-quadratic-base-change). The extension is chosen so that the decomposition subgroup at $v$ lies inside $G_K$ and the given inducing bridge extends with identical $M,j,L_0$. The Satake rule for the lift at relative residue degree one gives the very same $B$ at $w$.

On either side the spherical parameter is the unramified diagonal parameter with entries $b^{-1}$, $b\in B$, followed by the real norm twist $(1-a)/2$. The unramified operator depends only on Weil degree, and the twist only on degree and residue cardinality. Hence the spherical parameters also match on $g,c(g)$, including multiplicity and the normalization. Apply the good-scope polynomial comparison to $(P_K,r|_{G_K},w,B,c(g))$. Transport its left-hand side by the finite model comparison and its right-hand side by the spherical parameter comparison. This proves the all-scope polynomial equality for the **original** $r$, rather than constructing a different representation at each $v$.

In the pinned implementation the crystallinity premise of `holdsS_all_phi` is not used (the binder is anonymous). The fallback is handled by definition, not by assuming crystallinity at all places where `.phi` is invoked. This does not remove the separate crystallinity clause of `CoreAll`. Weak attachment for the chosen $r$, and the patching argument that produces such an $r$ in the first place, remain separate from the transport above.
