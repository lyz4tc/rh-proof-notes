# A bounded elliptic series translates through the local multiplicative chart

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

## Statement

Let $q$ be prime and use the good Weierstrass model, complex uniformization, coefficient isomorphism $\iota$, multiplicative chart $\ell(X)=\omega\log(1+X)$, and notation $u,w$ of [the Weierstrass flow proof](the-weierstrass-flow-identifies-the-small-point-shift.md). Suppose $p$ is a small point with multiplicative coordinate $\zeta$. Thus $|\zeta-1|_q<1$, and $\widehat u(\zeta-1)=\iota(u(p))$. Let $\phi$ be meromorphic and periodic for the model lattice and analytic at $0$. Assume the transported and substituted series $\widehat\phi$ has bounded coefficient norms.

Then $\phi(z+p)$ has an analytic germ at $z=0$, after filling in a removable value if needed. If $\psi$ denotes that germ, then
$$
                 \widehat\psi(X)=\widehat\phi\bigl(\zeta(1+X)-1\bigr).
$$
In particular the conclusion justifies shifting the CM norm quotient in [the proof of the unshifted Weyl law](two-cm-multipliers-determine-the-unshifted-theta-translation.md). Neither a coefficient bound for the translated complex germ nor a separate exclusion of a pole at $p$ is required. [Bounded elliptic translation statement](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/JetHarmonics/CpTransportedTaylorSeriesShiftEqTranslateStatement.lean#L7)

## Proof

Write $\mathcal S_\zeta f=f(\zeta(1+X)-1)$ for shift on bounded series. The shifted argument lies in the open unit disc whenever $|X|_q<1$. Hence this is analytic substitution, respecting addition and multiplication. It maps bounded series to bounded series: the binomial coefficient formula for coefficient $n$ is
$$
  \sum_{m\ge n} f_m\binom{m}{n}\zeta^n(\zeta-1)^{m-n}.
$$
Its summands tend to zero, and each has norm at most $\sup_m|f_m|_q$. The reversed shift $\mathcal S_{1/\zeta}$ provides an inverse. As a consequence it preserves the property of being a nonzero bounded series.

The analytic meromorphic function field of the elliptic torus is generated by its affine coordinates $x,y$, as used and proved in [the elliptic quotient argument](a-cm-theta-norm-quotient-is-an-integral-unit.md#the-quotients-and-their-coefficient-bounds). In particular, choose a nonzero polynomial $D$ with
$$
       \phi(z)D(x(z))=A(x(z))+B(x(z))y(z)
$$
as a meromorphic equality, where $A,B$ are polynomials. At generic $z$, $x(z)=u(z)/w(z)$ and $y(z)=-1/w(z)$. After multiplying the equality by a sufficiently high power of $w$, obtain $\phi H=J$ where $H,J$ are polynomials in the pair $u,w$. For instance choose $b\ge\max(\deg D,\deg A,\deg B+1)$, take $H=w^bD(u/w)$ and $J=w^b(A(u/w)-B(u/w)/w)$. On the torus $H$ is not identically zero because $w$ and $D(x)$ are nonzero meromorphic functions, with $x$ nonconstant. Therefore $H$ is nonzero on a sufficiently small punctured neighborhood of any given point. [Homogenization of the elliptic identity](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/JetHarmonics/CpTransportedTaylorSeriesShiftEqTranslate.lean#L71)

The local analytic pairs $(u,w)$ at zero and at $p$ have been matched by the Weierstrass flow proof. As $H,J$ are polynomial in this pair, their germs at both points are analytic, and their chart series are carried by $\mathcal S_\zeta$ to those of $H(z+p),J(z+p)$. At zero, the chart product law gives
$$
        \widehat\phi\,\widehat H=\widehat J .
$$
The series of $H,J$ are bounded since the initial $u,w$ series are integral and their polynomial coefficients are finite in number. Shift the equality to obtain
$$
        (\mathcal S_\zeta\widehat\phi)\,\widehat{H(\,\cdot+p)}
                       =\widehat{J(\,\cdot+p)} .
\tag{1}
$$
The shifted denominator is nonzero by invertibility of the chart map on formal germs and the nonvanishing of $H$. [Translation of the two coordinate series](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/JetHarmonics/MultiplicativeShiftEqTranslateBySmallPointStatement.lean#L7) · [Shifting the cleared identity](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/JetHarmonics/CpTransportedTaylorSeriesShiftEqTranslate.lean#L173)

It remains to ensure that (1) gives an actual analytic complex germ. For any nonzero analytic germ $F$ at zero, the order of $\widehat F$ in $X$ equals the order of $F$ in $z$. Indeed the first nonzero complex Taylor coefficient stays nonzero after applying $\iota$, then is multiplied by the corresponding power of $\omega\ne0$ under the zero-constant substitution. If $J$ is identically zero, so is $\phi$ as a meromorphic function and the result follows immediately. Otherwise (1) implies $\operatorname{ord}_{z=0}J(z+p)\ge\operatorname{ord}_{z=0}H(z+p)$ because $\mathcal S_\zeta\widehat\phi$ is a power series. The usual complex germ quotient $J(z+p)/H(z+p)$ thus has at most a removable singularity. Let $\psi$ be that analytic quotient. It agrees with $\phi(z+p)$ on the punctured neighborhood of zero. Apply the chart product law to $\psi H(z+p)=J(z+p)$ and cancel the nonzero denominator series in (1). This yields $\widehat\psi=\mathcal S_\zeta\widehat\phi$, as claimed.
