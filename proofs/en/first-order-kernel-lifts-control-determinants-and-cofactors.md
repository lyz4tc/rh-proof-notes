# First-order kernel lifts control determinants and cofactors

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $K$ be a field, $a\ge0$, and $\Psi(T)$ an $a\times a$ matrix over $K[T]$. Write $\Psi_0,\Psi_1$ for its coefficients of degrees zero and one. Define $V=\ker\Psi_0$, and define $W\subseteq V$ to be the vectors $x_0$ for which there is $x_1\in K^a$ satisfying $\Psi_1x_0+\Psi_0x_1=0$. Thus $W$ consists of the reductions of those solutions that lift to the equation $\Psi(T)x=0$ over $K[T]/(T^2)$.

**Proposition.** If $W=0$ and $\dim V\le m$, then $T^{m+1}\nmid\det\Psi$. If $a>0$, $\dim W\le1$, and $\dim V\le m+1$, then some entry of $\operatorname{adj}\Psi$ is not divisible by $T^{2m+1}$.

**Proof.** Over the discrete valuation ring $K[[T]]$, put $\Psi$ in Smith normal form by invertible row and column operations. The new diagonal entries are each a power $T^{e_j}$ or zero. Treat zero as exponent $+\infty$, and sort the exponents in increasing order. The column operations identify the reductions of the kernels over $K[T]/(T^2)$, since the change of coordinates is invertible both modulo $T^2$ and modulo $T$. They also identify the kernels modulo $T$. For the diagonal matrix, an entry of exponent zero contributes to neither kernel. An entry of exponent one contributes to $V$ but has no nonzero reduction among solutions modulo $T^2$. An entry of exponent at least two contributes to both $V$ and $W$. Thus $\dim V$ counts $e_j\ge1$ and $\dim W$ counts $e_j\ge2$.

If $W=0$, all exponents are zero or one, so $\operatorname{ord}_T\det\Psi=\dim V\le m$, since the row and column determinants are units. If $\dim W\le1$, the first $a-1$ exponents are all zero or one. Their sum is at most $m$: if an exponent is at least two, exactly that exponent is omitted, and if there is none, omitting the largest discards one exponent equal to one whenever the kernel is nonzero. The empty sum for $a=1$ has value zero. The product of the first $a-1$ diagonal entries is a corresponding minor of that order. The ideal generated by the $(a-1)$-minors is unchanged by invertible row or column operations, by Cauchy–Binet in both directions. Hence some $(a-1)$-minor of the original matrix has order at most $m$, and in particular at most $2m$. The minors are the signed transposed entries of the adjugate, proving the stated bound.
