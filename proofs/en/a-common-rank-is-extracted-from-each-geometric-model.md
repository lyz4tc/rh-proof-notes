# A common rank is extracted from each geometric model

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix a CM field $E$ with algebraic closure $\Omega$, a prime $\ell$, an isomorphism $\iota\colon\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\simeq\mathbb C$, and a definite-unitary automorphic representation $U$ of rank $a\ge1$ in the project's realization. Let $d>0$ with $\sqrt{-d}\in E$. A usable place is split over the maximal real subfield and lies over a rational prime $q$ for which $-d$ is a square in $\mathbb Q_q$. At a spherical place $w$ write $B_w$ for the Satake multiset and $S_w=q_w^{(a-1)/2}B_w$, where $q_w$ is the residue size. Call a Galois representation $\rho$ a geometric model for this statement if it meets the following conditions, denoted by `VBase`:
- It is continuous and semisimple over $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$.
- Except for a finite set of places, for every usable $w\nmid\ell$ at which $U$ has a Satake multiset, it is unramified and $P_{S_w}$ annihilates the inverse Frobenius after transfer of coefficients by $\iota^{-1}$.
- At some coefficient-prime place and induced embedding, for some compatible period data, its $p$-adic Hodge filtration has among its jumps a lacunary set of $a$ distinct integers, in the sense that disjoint nonempty subsets have unequal averages.

Suppose there is at least one such geometric model. Then there is a continuous semisimple rank-$a$ representation $r$ with characteristic polynomial $P_{S_w}$ at the inverse Frobenius for usable spherical $w\nmid\ell$ outside a finite set, whenever the Frobenius lies in a specified open dense subset of $G_E$. It is unramified at every such $w$. Moreover $r$ embeds in every geometric model, and every such model embeds in a finite sum of copies of $r$. Consequently a local property can be obtained from any model having the required local quality:
1. At every split $w\nmid\ell$, if $S_w$ is product-generic and the model supplies the unramified annihilation condition at $w$, then $r$ has the prescribed Frobenius polynomial there without the open-dense restriction.
2. Whenever a model is crystalline at $w\mid\ell$ and contains all the automorphic filtration jumps at each induced embedding and weight in the sense of `JumpsOcc`, $r$ is crystalline and de Rham with exactly those jumps at these embeddings.
3. At a split spherical $w\mid\ell$ with product-generic Satake data, if a model is crystalline there and its Weil polynomial at an element of degree $-1$ divides a power of $P_{S_w}$ in each compatible bridge, $r$ is crystalline and has the spherical Weil characteristic polynomials at all Weil elements.

This is `DivAll`, the rank extraction before taking congruence limits. ## Annihilators on a dense set

The center supplies a continuous rank-one character $\psi$ with inverse Frobenius value $\iota^{-1}(\prod S_w)$ at every split spherical $w\nmid\ell$, by the [determinant calculation](normalized-satake-determinants-and-annihilating-polynomials-pin-the-local-roots.md). For a geometric model $\rho$, take the set of inverse Frobenius elements at usable spherical places away from the finite exceptional set and $\ell$. This set is dense by the degree-one Čebotarev argument. Each $g$ in it has a monic annihilator $P_g=\iota^{-1}(P_{S_w})$ of degree $a$ with $\psi(g)=(-1)^aP_g(0)$. If $g$ has more than one witness place, one may choose any of these annihilators. [Producing the annihilators](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/ZetaCharacterDivisibility/PDivOne.lean#L35) · [Density at usable places](degree-one-frobenius-elements-are-dense-at-the-usable-split-places.md)

The rank selector uses an additional fact about $\rho$. Let $G$ be its Zariski closure, regarded over an algebraically closed extension of $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$. The $p$-adic Hodge cocharacter argument puts an element $X$ in the Lie algebra of $G$ whose eigenvalues include the lacunary jumps from the model. In particular there are $a$ distinct eigenvalues $e_i$ such that the averages over any two disjoint nonempty index sets are unequal. In Lean the cocharacter comes from the jump decomposition for the chosen embedding and satisfies the equations of the Zariski closure. Differentiation gives $X$, with the exponents as its diagonalized eigenvalues. [The cocharacter input](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/ZetaCharacterDivisibility/HtCocharOf.lean#L20)

Here the exact algebraic input, `DivWCoreLac`, is as follows. Let $\Gamma$ be a compact topological group, $K$ an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero with a Hausdorff topological-ring structure, and $K'$ an algebraically closed extension. Let $\rho\colon\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}_D(K)$ be continuous and semisimple. Suppose on a dense set there are monic degree-$a$ polynomials $P_g$ annihilating $\rho(g)$ with $(-1)^aP_g(0)=\psi(g)$ for a continuous character $\psi$. Suppose the Lie algebra of its Zariski closure over $K'$ contains an element with at least $a$ distinct eigenvalues forming a lacunary set. Then there exists a continuous semisimple rank-$a$ representation $r$ which embeds in $\rho$, and there is an embedding of $\rho$ into finitely many copies of $r$. For every $g$, the characteristic polynomial of $r(g)$ annihilates $\rho(g)$ and $\det r(g)=\psi(g)$. On the given dense set $P_g$ also annihilates $r(g)$. The set of $g$ where $r(g)$ has separable characteristic polynomial is open and dense. For $g$ in the original dense set with separable characteristic polynomial, it equals $P_g$. The same equality holds if $P_g$ is separable and its roots are product-generic. This internal algebraic theorem supplies the extraction of the rank. It follows that the representation extracted from this model has the required Frobenius comparison on an open dense subset: invert the open dense subset of elements having separable characteristic polynomial to obtain the corresponding subset of Frobenius elements. Unramifiedness follows from the embedding $r\hookrightarrow\rho$. The degree-$a$ monic annihilator is the characteristic polynomial at the separable element because its minimal polynomial then has degree $a$.

## Uniqueness across geometric models

If $r'$ is extracted from a different geometric model, remove the union of the two finite exceptional sets. The Frobenius elements at the remaining usable spherical places are dense, and their intersection with both of the open dense Frobenius subsets is dense. At each such Frobenius element the two representations have the same characteristic polynomial on its inverse. Their traces therefore agree on a dense set, and continuity gives equality everywhere. Semisimple representations over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero with equal traces are isomorphic. Thus the representation from the first model embeds in the second, and the second embeds in a finite direct sum of copies of that same representation. ## Local consequences

At a split place away from $\ell$, the embedding into a model transfers the unramified annihilator to $r$. Product-genericity of the Satake roots and the determinant $\psi$ give the required polynomial. At a coefficient-prime place where the relevant model is crystalline, semisimplicity makes $r$ a direct summand of the global model. Crystallinity descends to the summand. If the model contains all the desired jump values, its inclusion into copies of $r$ implies that each such value occurs in $r$. The automorphic jump multiset and the jump multiset of $r$ both have $a$ entries, and the former has no repeats, so this gives all their multiplicities. Finally a model whose degree-$-1$ Weil polynomial divides a power of $P_{S_w}$ transfers this divisibility to $r$. Its determinant is the product of the Satake roots, hence product-genericity gives equality at this Weil element and therefore at all Weil elements of the crystalline parameter.
