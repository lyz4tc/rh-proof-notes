# Two evaluations of the one-vector mean give the volume ratio

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E/F$ be the unramified-at-finite-places quadratic CM host of the construction, let $f=[F:\mathbb Q]$, and write $v_E=|D_E|^{1/(2f)}/2$, where $D_E$ is the absolute discriminant. For a Hermitian space with constant real signature and a self-dual lattice write $V(W)$ for the unitary adelic quotient volume with finite lattice stabilizer of mass one and the compact-dual normalization at infinity. Let $W$ have rank $n\ge3$ and signature $(P,Q)$ at every real place. Choose $\beta=1$ if $P\ge1$, or $\beta=-1$ if $Q\ge1$. At the respective step the intended complement has signature $(P-1,Q)$ or $(P,Q-1)$. Set $c_n=(2\pi)^n/(n-1)!$, and let $\eta$ be the quadratic character of $E/F$.

**Claim.** There is a vector $x$ of norm $\beta$ with a complement $W'=x^\perp$ carrying a self-dual lattice. The volumes satisfy
$$
 \frac{V(W')}{V(W)}
 =c_n^f\frac{((2v_E)^f)^{1/2-n}}{L(n,\eta^n)}.
$$
The proof uses the convergent one-vector Siegel–Weil identity in the following test-function case. With the trace-self-dual measures from a nontrivial additive character of $\mathbb A_F/F$, use any nonnegative continuous compactly supported function $\Phi_\infty$ on $W_\infty$ and the finite test $\mathbf1_{\widehat L}$ of a full lattice $L$. For every nonzero $\beta\in F$, the normalized unitary quotient mean of
$$
 \sum_{\substack{y\in W\\ h(y,y)=\beta}}
       \Phi_\infty((g^{-1}y)_\infty)\mathbf1_{\widehat L}((g^{-1}y)_f)
$$
equals the integral of $\Phi_\infty$ on the archimedean norm-$\beta$ fibre times the product of the local integrals of $\mathbf 1_{L_v}$ on the finite norm-$\beta$ fibres. The measures on these regular fibres are the continuous disintegrations of trace-self-dual additive measures for the norm map. This is the portion of `SW1b2` used here. For the finite test, the completed lattice is a restricted product and equals a coordinate lattice at almost all places, so its indicator is a pure finite test in that identity.

By the local classification of self-dual lattices, $W$ is isometric at all finite places to the signed diagonal space of rank $n$; this is the space with $P$ diagonal entries $+1$ and $Q$ entries $-1$. It has the same signatures at infinity. Landherr's local-to-global theorem gives a rational signed orthonormal basis. Take $x$ to be a basis vector of the prescribed sign. The span of this basis over $\mathcal O_E$ is self-dual, and the span of the other basis vectors is a self-dual lattice in $W'$. The finite stabilizers of any two self-dual lattices in $W$ are conjugate by an adelic isometry: use the matching local bases at the exceptional places, and the identity where the lattices already agree. The group is unimodular, so conjugation preserves Haar measure and their normalizations give the same quotient volume. This permits using the signed basis lattice $L$ for the calculation.

Choose $\Phi_\infty$ as a product of nonnegative continuous compactly supported bumps, positive in a neighborhood of $x$. Its integral $A$ on the real norm fibre is finite and strictly positive. Finiteness follows from compact support and local finiteness of the measures on the smooth fibres. Positivity follows from the positive smooth density near $x$ at each real place. By [unfolding the unit-norm orbit](unfolding-a-unit-norm-orbit-leaves-the-complement-volume.md), the normalized mean is the archimedean quotient integral times $V(W')/V(W)$. In the [compact-dual normalization](the-compact-dual-normalizes-the-real-unitary-quotient.md), that quotient integral is $c_n^{-f}A$. Thus the mean is $(V(W')/V(W))c_n^{-f}A$.

In the other evaluation, apply the stated Siegel–Weil identity. The real factor is $A$. At every finite place, self-duality and the unit value $\beta$ put the lattice test under the [residue norm count](counting-nonzero-residue-norms-gives-the-local-fibre-density.md). For the standard global additive character, the [Euler product of those counts](the-different-and-euler-factors-give-the-product-of-local-densities.md) is $((2v_E)^f)^{1/2-n}/L(n,\eta^n)$. Equating the two evaluations and dividing by $A c_n^{-f}$ gives the formula. In particular the even-rank denominator is $\zeta_F(n)$, since $\eta^n$ is trivial in even rank.
