# The new prime preserves the dyadic pairing and ray coordinates

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix an admissible localized family with $R$ first-block and $n<R$ second-block places at level $\ell^M$, where $M\ge\nu+2$. Each displayed place denotes its pair with the CM conjugate. Let $L\triangleleft G$ be the open small subgroup in the localization conditions, and let $L_0$ be its intersection with the kernels of the active ray characters, namely the first $n$ first-block characters and the $n$ second-block characters. Thus $L_0$ is open normal and stable under CM conjugation. Write $\bar g=\iota(g)$. Let $x,y$ be the ordinary adjugate-column cocycle and the dual cocycle on $A^r$, and assume that some $h\in L_0$ has
$$
B(h)=x(h)\cdot y(h)-x(\bar h)\cdot y(\bar h)\ne0 .
$$
The two actions $T_1,T_2$ are trivial on $L_0$ and satisfy $T_1(g)^{\mathsf T}T_2(g)=\epsilon(g)I$, where $\epsilon$ is the cyclotomic scalar in $A^\times$, invariant under CM conjugation. Under `Small2`, `PiLocalization₂` and `AdmFam`, Chebotarev gives a further allowed second-block place $p$, with a Frobenius lift $\sigma$ and inertia element $\gamma$ for which $B(\sigma)\ne0$. All old places are preserved. In additive ray coordinates, the first-block characters take the $n$-th standard basis vector on every Frobenius lift at $p$ (indices start at zero), and the new character takes $0$ at $\sigma$ and $1$ at $\gamma$.

On $L_0$, both cocycles are continuous homomorphisms into the additive module. Let $U$ be their joint kernel there, imposing the same two vanishing conditions after applying $\iota$. This is open normal: for an element of $L_0$, conjugating its cocycle value by $k\in G$ acts by the corresponding $T_i(k)$, by the cocycle identity and the trivial $L_0$-action. If $g\sigma g^{-1}h^{-1}\in U$, it follows that $x(\sigma)=T_1(g^{-1})x(h)$, and likewise for the other three values with $T_2$ and $\iota(g^{-1})$. The two dot products acquire the same unit $\epsilon(g^{-1})$. Consequently $B(\sigma)=\epsilon(g^{-1})B(h)\ne0$.

For an unused first-block place its inertia lies in $L_0$: it lies in $L$, and every active character is unramified there. On that inertia both twists and both untwisted representations are trivial. A tame inertia value of a local cocycle satisfies $T_i(\phi)u=Np_i\,u$ for a local Frobenius $\phi$. At these first-block places the untwisted residual Frobenius is a scalar different from $1$, and $Np_i\equiv1\bmod\ell$. Raising to the $\ell^M$-th power removes the scalar twist and leaves an invertible matrix in the eigenvalue equation, as in the [starting cofactor calculation](surplus-first-block-places-do-not-change-the-starting-cofactor-bound.md). Thus both cocycles vanish there and on the conjugate inertia. In particular all unused first-block inertia is in $U$.

Correct the unused first-block coordinates of $h$ by multiplying it on the left by a product $u\in U$ of first-block inertia elements. For each unused index, its inertia surjects to its own coordinate $\mathbb Z/\ell^M$ and is zero in every other first-block coordinate. Thus the correction can make coordinate $n$ equal to $1$ and every other coordinate equal to $0$. The already active first-block coordinates were zero on $h$. Apply Chebotarev to the conjugacy class of $uh$ modulo the open normal subgroup $V=U\cap\bigcap_{j<R}\ker d_j$, where $d_j$ are the first-block characters. Use the degree-one version, excluding the old pairs, all forbidden or exceptional places, places ramified in the fixed field of $U$, the primes dividing $2\ell$, and the finite exceptional set for the CM splitting condition. Thus the new place and its conjugate are split, unramified of degree one over the rational prime, and lie outside the prescribed exclusions. The new Frobenius $\sigma_0$ is conjugate to $uh$ modulo $V$. Its decomposition group is contained in $L$ because $L$ contains $\sigma_0$ and the inertia, which is contained in $U$. Since $L$ fixes the $2\ell^M$-th roots of unity, $Np\equiv1\bmod 2\ell^M$. The decomposition group also lies in $L_0$: the first $n$ first-block coordinates of $\sigma_0$ vanish, and the old second-block characters vanish on $h$, on $U$, and on the new inertia. These kernels are normal and closed.

The new admissible character $d_p$ surjects on its inertia and is unramified outside the new pair. Choose $\gamma$ in that inertia with $d_p(\gamma)=1$, and choose $\tau$ there with $d_p(\tau)=-d_p(\sigma_0)$, using additive character notation. Then $\sigma=\sigma_0\tau$ is still Frobenius and satisfies $d_p(\sigma)=0$. None of the old coordinates changes, since their characters are unramified there. Also $\tau\in U$, and $u\in U$, so $\sigma$ is conjugate to $h$ modulo $U$. The unit comparison gives $B(\sigma)\ne0$ for this same Frobenius with the corrected coordinates.
