# Two limits of one lattice count give the Siegel mass ratio

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E/F$ be a CM extension unramified at every finite place, $f=[F:\mathbb Q]$, and let $v_E=|\operatorname{disc}(E)|^{1/(2f)}/2$, so the archimedean covolume of $\mathcal O_E$ in one complex embedding from each pair is $v_E^f$. For a positive definite Hermitian space over $E$ at all archimedean places, the genus of a full integral lattice $L$ is the set of full lattices in the same space locally isometric to $L$ at every finite place. The mass $\mu(L)$ is the sum of $1/|\operatorname{Aut}(M)|$ over rational isometry classes $M$ in that genus. If $L_m,L_{m-1}$ have integral orthonormal bases of ranks $m,m-1$, respectively, and $m\ge3$, then
$$
 \frac{\mu(L_m)}{\mu(L_{m-1})}
   =B_m,\qquad
 B_m=\frac{((m-1)!)^f\,v_E^{fm}}{\pi^{fm}\sqrt{(2v_E)^f}}\,
             L(m,\eta^m),
\tag{1}
$$
where $\eta(\mathfrak p)=1$ at split primes and $-1$ at inert primes of $F$, and $L(m,\eta^m)=\prod_{\mathfrak p}(1-\eta(\mathfrak p)^m\mathrm N\mathfrak p^{-m})^{-1}$. This is the successive rank ratio used to compare the standard masses in the column dimension estimate. The induction below uses $B_j$ also at $j=2$. ## The same finite count in two orders

Use any full integral $L$ in a totally positive space of rank $m$. Its discriminant ideal is generated by all determinants of the $m\times m$ Hermitian Gram matrices of $m$-tuples in $L$. Put $D_L=\sqrt{\mathrm N_E(\operatorname{disc} L)}$. Let $r_M(a)$ count the vectors $x\in M$ with $h(x,x)=a$, and put $R_L(a)=\sum_M r_M(a)/|\operatorname{Aut}(M)|$. The class set is finite and its stabilizers are finite by definiteness. The positive box $0<\sigma(a)\le j^2$ at every real embedding $\sigma$ meets $\mathcal O_F$ in a finite set, since its embedding is a lattice in $\mathbb R^f$. The finite weighted number of vectors with $\sigma(h(x,x))\le j^2$ can be counted by each genus representative or by each norm $a$. The zero vector contributes $\mu(L)$. Therefore
$$
 \sum_M\frac{\#\{x\in M:\sigma(h(x,x))\le j^2\ \forall \sigma\}}{|\operatorname{Aut}(M)|}
 =\mu(L)+\sum_{\substack{a\in\mathcal O_F\\0<\sigma(a)\le j^2\ \forall\sigma}}R_L(a).
\tag{2}
$$
The divided count on the left, with denominator $j^{2fm}$, tends to
$$
 \mu(L)\frac{(\pi^m/m!)^f}{v_E^{fm}D_L}.
\tag{3}
$$
For each $M$ this follows from [the count in the dilated norm ellipsoids](the-discriminant-combines-the-volume-and-lattice-index-of-a-hermitian-ball.md). Its unit volume is $(\pi^m/m!)^f$ divided by the product of the embedded Gram determinants, and the lattice and Gram determinants combine to $v_E^{fm} D_L$. The discriminant ideal depends only on the local lattices, hence is the same for all $M$ in the genus. ## The limit from representation densities

Here are the local quantities used to express the other side of (2). For $\mathfrak p$ of $F$, let $q=\mathrm N\mathfrak p$, write $\Delta_{\mathfrak p}$ for the largest $e$ such that $\operatorname{disc} L\subseteq(\mathfrak p\mathcal O_E)^e$, and put $A_{\mathfrak p}(m)=\prod_{i=1}^m(1-\eta(\mathfrak p)^i q^{-i})$. Let $\alpha_{\mathfrak p}(a)$ be the limit of the number of vectors of norm $a$ modulo $\mathfrak p^k$, divided by $q^{(2m-1)k}$. Let $\alpha_{\mathfrak p}(L)$ be the limit of the number of endomorphisms preserving the Hermitian form modulo $\mathfrak p^k$, divided by $q^{m^2k}$. The congruences here are at $\mathfrak p$, in the local lattice over $\mathcal O_E\otimes_{\mathcal O_F}\mathcal O_{F,\mathfrak p}$. Set
$$
 \lambda(L)=\prod_{\mathfrak p}\frac{A_{\mathfrak p}(m)q^{m\Delta_{\mathfrak p}}}{\alpha_{\mathfrak p}(L)},
 \qquad
 S_L(a)=\prod_{\mathfrak p}\frac{\alpha_{\mathfrak p}(a)}{1-\eta(\mathfrak p)^m q^{-m}} .
$$
The first product has trivial factors at unimodular places. The second has trivial factors away from $\operatorname{disc} L$ and $a$. In particular $\lambda(L)=1$ for an orthonormal integral lattice. A constant in rank $m$ means a number $c_m$ with $\mu(L)=c_m\lambda(L)$ for every totally positive integral lattice of that rank. [Local densities, determinant and products](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/GL3MeanSquares/GenusMeanAsymptotics/UnimodularClassRepresentatives/LocalDensityRatio.lean#L51) · [Local factors for orthonormal lattices](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/GL3MeanSquares/GenusMeanAsymptotics/UnimodularClassRepresentatives/LamGEqOneOfOrtho.lean#L17)

Assume there is a positive constant $c_{m-1}$ in rank $m-1$. For $m\ge2$, the representation mass formula gives
$$
 R_L(a)=c_{m-1}\lambda(L)D_L^{-1}\,\mathrm N_F(a)^{m-1}\,S_L(a)
 \quad(a\in\mathcal O_F\text{ totally positive}).
\tag{4}
$$
First [choose a rational vector of norm $a$](a-totally-positive-binary-hermitian-form-represents-every-positive-norm.md), then use [the orbit sum for its orthogonal complement](orthogonal-complements-factor-the-weighted-representation-count.md) and [the identity for the fibre densities](a-unit-vector-pins-the-local-representation-mass-factor.md). The averaged local product has the limit
$$
 T^{-fm}\sum_{0<\sigma(a)\le T\ \forall\sigma}\mathrm N_F(a)^{m-1}S_L(a)
 \longrightarrow
 \frac{m^{-f}}{\sqrt{(2v_E)^f}}\,L(m,\eta^m)
 \qquad (T=j^2,\ j\longrightarrow\infty).
\tag{5}
$$
[This limit](divisor-expansion-averages-the-full-singular-product.md) uses the mean of the congruence counts at fixed primes and a summable majorant for the remaining prime powers. These are the exact arithmetic inputs to this step. [Representation mass identity](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/GL3MeanSquares/GenusMeanAsymptotics/UnimodularClassRepresentatives/FactorIdentity.lean#L101) · [Averaged singular product](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/GL3MeanSquares/GenusMeanAsymptotics/UnimodularClassRepresentatives/TendstoSingSSum.lean#L100)

## Compare the limits and remove the rank-one constant

The constant term $\mu(L)$ on the right of (2) tends to zero after division by $j^{2fm}$. Apply (4) and (5) to the remaining term. Its limit is
$$
 c_{m-1}\lambda(L)D_L^{-1}\frac{m^{-f}}{\sqrt{(2v_E)^f}}\,L(m,\eta^m).
$$
This equals (3) by (2). Cancel $D_L^{-1}$ and use $m!/m=(m-1)!$. We obtain $\mu(L)=c_{m-1}B_m\lambda(L)$, so $c_m=c_{m-1}B_m$ is a common constant in rank $m$. [The rank-one argument](fractional-ideal-multiplication-fixes-the-rank-one-mass.md) gives one positive $c_1$ for all rank-one lattices. All $B_j$ are positive since $j\ge2$ and the Euler product converges to a positive number. Inductively $c_m=c_1\prod_{j=2}^m B_j$. For the orthonormal lattices the corrections $\lambda$ are one, so their quotient cancels $c_1$ and all the factors through $m-1$, leaving (1). The original use of the theorem requires $m\ge3$.
