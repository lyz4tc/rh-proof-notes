# Leading minors give the two archimedean types in theta

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Use the CM base, source $X$ and definite target $W$ of the [stable-range lift](a-definite-theta-lift-in-the-stable-range.md), with ranks $1\le n\le d$. Fix the model $M$, its Weil action $\omega$ for splitting characters $\chi_X,\chi_W$, and the identifications of the source and target unitary groups with the standard definite groups. Let $U$ be an irreducible automorphic representation of the source group with dominant weights $\lambda_v$ at the chosen embeddings. Suppose $d+m_W(v)\le2\lambda_v(i)$ at every $v,i$. Then there is a nonzero archimedean polynomial Schwartz vector $f_\infty$ and a nonzero $\phi\in U$ obtained from the same vector in the same irreducible representation of the source group at infinity by two equivariant maps. At each real place $v$ the vector $f_\infty$ is also in the image of a map equivariant for the target factor alone, from the irreducible representation of weight $\lambda'_v$ in the stable-range weight formula. These simultaneous conclusions prescribe the vector that will later be used globally. ## Writing down the common highest-weight vector

The archimedean comparison, `fockOf_k`, identifies the polynomial Schwartz sector with the tensor product over real places of copies of $P=\mathbb C[Z_{ij}:1\le i\le n,\,1\le j\le d]$. It gives integers $a_v,b_v$ such that
$$
2 a_v=d+m_W(v),\qquad 2 b_v=n+m_X(v).
$$
The source acts at $v$ by the substitution $Z_{ij}\mapsto\sum_r g_{ri}Z_{rj}$, multiplied by $(\det g)^{a_v}$. The target acts by $Z_{ij}\mapsto\sum_s h_{sj}Z_{is}$, multiplied by $(\det h)^{b_v}$. The project establishes the comparison by identifying the Fock polynomial subspace and computing both infinitesimal actions. On the constant polynomial only the determinant scalar remains. Its return after a full revolution in a single compact circle gives the integrality of the half-exponents. Exponentiation of the computed actions gives the two compact-group formulas. The archimedean Fock and infinitesimal Weil-action inputs are used here. [Fock comparison](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/ThetaKernels/GRPFockOf.lean#L259) · [Polynomial actions](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/BlockDeterminants/ExponentialEntries/RightSub.lean#L14)

For each $v$ write $\mu_i=\lambda_v(i)-a_v$ for $1\le i\le n$ and $\mu_{n+1}=0$. The hypothesis gives $\mu_n\ge0$, and dominance gives $\mu_i\ge\mu_{i+1}$ for $i<n$. Let $D_j$ be the leading $j\times j$ minor of $Z$, and set
$$
J_v=\prod_{j=1}^n D_j^{\mu_j-\mu_{j+1}} .
$$
The minor $D_j$ is fixed by an upper triangular unipotent matrix acting on the row indices of the variables, since it acts on the span of the first $j$ indices with determinant $1$. The same holds for the column indices. Under either diagonal action $D_j$ has weight the product of the first $j$ diagonal entries. It follows by telescoping that $J_v$ has untwisted row weight $\mu$ and untwisted column weight $(\mu,0,\ldots,0)$. It is nonzero, as evaluation on $[I_n\mid0]$ gives $1$. Accounting for the determinant powers, its two weights are
$$
\lambda_v=(\mu_1+a_v,\ldots,\mu_n+a_v),\qquad
\lambda'_v=(\mu_1+b_v,\ldots,\mu_n+b_v,b_v,\ldots,b_v).
$$
In the square polynomial model of a dominant weight $\gamma$ of length $r$, put $\nu_i=\gamma_i-\gamma_r$. Its underlying Weyl module is generated by the leading-minor polynomial for $\nu$, with the row substitution action multiplied by $(\det g)^{\gamma_r}$. The Lean proof supplies the two maps directly from these models.

For the source map, embed the $n\times n$ variables as the first $n$ columns of $Z$, and multiply by $D_n^{\mu_n}$. Embedding commutes with the row substitutions. Since $D_n$ transforms by $\det g$ for this action and $a_v+\mu_n=\lambda_v(n)$, multiplication gives precisely the change in determinant twist. The square module has leader with exponents from $\nu_i=\mu_i-\mu_n$, so the map sends it to $J_v$. For the target map, send the $d\times d$ variable in row $i$, column $j$ to $Z_{ji}$ for $j\le n$ and to $0$ otherwise. This commutes with row substitution on the square model and column substitution on $Z$. If $d>n$, the square module's normalized weight is $(\mu,0,\ldots,0)$ and its determinant twist is $b_v$. Its leader goes directly to $J_v$, since all leading minors of size greater than $n$ have exponent zero. If $d=n$, multiply additionally by $D_n^{\mu_n}$. Now this top minor transforms by $\det h$ and the determinant twist is $b_v+\mu_n$, giving the column intertwiner and hitting $J_v$ as before. Thus both equivariant maps have preimages $x_v,x'_v$ of the same vector. ## Matching the input form

The product of the source compact groups acts on the tensor product $V=\bigotimes_v V_{\lambda_v}$, which is irreducible for that product. The individual types of $U$ give an equivariant embedding of $V$ in $U$. To see the joint occurrence, for each $v$ the full-group translates of a single local copy of $V_{\lambda_v}$ are copies of the same type because the other adelic factors commute with the factor at $v$. Their span is nonzero and invariant under the whole adelic group, hence is $U$. Thus every vector of $U$ is isotypic for each separate factor. Its finite-dimensional archimedean orbit, decomposed under the product compact group, can have only the tensor product type. Taking a nonzero orbit supplies the embedding $j$ used in the proof. [Joint archimedean type in the automorphic forms](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/ThetaParseval/BlockDeterminants/ExponentialEntries/FormEx.lean#L227)

Let $x=\bigotimes_v x_v$. The tensor product of the row maps sends $x$ to $J=\bigotimes_v J_v$, which is nonzero since all its factors are nonzero over $\mathbb C$. The Fock comparison converts it to $f_\infty$, and $j$ takes $x$ to the nonzero form $\phi=j(x)$. These are the required equivariant images of $x$. To read the target type at one place $v$, use the column map there and hold all $J_w$ with $w\ne v$ fixed. The target factor at $v$ acts trivially on those other factors, so the resulting map remains equivariant and hits $f_\infty$ at $x'_v$. Substituting the formulas for $a_v,b_v$ gives the displayed stable-range weight formula. This finishes the simultaneous construction.
