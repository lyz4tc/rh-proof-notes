# The torsion Selmer functor is left exact and has finite coefficient support

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix the CM host, its integral rank-$r$ representation, and the auxiliary inverse twist over the local coefficient algebra $\Lambda$, of residue characteristic $\ell$. Use the Selmer functor on discrete modules $B$ whose elements are killed by powers of the maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. At the places over $\ell$ use the finite conditions generated by integral crystalline cocycles. Assume the auxiliary twist is trivial there. At the remaining allowed places impose no restriction, and require unramified cocycles elsewhere, where the representation and twist are unramified. Assume $r\ge2$, the residual matrices of the integral representation span the full matrix algebra, and the scalar auxiliary twist has $\ell$-power order and open kernel.

**Proposition.** The Selmer functor is left exact, and each class for $B$ comes from the Selmer group for some finitely generated submodule $B_0\subseteq B$.

**Proof.** First, for every such $B$ the global invariant group of the twisted representation on $B^r$ is zero. If an invariant vector were nonzero, some power of $\mathfrak m$ would kill it. Choose the least such power $\mathfrak m^n$. Multiplication by an element of $\mathfrak m^{n-1}$ gives a nonzero invariant vector annihilated by $\mathfrak m$. On this part of the module the auxiliary character acts trivially, since its values reduce to one. Any other scalar change of the integral representation merely makes each residual matrix act by a scalar on that vector. By the spanning assumption every matrix would then send the vector to a scalar multiple of itself. In detail, choose a nonzero coordinate $j$. The matrix $E_{jj}$ forces every other coordinate to vanish, as the scalar on the nonzero coordinate must be one. For $i\ne j$, the matrix $E_{ij}$ gives a nonzero vector supported at $i$, a contradiction. This proves the assertion about invariants.

Consider $0\to B'\to B\to B''$ exact through $B$, with map from $B$ allowed to have proper image in $B''$. If a cocycle for $B'$ becomes a coboundary in $B$, the vector giving the coboundary maps to an invariant in $B''$, so it maps to zero. It therefore comes from $B'$, and the original class is already zero. If a class for $B$ maps to zero in $B''$, its cocycle maps into the coboundary of a vector in $(B'')^r$. Modulo the image of $B^r$, that vector is invariant, hence zero, since the quotient is again a torsion coefficient module. Lift the vector back to $B^r$ and subtract its coboundary. The resulting cocycle takes values in the kernel $B'^r$.

Check the local conditions for this lift. At a place over $\ell$, its image satisfies the finite condition and the [saturated cocycle argument](a-saturated-crystalline-cocycle-module-reflects-the-local-condition.md) reflects that condition through $B'\hookrightarrow B$. At the allowed places away from $\ell$ there is no condition to check. At any other place the inertia action is trivial on both modules, so a class that restricts to zero on inertia is represented by a cocycle vanishing there. Hence the lifted cocycle vanishes there by injectivity. These checks prove left exactness.

Finally a continuous cocycle with discrete coefficients has finite image by compactness of the absolute Galois group. Take $B_0$ to be the $\Lambda$-span of the finitely many coordinates of its values. The action is by matrices over $\Lambda$, so the cocycle law holds inside $B_0^r$. Its class belongs to the Selmer group there, by exactly the same reflection of local conditions for $B_0\hookrightarrow B$. It maps to the given class.

**Lean correspondence.** `exists_socle`, `eq_zero_of_span` and `invariants_eq_bot` supply the absence of global invariants. `mapH1_injective` and `exists_lift` are the cochain steps, and `selmer_left_exact` checks the local restrictions. `selColim` uses the finite image of a cocycle and the same reflection at the local places.
