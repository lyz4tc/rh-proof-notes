# Normalized Satake determinants and annihilating polynomials pin the local roots

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $S$ be a multiset of $a$ nonzero complex numbers. Assume $S$ is product-generic in the following sense: among multisets $T$ of size $a$, with entries all belonging to the support of $S$, the equality $\prod T=\prod S$ implies $T=S$. Put $P_S(X)=\prod_{s\in S}(X-s)$. A monic polynomial $P$ of degree $a$ that divides $P_S^m$ for some $m\ge0$ and whose roots have product $\prod S$ is exactly $P_S$. The matrix version says that a matrix of size $a$ annihilated by $P_S$ and with determinant $\prod S$ has characteristic polynomial $P_S$. These assertions give two local comparisons for the rank extracted from a geometric representation. ## Polynomial and matrix arguments

Every root of the monic polynomial $P$ belongs to the support of $S$ because $P\mid P_S^m$. Its multiset of roots has size $a$, since it splits over $\mathbb C$ and has degree $a$. The product condition and product-genericity force its roots to equal $S$ with multiplicity. Monicity now gives $P=P_S$. For a matrix $A$ with $P_S(A)=0$, each eigenvalue belongs to the support of $S$, by applying $P_S(A)$ to a corresponding eigenvector. The characteristic polynomial splits and its roots have product $\det A$. The same argument gives its characteristic polynomial. When the entries lie in $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$, transport the polynomial, matrix and determinant using $\iota$. Polynomial evaluation and taking determinants or characteristic polynomials commute with that transport. [Transport and matrix argument](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/ZetaCharacterDivisibility/PGenPin.lean#L28)

## Obtaining the determinant from the center

For a rank-$a$ definite-unitary automorphic datum $U$, at a split finite place $w$ put $q_w=|\mathcal O_E/w|$ and let $B$ be the local Satake multiset of $U$. Set
$$
S_w=q_w^{(a-1)/2}B,\qquad \prod S_w=q_w^{\binom a2}\prod B.
$$
The unitary center provides an algebraic Hecke character $\chi$ of $E$ whose local component is unramified at such a spherical $w$ with value $\prod B$ on a uniformizer. The map from ideles into the center is $z\mapsto(z/\overline z)I_a$. A central element acts by a scalar on $U$ by Schur's lemma. At the split place this pulls back to the central action of scalar matrices on the $\mathrm{GL}_a$ component. Integral scalar units lie in the spherical stabilizer and fix the spherical vector. The scalar uniformizer corresponds to the top spherical Hecke operator. Its double coset has a single coset because the scalar is central, and its eigenvalue is the top elementary symmetric function $\prod B$. The algebraicity of the resulting global scalar character follows from the integral archimedean weight. Twist $\chi$ by the absolute value to the power $-\binom a2$. Its value on a uniformizer is multiplied by $q_w^{\binom a2}$, so supplies $\prod S_w$. By rank-one reciprocity let $\psi$ be its $\ell$-adic realization in the project's inverse-Frobenius convention. Away from $\ell$, $\iota(\psi(\mathrm{Frob}_w^{-1}))=\prod S_w$, and the corresponding crystalline line at $w\mid\ell$ has this scalar at an element of Weil degree $-1$. For an extracted rank-$a$ representation whose characteristic polynomial agrees with $P_{S_w}$ on an open dense subset of Frobenius elements away from $\ell$, equality $\det r=\psi$ holds everywhere by continuity. To see this, intersect the open dense set with the dense allowed Frobenius set, use equality of the signed constant terms, then extend equality in $\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$. ## The two local comparisons

Suppose the geometric model $\rho$ contains $r$ as a subrepresentation and provides the local annihilating or divisibility condition. Away from $\ell$, assume $\rho$ is unramified at $w$ and $P_{S_w}$ annihilates its inverse Frobenius. Then $r$ is also unramified and this polynomial annihilates its inverse Frobenius. Its determinant is prescribed by the preceding paragraph. Product-genericity and the matrix argument give the exact characteristic polynomial. [The Frobenius condition](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/ZetaCharacterDivisibility/PhiOfDivOf.lean#L148) · [Matrix comparison](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/IntegralStructures/ZetaCharacterDivisibility/PGenPin.lean#L28)

At $w\mid\ell$, assume $\rho$ is crystalline and that, for any fixed compatible period bridge and prolongation, there exists a Weil element $g$ of degree $-1$ for which its Weil polynomial divides a power of $P_{S_w}$. The semisimple global model splits as $r\oplus r_1$. This splitting gives crystallinity for $r$ and divisibility of the Weil characteristic polynomial of $r$ by the one for $\rho$. The Weil realization respects determinants for crystalline representations, so the product of the roots for $r$ at $g$ is the eigenvalue of the rank-one realization of $\psi$, namely $\prod S_w$. The polynomial argument identifies the Weil polynomial of $r$ at $g$ with $P_{S_w}$. Its crystalline Weil action and the reference Satake action are unramified and Frobenius-semisimple. Equality of characteristic polynomials at the degree-$-1$ generator gives equality of eigenvalue multisets there, and taking integral powers gives equality at all Weil elements.
