# A split CM idempotent represents every good theta lift

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

## Statement

Let $d,\mathcal O,H,B,\Lambda$ satisfy the assumptions of [the Hermitian dual-basis theorem](a-prime-to-q-multiple-clears-the-hermitian-dual-basis.md), including the test that any $y$ with $\operatorname{Im}H(\Lambda,y)\subseteq\mathbb Z$ is in $\Lambda$. Take a split prime $q$ of $\mathcal O$, allowing $q=2$, a chosen embedding $\iota:\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})\hookrightarrow\mathbb C_q$, and a basis list $b_1,\ldots,b_r$ satisfying the same prime-to-$q$ clearing and independence assumptions. In Lean the embedding is given on the complex algebraic numbers. Only its restriction to this quadratic field is used in the congruences below. Write $|\cdot|_q$ with $|q|_q=q^{-1}$, and put $\mathfrak q$ for the prime above $q$ chosen by $\iota$.

For a function $F:\mathbb C^g\to\mathbb C$ and $x,z\in\mathbb C^g$, set
$$
 \begin{split}
  (U_xF)(z)&=\exp(-\pi H(z,x)-\tfrac\pi2 H(x,x))F(z+x),\\
  W_xF&=\exp(\pi i B(x,x))U_xF,\qquad
  \kappa(x,z)=\exp(-2\pi i\,\operatorname{Im}H(x,z)).
 \end{split}
$$
Take an infinitely complex-differentiable theta $e$ satisfying $W_xe=e$ for every $x\in\Lambda$. Let $N_0>0$ be prime to $q$ and let $N_0t\in\Lambda$. For $n\ge0$ take $y$ in the additive subgroup generated by the points
$$
      \frac{N_0\gamma}{q^n}x,\qquad
      x\in\Lambda,\quad \gamma\in\mathcal O,\quad
      |\iota(\bar\gamma)|_q\le q^{-2n}.
$$
Then $B(y,y)$ is an integer, and there exist $m\ge2n$, $\epsilon\in\mathcal O$, and digits $0\le a_i<q^n$ such that
$$
 |\iota(\epsilon)-1|_q\le q^{-m},\qquad
 |\iota(\bar\epsilon)|_q\le q^{-m},\qquad
 \operatorname{Im}H(\epsilon b_i,t)\in\mathbb Z\quad(1\le i\le r).
$$
Put $Y_\epsilon=(\epsilon/q^n)\sum_i a_i b_i$. It has the same action and the same finite characters as $y$:
$$
 \begin{split}
   W_y U_t e&=W_{Y_\epsilon}U_t e,\\
   \kappa\!\left(\sum_i j_i b_i,y\right)
        &=\kappa\!\left(\sum_i(j_i\bmod q^n)b_i,Y_\epsilon\right)
          &&(j_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}).
 \end{split}
\tag{1}
$$
Here $\epsilon$ is idempotent modulo $q^m$ under the two-factor decomposition at the split prime. The additive subgroup in the hypothesis uses finite integer linear combinations. This is the good-lift comparison used when passing from [the normalized theta measure to the original point tables](transporting-the-bounded-measure-to-the-normalized-theta-pair.md). ## Proof

### Keeping one split valuation at precision $2n$

Every good lift can be written $y=(N_0/q^n)v$ where $v\in\Lambda$ and
$$
           |\iota(B(x,v))|_q\le q^{-2n}\qquad(x\in\Lambda).
\tag{2}
$$
For a generator take $v=\gamma x_0$. The lattice is stable under $\mathcal O$, and $B(x,\gamma x_0)=\bar\gamma B(x,x_0)$. The second factor is integral at the chosen place. For sums and additive inverses, (2) follows from the ultrametric bound and additivity. The value $B(v,v)$ is real in $\mathcal O$, so is an integer. By (2) it is divisible by $q^{2n}$. Hence $B(y,y)=(N_0^2/q^{2n})B(v,v)$ is integral.

By the Chinese remainder theorem choose $\epsilon_0\in\mathcal O$ with $N_0\epsilon_0\equiv1\bmod\mathfrak q^{2n}$ and $\epsilon_0\equiv0\bmod\overline{\mathfrak q}^{2n}$. Put $\epsilon=N_0\epsilon_0$ and $m=2n$. The displayed bounds on $\epsilon$ follow, and
$$
 \operatorname{Im}H(\epsilon b_i,t)=\operatorname{Im}H(\epsilon_0b_i,N_0t)\in\mathbb Z
$$
by integrality of $B$ on $\Lambda$. In fact if $B(x,x')=u+v\eta\in\mathcal O$, then $\operatorname{Im}H(x,x')=v$. [Reduction of the good lift to one lattice vector](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/GlobalInterpolation/GoodLiftEqIdempotentLift.lean#L113) · [Choice of idempotent and the integrality against (t)](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/GlobalInterpolation/GoodLiftEqIdempotentLift.lean#L161)

### Digits from the other split component

Choose $s,c_j$ from the dual basis theorem. The $q$-adic numbers $\iota(B(v,c_j))$ belong to $\mathbb Z_q$. Choose $a_j$ as the representative between $0$ and $q^n-1$ of
$$
      a_j\equiv N_0s^{-1}\iota(B(v,c_j))\pmod{q^n}.
$$
Write $V=\sum_i a_i b_i$ and $w=v-\epsilon_0V$, both in $\Lambda$. The first valuation in (2) is retained:
$$
            |\iota(B(x,w))|_q\le q^{-2n}\qquad(x\in\Lambda),
\tag{3}
$$
because $B(x,\epsilon_0 V)=\bar\epsilon_0 B(x,V)$, with $\bar\epsilon_0$ small at the chosen place. To bound the opposite pairing, first note that $B(V,c_j)=s a_j$ by (2) of the dual basis theorem and Hermitian symmetry. Then
$$
    \iota(B(w,c_j))
      =\iota(B(v,c_j))-\iota(\epsilon_0)s a_j\equiv0\pmod{q^n},
$$
using both the digits and $N_0\iota(\epsilon_0)\equiv1\bmod q^{2n}$, and that $N_0$ is a unit. For any $x\in\Lambda$, expand the second argument $s x$ in the dual basis to get
$$
       sB(w,x)=\sum_j B(b_j,x)\,B(w,c_j).
$$
Thus $|\iota(B(w,x))|_q\le q^{-n}$. This is also the bound on the conjugate of $B(x,w)$ by Hermitian symmetry. [Choice of finite digits and the two valuation estimates](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/GlobalInterpolation/GoodLiftEqIdempotentLift.lean#L181)

An element $a\in\mathcal O$ satisfying both $|\iota(a)|_q\le q^{-n}$ and $|\iota(\bar a)|_q\le q^{-n}$ is divisible by $q^n$ in $\mathcal O$. Those tests give divisibility by both $\mathfrak q^n$ and $\overline{\mathfrak q}^n$, whose product is the ideal $(q^n)$. Therefore $B(x,w)/q^n\in\mathcal O$ for every $x\in\Lambda$. Put $L=w/q^n$. The imaginary parts of $H(x,L)$ are integral by that membership, so the hypothesis of self-duality gives $L\in\Lambda$. Bound (3) gives in addition $|\iota(B(x,L))|_q\le q^{-n}$. In particular
$$
             y=Y_\epsilon+\lambda,\qquad \lambda=N_0 L\in\Lambda .
\tag{4}
$$
[Dividing the error into the period lattice](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/GlobalInterpolation/GoodLiftEqIdempotentLift.lean#L245)

### Equality of the operators and characters

First $\kappa(\lambda,t)=1$, since $H(\lambda,t)=H(L,N_0t)$ has integral imaginary part. The exponential definitions give $W_\lambda U_t=\kappa(\lambda,t)U_tW_\lambda$, hence $W_\lambda U_t e=U_t e$. To factor $W_y$ using (4), the possible scalar is controlled by
$$
       B(Y_\epsilon,\lambda)=\frac{N_0^2\epsilon_0 B(V,L)}{q^n}\in\mathcal O.
$$
Indeed the chosen-place valuation of its numerator is at least $n$ by the bound on $B(V,L)$, and the other valuation is at least $2n$ by $\epsilon_0$. Direct multiplication of the Weyl operators shows
$$
 W_z W_w=\exp\bigl(-\pi i(2\operatorname{Re}B(z,w)+\operatorname{Im}H(z,w))\bigr)W_{z+w}.
$$
If $B(z,w)=u+v\eta\in\mathcal O$, the exponent in parentheses is $2u+v+v=2(u+v)$. Thus $W_y=W_{Y_\epsilon}W_\lambda$ and the first equality of (1) follows.

To check the second, put $B_j=\sum_i j_i b_i$ and $B_j^{(n)}=\sum_i(j_i\bmod q^n)b_i$. Pairing $B_j$ with $\lambda$ gives $\kappa(B_j,\lambda)=1$ because both are periods. Also $B_j-B_j^{(n)}=q^n x$ for some $x\in\Lambda$, and $H(q^n x,Y_\epsilon)=H(x,\epsilon V)$ with $\epsilon V\in\Lambda$. Thus $\kappa(B_j-B_j^{(n)},Y_\epsilon)=1$. Bilinearity of the alternating exponent implies the remaining equality of (1).
