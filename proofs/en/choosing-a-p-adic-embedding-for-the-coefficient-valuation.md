# Choosing a $p$-adic embedding for the coefficient valuation

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

### Statement

Let $K$ be a number field, $\sigma_0:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$ a fixed complex embedding, and $w$ a finite place of $K$ above the rational prime $p$. Write $\mathbb C_p$ for the completed algebraic closure of $\mathbb Q_p$ with its normalized $p$-adic norm. There is a field embedding
$$
\lambda:\overline{\mathbb Q}\cap\mathbb C\longrightarrow\mathbb C_p
$$
such that for all $x\in K$,
$$
x\in\mathcal O_{K,w}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigl|\lambda(\sigma_0(x))\bigr|_p\le1.
$$
There is also an abstract field isomorphism $\iota:\mathbb C\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb C_p$ extending $\lambda$. It gives the same norm test on the image of $K$. In the project, this identifies the valuation in the coefficient field with the norm used in the split theta estimate.

### Realizing the specified place

For an embedding $\nu:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C_p$ define $\mathfrak m_\nu=\{y\in\mathcal O_K:|\nu(y)|_p<1\}$. Every image of an algebraic integer has norm at most one. Indeed, if a root of a monic integral polynomial had norm greater than one, the leading term would have strictly larger norm than all other terms, so their sum could not vanish. The ultrametric inequality and multiplicativity now give that $\mathfrak m_\nu$ is a prime ideal. It contains $p$, so it is a nonzero maximal ideal of $\mathcal O_K$.

There are finitely many embeddings $\nu$, since $K/\mathbb Q$ is finite and separable. Suppose all the ideals $\mathfrak m_\nu$ differ from $w$. By the Chinese remainder theorem choose $y\in w$ with $y\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak m_\nu}$ for every distinct ideal in the list. Thus $|\nu(y)|_p=1$ for every $\nu$. The field norm $\mathrm N_{K/\mathbb Q}(y)$ is the product of the $\nu(y)$ in the algebraically closed field $\mathbb C_p$, so its $p$-adic norm is one. On the other hand it is an integer in the ideal generated by $y$. For example, multiplication by $y$ on $\mathcal O_K$ has this determinant, and its adjugate applied to $1$ gives an element whose product with $y$ equals the determinant. Therefore $\mathrm N_{K/\mathbb Q}(y)\in w\cap\mathbb Z=(p)$, a contradiction. Some embedding $\nu$ satisfies $\mathfrak m_\nu=w$.

Fix that $\nu$. For $x\in\mathcal O_{K,w}$, write $x=a/b$ with $a,b\in\mathcal O_K$ and $b\notin w$. Then $|\nu(a)|_p\le1$ and $|\nu(b)|_p=1$, proving $|\nu(x)|_p\le1$. If $x\notin\mathcal O_{K,w}$, then $x^{-1}$ belongs to the maximal ideal of the local discrete valuation ring $\mathcal O_{K,w}$. It is $a/b$ with $a\in w$ and $b\notin w$. Hence $|\nu(x^{-1})|_p<1$ and $|\nu(x)|_p>1$. This proves the required equivalence.

### Extend to the algebraic and complex coordinates

On $\sigma_0(K)$ use the embedding $\nu\circ\sigma_0^{-1}$. It extends to the algebraic closure of $\mathbb Q$ in $\mathbb C$ because $\mathbb C_p$ is algebraically closed. This gives $\lambda$. The field $\lambda(\overline{\mathbb Q}\cap\mathbb C)$ is countable, and so is its source. The fields $\mathbb C$ and $\mathbb C_p$ both have the cardinality of the continuum. For $\mathbb C_p$, the lower bound follows from the $p$-adic digit sequences in $\mathbb Q_p$. There are at most continuum many polynomials over $\mathbb Q_p$, each with finitely many roots, so its algebraic closure has cardinality at most continuum. Taking the metric completion adds at most the cardinality of its set of sequences, still continuum.

Over a countable field, a field generated by a transcendence basis of cardinality $\kappa$ and then algebraically extended has cardinality $\max(\aleph_0,\kappa)$. Thus a transcendence basis of $\mathbb C$ over $\overline{\mathbb Q}\cap\mathbb C$ and one of $\mathbb C_p$ over its image under $\lambda$ both have cardinality continuum. A bijection between the bases extends $\lambda$ to the respective rational function fields. Their algebraic closures are $\mathbb C$ and $\mathbb C_p$, so the isomorphism extends to $\iota$ by uniqueness of algebraic closures.

In the type-prime application the rationality of the chosen theta jet gives an expression in $\sigma_0(K)$. The bound on its $\iota$-norm then gives a bound at the originally specified $w$. Choosing $\iota$ for that $w$ is what allows the estimate to apply at each coefficient prime above $p$.
