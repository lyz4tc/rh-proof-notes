## Statement and scope

Let $\Gamma$ be a compact topological group and let $K$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, with a Hausdorff topological-ring structure. Let $K'$ be an algebraically closed extension. Let $\rho:\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}(V)$, $V=K^N$, be continuous in its matrix entries and semisimple in the sense that each invariant subspace has an invariant complement. Let $S\subset\Gamma$ be dense. For every $s\in S$, suppose that a monic polynomial $P_s\in K[t]$ of degree $n$ annihilates $\rho(s)$. Suppose that $\psi:\Gamma\to K^\times$ is a character, continuous as a $K$-valued function, and satisfies
$$
 \psi(s)=(-1)^n P_s(0) \qquad(s\in S).
$$
No continuity of $P_s$ is assumed.

The tangent hypothesis is that $X$ over $K'$ is tangent at $I$ to the matrix Zariski closure of $\rho(\Gamma)$: all derivatives at $I$ of the entry-polynomials over $K$ vanishing on $\rho(\Gamma)$ vanish in direction $X$. There are $n$ specified distinct roots $e_i$ of its characteristic polynomial, with the property that the averages of any two disjoint nonempty subsets of this list are unequal (`IsLac`). The ambient representation need not have dimension $n$.

Then there is a continuous semisimple $r:\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}_n(K)$, an equivariant injection $K^n\hookrightarrow V$, and an equivariant injection $V\hookrightarrow K^n\otimes K^k$ for some finite $k$, where the second factor carries the trivial action. For each $g$, $\operatorname{charpol}(r(g))$ annihilates $\rho(g)$ and $\det r(g)=\psi(g)$. Each $P_s$ annihilates $r(s)$. The set where $\operatorname{charpol}(r(g))$ is separable is open and dense. For $s\in S$, equality $\operatorname{charpol}(r(s))=P_s$ holds if $\operatorname{charpol}(r(s))$ is separable, or if $P_s$ is separable and its roots are product-generic: no different multiset of $n$ roots from its support, with repetitions allowed, has the same product. [Exact statement](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/WeilBound/CoreStatements/PadicHodgeComparison/CocharacterLieDivisors/DivWCoreLacUseType.lean#L90-L122) · [Assembly](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/GoalHolds.lean#L19).

When $N=0$, necessarily $n=0$ from the tangent hypothesis and the assertions reduce to the zero-dimensional representation and the equality $\psi=1$ by density. If $N>0$ then $n>0$: a nonzero constant cannot annihilate an endomorphism of a nonzero space. Below assume $N,n>0$.

## From the degree bound to scalar corners

Let $M$ be the matrix Zariski closure of $\rho(\Gamma)$; we do not require every point of this affine closure to be invertible. The degree bound `Deg n` says that $I,Y,\ldots,Y^n$ are linearly dependent in the matrix space. It is cut out over $K$ by the maximal minors of the matrix with those columns. Thus the bound holds on all of $\rho(\Gamma)$ by continuity and density, and then on $M$ algebraically. [Degree equations and continuity](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/FinDegAll.lean#L5).

First descend the specified tangent point and its eigenvalues from $K'$ to $K$. Regard the entries and the marked roots as one tuple. Put all polynomial equations it satisfies into an ideal over $K$, and take the product of the finitely many nonzero lacunarity tests. That product is not in the radical of the ideal, because evaluating at the tuple sends the ideal to zero but not the product. The Nullstellensatz gives a $K$-point preserving the equations and nonvanishings. In particular the tangent equations, the marked-root determinant equations and lacunarity survive. Write $X$ for the descended point. [Specialization](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/SpecPtPf.lean#L5) · [Tangent and root equations](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/SpecLiePf.lean#L7).

Write $\mathfrak g$ for the tangent space at $I$. For an entry-polynomial $f$ and a matrix $Y$, define the derivation $\mathcal D_Y f$ by $(\mathcal D_Y f)(Z)=df_Z(YZ)$. If $f$ vanishes on a multiplicative matrix monoid $H$ with identity and $Y$ is tangent to it at $I$, then $\mathcal D_Y f$ also vanishes on $H$: for $Z\in H$, differentiate the polynomial $W\mapsto f(WZ)$ at $I$ in direction $Y$. Conversely, preservation of this vanishing ideal implies tangency by evaluation at $I$. The commutator of the two derivations sends a coordinate matrix $Z$ to $(Y'Y-YY')Z$, so the tangent space is bracket-closed; the sign does not matter for closure. Conjugation by $\rho(\Gamma)$ preserves the defining equations and tangency.

In characteristic zero the matrix formal exponential $F_Y(t)=\sum_{j\ge 0}Y^j t^j/j!$ satisfies $F'_Y=YF_Y$. Thus $(f(F_Y))'=(\mathcal D_Yf)(F_Y)$, and by induction the $j$th coefficient of $f(F_Y)$ is $(\mathcal D_Y^jf)(I)/j!$. It vanishes for every $j$ if $f$ is in the vanishing ideal; this is `ExpVan`. No analytic exponential over $K$ is needed. Over $K((t))$ apply the bound to $\exp(tY)$ and make the invertible affine change $Z(t)=(\exp(tY)-I)/t$. All degree minors of $Z(t)$ vanish over $K((t))$, hence over $K[[t]]$, and taking constant terms gives the same bound for $Y$. If $n+1>N^2$, the column dependence is automatic and the empty collection of maximal minors expresses that case. In particular, $\prod_i(t-e_i)$ divides the minimal polynomial of $X$ and accounts for the whole degree bound. It is therefore the minimal polynomial. Its Lagrange projectors $p_i$ are nonzero, mutually orthogonal, sum to $I$, and give $X=\sum_i e_i p_i$ with $X|_{p_iV}=e_i I$. [Formal exponential](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/ExpVanPf.lean#L47) · [Degree at a tangent vector](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/TangentDegree.lean#L45) · [Projectors](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/ProjPf.lean#L21).

There are two further consequences of the same degree bound and the marked weights.

1. For $i\ne j$, the value $e_i-e_j$ identifies the ordered pair $(i,j)$. Indeed, from $e_i+e_l=e_k+e_j$, cancel shared terms. If the pairs were different, the identity would equate the averages of two disjoint sets: either two singletons, a singleton and a two-element set (when one summand was repeated), or two two-element sets. This contradicts lacunarity. On $p_i\operatorname{End}(V)p_j$, the operator $[X,-]$ acts by $e_i-e_j$. Its interpolation projector thus extracts $p_iYp_j$ from any $Y\in\mathfrak g$, keeping it in $\mathfrak g$.
2. If $Y\in\mathfrak g$ commutes with every $p_i$, then $Y$ is scalar on each $p_iV$. To see this, take $s\ne0$ outside the finite set of values where spectra of distinct blocks of $X+sY$ intersect. They are separated since each block has eigenvalues $e_i+s\mu$ with finitely many $\mu$. The minimal polynomial of the direct sum has degree equal to the sum of the block minimal-polynomial degrees, since their spectra are disjoint. Each block is nonzero, and $X+sY\in\mathfrak g$ has minimal-polynomial degree at most $n$. So each block has degree one, which forces scalar action and rules out Jordan blocks. This is a shorter explanation of the scalar conclusion; Lean gets it from the fixed-cycle case of its cycle lemma after an invertible scalar shift.

Now put $\mathcal A=K[\mathfrak g]\subset\operatorname{End}(V)$, the associative algebra generated by $\mathfrak g$. The two properties imply that every corner $p_i\mathcal A p_i$ is $Kp_i$, while $p_i\mathcal A p_j$ lies in $\mathfrak g$ for $i\ne j$. For the generators, subtract their off-diagonal corners to get an element of $\mathfrak g$ commuting with all $p_i$. For multiplication, a typical contribution through block $j$ is $ab$ with $a$ in the $(i,j)$ corner and $b$ in the $(j,k)$ corner. If $i\ne k$ and neither factor is diagonal, $ba=0$, so $ab=[a,b]\in\mathfrak g$. If $i=k\ne j$, $[a,b]$ is diagonal, and its $i$-corner is scalar and equals $ab$. Diagonal factors act as scalars. This proves closure under multiplication, and $p_i\in K[X]\subset\mathcal A$. The algebra is normalized by $\rho(\Gamma)$ and preserves every $\rho$-invariant subspace. For the latter assertion, the linear equations for a matrix to preserve a fixed subspace vanish on $\rho(\Gamma)$, so every tangent vector preserves the subspace as well. [Off-diagonal interpolation](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/GradePf.lean#L45) · [Scalar diagonals](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/DiagScalar2.lean#L59) · [Product of corners](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/AlgStruct2.lean#L52) · [Structure assembly](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/StructPf.lean#L13).

## Moving a coset to a point with exactly $n$ distinct eigenvalues

Write $M_H$ for the matrix Zariski closure of $\rho(H)$ for a normal finite-index subgroup $H\le\Gamma$. The tangent spaces at $I$ of $M$ and $M_H$ coincide, even if $H$ is not open for the topology of $\Gamma$. Here is the derivative argument. Set $G_c=\rho(g_c)$ for a representative of each coset. If $I\notin G_c M_H$, choose an entry-polynomial $q_c$ vanishing on $G_c M_H$ with $q_c(I)\ne0$. If $I\in G_c M_H$, then $G_c^{-1}\in M_H$. The positive order $a$ of $g_c$ in $\Gamma/H$ gives $G_c^a\in\rho(H)$ and hence $G_c=G_c^a(G_c^{-1})^{a-1}\in M_H$. Thus $G_c M_H\subset M_H$, and take $q_c=1$. For each $f$ vanishing on $M_H$, the product $f\prod_c q_c$ vanishes on $M$, and its derivative at $I$ is $df_I\prod_c q_c(I)$ because $f(I)=0$. The product of the values $q_c(I)$ is nonzero, so tangency to $M$ implies tangency to $M_H$; the reverse is immediate. [Finite-index tangent lemma](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/TangentIndexDvd.lean#L32-L111).

Fix $g\in\Gamma$ and write $G=\rho(g)$ and $q_i=G^{-1}p_iG$. The algebra $\mathcal A$ has zero Jacobson radical: the radical is nilpotent and normalized by $\rho(\Gamma)$; its image $JV$ has a $\Gamma$-invariant complement, also stable under $\mathcal A$. The radical therefore kills that complement, hence $JV=J^2V=\cdots=0$, and faithfulness gives $J=0$. Thus it is a product of matrix algebras over $K$. Each $p_i$, and each $q_i$, corresponds to a rank-one idempotent in one simple block since its corner is $K$. In each simple block choose a basis for the decomposition by the $p_i$ and a basis for the decomposition by the $q_i$. Match these bases, requiring index $i$ to be matched to itself if $q_i\mathcal A p_i\ne0$ (precisely when the two belong to the same block). Conjugation by the resulting $v\in\mathcal A^\times$ maps $p_i$ to $q_{\pi(i)}$ for a permutation $\pi$ with this fixing property. [Radical argument](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/SemisPf.lean#L51) · [Idempotent conjugacy with fixed indices](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/ConjugateIdempotents.lean#L163).

In these matrix blocks, Gaussian elimination factors $v=uD$ with $D=\sum_i d_i p_i$, $d_i\ne0$, and $u$ a product of elementary transvections $I+Z$ with $Z\in p_i\mathcal A p_j$, $i\ne j$. Indeed diagonal units and elementary transvections generate each full matrix block, and diagonals normalize the transvections, so they can be moved to the right. Since $Z\in\mathfrak g$ and $Z^2=0$, $\exp(tZ)=I+tZ$ belongs to the defining polynomial zero set of $M_H$, coefficientwise, and evaluating this polynomial identity at $t=1$ gives $I+Z\in M_H$. The closure is closed under matrix multiplication (translate the defining equations one variable at a time), so $u\in M_H$. Now $x=Gu$ permutes the $p_iV$ by $\pi$. [Unit factorization](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/UnitFactorizationTheorem.lean#L362) · [Transvections in the closure](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/SumMulSum.lean#L75).

We need a block-diagonal $D_\tau=\sum_i\tau_i p_i$ in $M_H$ so that different cycles of $\pi$ give disjoint spectra for $y=xD_\tau$. On a cycle $O$ of length $a$, the $a$-step return of $y$ is the return of $x$ scaled by $\tau_O=\prod_{i\in O}\tau_i$. If another cycle $O'$ of length $b$ shares an eigenvalue $\lambda$ with this one, then $\lambda^a=\tau_O\beta$ and $\lambda^b=\tau_{O'}\beta'$ for nonzero eigenvalues $\beta,\beta'$ of the respective returns. Hence
$$
     \tau_O^b=(\beta'^a/\beta^b)\tau_{O'}^a .
$$
For all pairs of cycles, only finitely many nonzero ratios must be avoided. Use $\tau_i(t)=\exp(te_i)$ in formal power series. The associated diagonal curve is $\exp(tX)$, so it satisfies the polynomial equations of $M_H$. Each forbidden relation fails for this formal tuple: if its two terms agreed, the constant term would force the ratio to be $1$, and the linear term would contradict the unequal averages of the two disjoint cycles. Each coordinate is also nonzero. Specialize by the same Nullstellensatz argument to obtain scalars $\tau_i\in K^\times$ satisfying the closure equations and avoiding all collisions. One does not replace a $K$-point by a transcendental or analytically convergent exponential. [Spectral collision calculation](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/GenSpecProof.lean#L102) · [Torus point by specialization](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/TorusPtPf.lean#L66).

On a cycle of length $a$, the return on any one block is invertible and has an eigenvector $w$ of eigenvalue $\beta_0\ne0$. For each of the $a$ distinct roots $\lambda$ of $t^a-\beta_0$, the vector $\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}\lambda^{-j} y^j w$ is nonzero (the summands are in distinct blocks) and is a $y$-eigenvector of eigenvalue $\lambda$. Across cycles these roots cannot collide. Thus $y$ has at least $n$ distinct eigenvalues. It belongs to $G M_H\subset M$, so the degree bound forces exactly those roots and a squarefree minimal polynomial. In a fixed block of $\pi$ there is just one eigenvalue and no Jordan part, so $y$ acts on it as a scalar. [Cycle count](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/CycleCount2.lean#L70) · [The two conclusions of the key lemma](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/SumMulSum.lean#L90).

This has a second use, beyond finding one good point in each coset. If some $a\in\mathcal A$ has $p_i aG p_i\ne0$, multiplication on the left by $G^{-1}$ shows $q_i\mathcal A p_i\ne0$, using normalization. Thus $\pi(i)=i$, and $p_i y=c_i p_i$. It follows that $p_iG=p_i w_i$ for $w_i=c_i(uD_\tau)^{-1}\in\mathcal A$. Let $\mathcal R$ be the span of all $aG$, $a\in\mathcal A$, $G\in\rho(\Gamma)$. It is an algebra by normalization. For a generator, the diagonal corner is zero or
$$
 p_i aG p_i=p_i G(G^{-1}aG)p_i=p_i w_i(G^{-1}aG)p_i\in Kp_i.
$$
So all diagonal corners remain scalar even after adding the full group image. This step matters because the tangent algebra alone need not contain every $\rho(g)$. [Enlarging the algebra](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/BigCorner2.lean#L48).

## Selecting and identifying the rank-$n$ representation

The invariant subspaces for $\mathcal R$ are exactly the $\rho$-invariant subspaces, since $\mathcal R$ contains $\rho(\Gamma)$ and preserves such spaces. Thus $V$ is a faithful semisimple $\mathcal R$-module, and $\mathcal R$ has zero radical. Its matrix units for the corner decomposition identify
$$
 \mathcal R\simeq\prod_b M_{m_b}(K),\qquad \sum_b m_b=n .
$$
Indeed each $p_i$ is primitive and the $p_i$ sum to $I$. There are $m_b$ of them in simple block $b$. Let $U$ be the direct sum of one standard module of each such block, and let $r$ be the resulting action on $U$ of the matrices $\rho(g)$. Then $\dim U=n$. Every block occurs in $V$ by faithfulness. Choosing one copy in $V$ of every standard module gives an intertwining split injection $A:U\to V$ with left inverse $B_0$. Taking sufficiently many copies of $U$ gives an equivariant split injection $V\to U^{\oplus k}$. This describes the same matrix-unit construction in module language: Lean constructs the action coefficients from $p_i a p_j$ in `Rep_of`, proves the images of the intertwiners span, and constructs the second injection from a finite identity decomposition $\sum_j T'_j T_j=I$ in `FinEmbed_of`. The stack $Bv=(T_jv)_j$ satisfies $(r(g)\otimes I_k)B=B\rho(g)$, with left inverse $(u_j)_j\mapsto\sum_j T'_ju_j$. In particular it has rank $N$, whereas $A$ has rank $n$. [Matrix units](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/MatUnitsOf.lean#L117) · [The rank-$n$ action](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/MinCongr.lean#L180) · [Finite splitting](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/FinEmbedPf.lean#L155) · [Stack and rank calculation](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/Imports.lean#L6).

As $r(g)=B_0\rho(g)A$, it is continuous. It is semisimple because it is a subrepresentation of the semisimple $\rho$; the two embeddings show that $\rho(g)$ and $r(g)$ have the same annihilating polynomials. For the density argument, at the point $y=Gm$ found in $G M_H$, compression $\phi(z)=B_0 GzA$ extends the evaluation $r(gh)$ from $z=\rho(h)$. For every intertwiner $T:U\to V$, the equation $(Gz)T=T\phi(z)$ holds on $\rho(H)$ and so on $M_H$ by its polynomial entries. Its images span $V$, so the characteristic polynomial of $\phi(m)$ annihilates $Gm$. It has degree $n$ and must have all $n$ distinct roots of $Gm$; thus its discriminant is nonzero. The discriminant of $\phi(z)$ is a polynomial in $z$, so it cannot vanish on all of $\rho(H)$. This shows there is some $h\in H$ with $r(gh)$ having separable characteristic polynomial. The separable locus in $\Gamma$ is open by continuity. If its complement contained a nonempty ordinary-open set $O$, compactness would give a finite cover of $\Gamma$ by inverse left translates of $O$. Give $\Gamma$ instead the auxiliary Zariski topology induced by the entries of $r$; this need not be its original topology. It is Noetherian as a subspace of the affine matrix Zariski space, left translations are homeomorphisms, and the discriminant-zero set $D$ is closed. The same finite translates of $D$ now form a closed cover. An irreducible component $X$ containing the identity is contained in one of them, say $g^{-1}D$. Left translation permutes a finite set of irreducible components, so the subgroup $H$ fixing all of them is normal of finite index. For $h\in H$, $h\in hX=X$, since $1\in X$. Thus $gH\subseteq D$, a contradiction. This is the compact-group argument without any profiniteness or openness assumption on $H$. [Compression on the closure](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/DenseAlg2.lean#L48) · [Open-dense step](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/EtaleCohomology/DivisorCycles/FinDensePf.lean#L19) · [Compact coset lemma](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/IntegralCasts/CapelliIdentities/CosetOfCover.lean#L52-L149).

Finally, on the intersection of $S$ with this open dense locus every root of the separable degree-$n$ characteristic polynomial of $r(s)$ is a root of $P_s$. Hence the polynomials coincide there, and $\det r(s)=(-1)^n P_s(0)=\psi(s)$. That intersection is dense; continuity gives the determinant identity on all $\Gamma$. At an individual $s$ with product-generic $P_s$, the $n$-element characteristic-root multiset of $r(s)$ is drawn from the roots of $P_s$ and has product $\det r(s)=(-1)^nP_s(0)$. Product-genericity forces equality even without previously knowing separability of $r(s)$. This is where the determinant hypothesis is used; the rank construction itself does not use it. ## What remains to check in a full exposition

The rank-selection argument above ends at the output of `DivWCoreLac`. The proof is in ordinary linear and algebraic language; it uses the Nullstellensatz and finite-dimensional semisimple-algebra structure rather than reproducing every low-level specialization and matrix-unit lemma. The Hodge cocharacter, construction of geometric models, local–global compatibility and subsequent transfer to other representations belong to different chapters.
