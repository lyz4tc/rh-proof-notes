# Finite projections determine the adelic norm-orbit measure

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E/F$ be a quadratic CM extension, $W$ a nondegenerate Hermitian $E$-space of rank $n\ge3$, and $Q(x)=h(x,x)$. Fix $\beta\in F^\times$ and rational coordinates on $W$ as a vector space over $F$. Let $L_v$ be the corresponding coordinate lattice at each finite place. Write $X_v=\{Q=\beta\}\subset W_v$ at each place, and $X_\infty$ for the product of these sets at the real places. On them take the continuous coordinate fibre measures $\omega_v,\omega_\infty$, using additive measures of mass one on the coordinate lattices at finite places and of covolume one in the coordinates of $F_\infty$ at infinity. Let $I$ be a locally finite Borel measure on $W(\mathbb A_F)$, supported on the locus $Q=\beta$, invariant under $U(W)(\mathbb A_F)$.

**Claim.** There are $c\ge0$ and a finite set $S_0$ of finite places such that, for every finite $S\supseteq S_0$ and nonnegative Borel tests $\varphi_\infty,\varphi_v$ for $v\in S$, the following holds:
$$
 \int \varphi_\infty(y_\infty)
       \prod_{v\in S}\varphi_v(y_v)\prod_{v\notin S}\mathbf1_{L_v}(y_v)\,dI(y)
 =c\left(\int\varphi_\infty\,d\omega_\infty\right)
      \prod_{v\in S}\left(\int\varphi_v\,d\omega_v\right)
      \prod_{v\notin S}\omega_v(L_v).
$$
The tail product converges absolutely. If any local fibre is empty, the asserted integral is zero. This is the rank-one orbital measure result needed to compare the factors in [the normalized one-vector mean](a-dilation-fixes-the-scalar-in-the-one-vector-siegel-mean.md).

The equation $Q=\beta$ on the adeles is the system of its equations at every place because the coordinate projections commute with $Q$ and jointly determine an adelic vector. A missing local solution therefore forces $I=0$. Assume every local fibre is nonempty. Each is smooth because $dQ_x(x)=2\beta$, and its measure gives positive mass to every nonempty relatively open subset. An isometry of $W_v$ preserves additive Haar measure. For example the trace pairing is preserved, so the square of its determinant over $F_v$ is one, with absolute value one. The map preserves $Q$ as well. It therefore preserves $\omega_v$ by continuity and uniqueness of disintegration. The corresponding statement holds for the real product.

At each place the unitary group acts transitively on $X_v$, by extending the isometry between the lines of any two vectors of norm $\beta$. At a split place this also follows directly from the description $(x,\ell)$ with $\ell(x)=\beta$: choose an isomorphism sending the given line to the new line with the prescribed vector and sending $\ker\ell$ to the new kernel. Here the local orbit map is open and induces a homeomorphism from the quotient by the stabilizer. One proof is to check that its differential is onto the tangent $dQ_x^{-1}(0)$ at $x$. With the form linear in the second variable, write a tangent vector as $y=a x+z$ with $z\perp x$ and $a+\bar a=0$. The diagonal skew-Hermitian map $a$ on $Ex$, together with the skew-Hermitian off-diagonal pair sending $x$ to $z$ and $w\in x^\perp$ to $-h(z,w)x/\beta$, has image $y$ at $x$. The same calculation works in the split algebra. The local inverse-function theorem applied in coordinates of the analytic unitary group gives openness. Thus an invariant locally finite measure on $X_v$ is unique up to scalar by the Haar quotient formula. For a finite product of such spaces and groups the same conclusion holds, with the product measure as one nonzero reference. The source carries this out as a uniqueness assertion on each projected orbit.

For a given $S$, restrict $I$ by the condition $y_v\in L_v$ for all finite $v\notin S$, and project to $W_\infty\times\prod_{v\in S}W_v$. The resulting measure $I_S$ is locally finite. Indeed, a compact set in the displayed finite product has compact inverse image under this projection with the integral tails imposed, since $\prod_{v\notin S}L_v$ is compact in the restricted product. A local group element in the finite product, extended by the identity at other places, is adelic and preserves this integral-tail condition. Hence $I_S$ is invariant under the product group and is supported on $X_\infty\times\prod_{v\in S}X_v$. It follows that $I_S=c_S\,\omega_\infty\prod_{v\in S}\omega_v$ for some $c_S\ge0$.

Put $l_v=\omega_v(L_v)$, finite by compactness. For $v\notin S$, the same integral can be read using $I_{S\cup\{v\}}$ if the added test is $\mathbf1_{L_v}$. Test the other factors on sets of finite positive fibre measure and cancel their product to get $c_S=c_{S\cup\{v\}}l_v$. By the [standard-place estimate](primitive-residue-vectors-control-all-nonzero-integral-norm-densities.md) and integrality of $\beta$ away from finitely many places, $\sum_v|l_v-1|<\infty$, and the zeros among $l_v$ lie in a finite set. Choose $S_0$ containing those zeros. The product $P_0=\prod_{v\notin S_0}l_v$ is strictly positive by the logarithm criterion for infinite products. Set $c=c_{S_0}/P_0$. Iterating the consistency equality over the finite difference $S\setminus S_0$ gives $c_S=c\prod_{v\notin S}l_v$. The result follows by evaluating $I_S$ on the indicated tests. This shows exactly how the finite projection constants fit together even when a few unscaled coordinate lattices have zero mass.
