# A residual scalar away from one kills local cohomology

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $F_v$ be a nonarchimedean local field of residue cardinality $q$ and residue characteristic different from $\ell$. Let $\phi$ be an arithmetic Frobenius lift. Let $O\to\Lambda$ be a local map of local rings of residue characteristic $\ell$, with $T:G_{F_v}\to\operatorname{GL}_r(O)$ continuous for the adic topology. Suppose $q\equiv1\pmod\ell$, and $T(\phi)$ reduces to $cI$ with $c\ne1$. Multiply $T$ by a scalar character $\kappa$ of open kernel with values of $\ell$-power order. For any discrete $\Lambda$-module $B$ killed elementwise by powers of the maximal ideal, let $A=B^r$ carry the resulting continuous local action.

**Proposition.** The groups $H^0(F_v,A)$, $H^1(F_v,A)$ and $H^2(F_v,A)$ vanish. The same conclusion applies to the cyclotomic dual of $T$ with a twist of the same kind.

**Proof.** The reduction of $\kappa(\phi)$ is $1$. Indeed in a field of characteristic $\ell$ an $\ell^a$-th root of unity satisfies $(x-1)^{\ell^a}=0$. Thus both matrices $\kappa(\phi)T(\phi)-I$ and $\kappa(\phi)T(\phi)-qI$ reduce over $\Lambda$ to the nonzero scalar $c-1$ times the identity. Their determinants are units, so both matrices are invertible over $\Lambda$. In particular neither difference has a nonzero kernel on $A$. The first kernel condition gives $H^0(F_v,A)=0$.

First take any finite stable additive subgroup $A_0\subseteq A$ killed by a power of $\ell$. Both Frobenius differences remain injective on $A_0$, and thus bijective by finiteness. If $f:A_0\to\mu_{\ell^n}$ is an invariant in the cyclotomic dual with $\ell^n A_0=0$, its Frobenius relation is $f(\phi x)=qf(x)$ in additive coordinates. Such an $f$ vanishes on $(\phi-q)A_0=A_0$. Local Tate duality therefore gives $H^2(F_v,A_0)=0$. Also $H^0(F_v,A_0)=0$; the local Euler characteristic away from $\ell$ gives $\#H^1=\#H^0\,\#H^2=1$. Both the duality and Euler formula are in [Milne, *Arithmetic Duality Theorems*, Corollary I.2.3 and Theorem I.2.8](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/ADTnot.pdf).

For arbitrary $A$, a continuous cocycle in either degree one or two has finite image by compactness. The orbit of each value is likewise finite. The additive subgroup generated by the union of these finitely many orbits is finite, stable, and killed by one power of $\ell$, since $\ell$ belongs to the maximal ideal of $\Lambda$. The cocycle takes values in that subgroup. The finite case makes it a coboundary there and hence in $A$.

For the cyclotomic dual, the residual scalar of untwisted Frobenius is $\bar q\,c^{-1}=c^{-1}\ne1$, from the inverse transpose and the reduction of the cyclotomic character. The same argument therefore applies. In the auxiliary-prime construction a first-block Frobenius is conjugate modulo the residual-trivial subgroup to the designated scalar inertia element. At its conjugate prime the conjugate-dual identity gives the inverse scalar, using the norm congruence and the cyclotomic factor. These are precisely the first-block scalar hypotheses in the proposition.
