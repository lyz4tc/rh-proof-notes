# Unramified Selmer classes descend to one finite set of places

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $F$ be a number field, $\ell$ a rational prime, and $\Sigma$ a finite set of finite places of $F$. Set $S=\Sigma\cup\{v:v\mid\ell\}$. Write $G_F$ for the absolute Galois group and $G_{F,S}$ for the quotient by the closed normal subgroup $N_S$ generated by inertia at the places outside $S$. Choose one decomposition group and inertia subgroup at each finite place. On a discrete continuous $G_F$-module $D$ unramified outside $S$, impose any specified local condition $L_v(D)\subseteq H^1(F_v,D)$ at $v\mid\ell$, allow all classes at the remaining places in $S$, and require the class to restrict to zero on inertia outside $S$.

**Proposition.** Inflation identifies this Selmer group with the classes in $H^1(G_{F,S},D)$ whose localizations lie in $L_v(D)$ at $v\mid\ell$. In particular, for a short exact sequence $0\to A\to B\to C\to0$ of such modules, the lifting quotient can be computed on $G_{F,S}$. If $F$ is totally imaginary, $A$ is finite and killed by $\ell$, its global invariants and cyclotomic-dual global invariants vanish, and the local conditions satisfy

$$
 u_*L_v(A)\subseteq L_v(B),\qquad w_*L_v(B)=L_v(C),\qquad
 \prod_{v\mid\ell}\#L_v(A)=
 \left(\prod_{v\mid\ell}\#H^0(F_v,A)\right)(\#A)^{r_2(F)},
$$

then the lifting quotient is finite of cardinality at most $\#\operatorname{Sel}(A)$. Here $u,w$ are the maps of the short exact sequence.

**Proof.** For an unramified module the action kernel is closed, since it is the intersection of the stabilizers of its elements. It contains all the inertia groups outside $S$, so contains $N_S$; this gives the action of $G_{F,S}$. An inflated cocycle vanishes on $N_S$, and inflation is injective on cohomology: if a cocycle becomes a coboundary after composing with the quotient map, surjectivity of that map gives the same coboundary on the quotient.

Conversely, let $c:G_F\to D$ be a continuous cocycle in the Selmer group. At a chosen inertia group outside $S$, its restriction is a coboundary. Since that inertia group acts trivially, the coboundary is zero. On $N_S$ the cocycle identity is the homomorphism identity, as the action there is trivial. Its zero subgroup within $N_S$ is closed. It is also normal in $G_F$, since for $n\in N_S$ the cocycle identity gives $c(gng^{-1})=g\,c(n)$. Thus vanishing on the chosen inertia groups implies vanishing on their conjugates and the closed normal subgroup they generate. Now $c(gn)=c(g)$ for $n\in N_S$, so $c$ descends to a continuous cocycle on $G_{F,S}$ by the quotient topology. The local conditions at $v\mid\ell$ refer to the same class on the chosen decomposition group, and all other allowed places are unrestricted. This proves the identification, and the construction commutes with the coefficient maps.

On this quotient the [two-obstruction bound](two-obstructions-measure-the-failure-to-lift-selmer-classes.md) applies with the places over $\ell$ as the local tests. The upper bound is $\# S^2(A)\,\# Q^1(A)$ in the notation of that proof. The [residual Euler count](euler-characteristics-count-the-residual-selmer-obstructions.md) identifies the product with the finite cardinality of the residual Selmer group under the stated hypotheses. Inflation carries the resulting bound back to the absolute group.

**Lean correspondence.** The descent of the action is `exists_descent` and the descent of a Selmer cocycle is `selAllLeInf`, using the closed normal subgroup argument in `vanish`. `inf_injective` and `inf_mem_iff` establish the comparison of local conditions. `obstruction_le` combines it with the obstruction and cardinality arguments.
