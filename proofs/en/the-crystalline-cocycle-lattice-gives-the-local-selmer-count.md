# The crystalline cocycle lattice gives the local Selmer count

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $O$ be a coefficient discrete valuation ring with fraction field $E$, uniformizer $\pi$ and finite residue field $k$. Let $T=O^r$ be a stable Galois lattice at a place $v\mid\ell$, $V=T\otimes_O E$, and suppose $H^0(F_v,V)=0$. Let $W$ be the $E$-space of continuous cocycles whose class is in $H^1_f(F_v,V)$. Here the finite condition is the Bloch–Kato condition that the class becomes zero with crystalline period coefficients. Write $b_v=\dim_E H^1_f(F_v,V)$. Assume $H^1_f(F_v,V)$ is finite-dimensional. The integral cocycles are $Z=W\cap\operatorname{Map}(G_{F_v},O^r)$. For a finite coefficient module $B$ on which $O$ acts through $k$, the residual finite condition $L_v(B)$ is the space of classes represented by sums of cocycles obtained from elements of $Z$ by reduction and multiplication by elements of $B$.

**Proposition.** For $A=B^r$ with the reduced Galois action, one has $\#L_v(B)=\#H^0(F_v,A)\,(\#B)^{b_v}$.

**Proof.** The space $W$ contains the rational coboundaries. Their dimension is $r$ because the kernel of the coboundary map is $H^0(F_v,V)=0$. The quotient by them is $H^1_f(F_v,V)$, so $\dim_E W=r+b_v$.

The lattice $Z$ is finite free of that rank. To see finite generation, choose finitely many coordinate evaluations that separate $W$. One can choose them successively: as long as the common kernel is nonzero, some remaining coordinate is nonzero on a vector in it and lowers its dimension. Evaluation embeds $Z$ in a finite power of $O$, and submodules of that finite free module are finite free over a discrete valuation ring. Each cocycle in $W$ has bounded denominators because its domain is compact and its image in $E^r$ is continuous. Multiplying by a sufficiently high power of $\pi$ brings it into $Z$. Thus $Z$ spans $W$. Moreover $Z$ is saturated in the module of all integral functions: if a nonzero scalar multiple belongs to $W$, the original function belongs to $W$ by $E$-linearity. It follows that coefficientwise reduction gives an injection $Z/\pi Z\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Map}(G_{F_v},k^r)$.

For $B$ killed by $\pi$, tensor this reduction with the finite-dimensional $k$-vector space $B$. Its image consists exactly of the reduced cocycles used to define $L_v(B)$. Injectivity persists: after choosing a basis of $B$, every relation is a relation among the reduced functions in each basis component, so every component vanishes separately. The image has size $(\#B)^{r+b_v}$.

The coboundaries of $A$ are in this image, since the integral coboundaries of the coordinate vectors of $T$ reduce to generators for those of $A$. Their size is $(\#B)^r/\#H^0(F_v,A)$, by counting the kernel of $A\to Z^1(F_v,A)$. Dividing the size of the reduced cocycle space by this size gives the proposition.
