# Removing auxiliary primes factors the Hecke determinant

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Work at a fixed residual prime $\ell$, a fixed layer and side, and with one of the untwisted lattices in the Hecke comparison. Let $O$ be the coefficient discrete valuation ring with algebraically closed residue field $k$, let $K$ be its fraction field, and let $L$ be the $O$-lattice with basis $b_1,\ldots,b_d$ in the relevant finite-dimensional form space. The projected untwisted theta forms $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_d$ are linearly independent and span the same $K$-space. Choose a fixed nonzero scalar $x=\ell^c$ as in the integral image bound. For each allowed conjugation-stable auxiliary support $Q$, the images, under the projection, of $x$ times the group-ring-valued theta forms lie in $L$ tensored with the auxiliary group ring. All Hecke operators used here preserve $L$ and commute with the projection and with one another. Write $T_m(P)$ for their matrices on $L$, $1\le m\le r$, where $r$ is the rank parameter, and define

$$
B_P(z)=I_d+\sum_{m=1}^{r}(-1)^m
  (\mathrm NP)^{m(m-1)/2}z^m T_m(P).
$$

The Hecke relation for removal of a prime pair is an input. It prescribes one prime $P$ of the pair and a sign common to this layer and side. If the character on the larger support is inflated from the smaller one, then the specialized theta form at the larger support is obtained by applying this Hecke polynomial at the signed Frobenius value $z=\chi(\operatorname{Frob}_P)^{\pm1}$ to the form at the smaller support. The empty support gives the untwisted forms. At the prescribed prime in each first-block pair, assume that the scalar polynomial at $z=1$ is nonzero in $k$ on every simultaneous residual Hecke eigensystem of $L/\varpi L$. In the application this follows from the [first-block Frobenius test and the Galois comparison](tame-inertia-makes-the-first-block-euler-factors-units.md).

Put $n=\ell^M$ with $M\ge1$. The selected positions consist of two blocks of size $R$. Call the full support $Q$ and the first block support $Q_1$. With the last block of root coordinates trivial, the given auxiliary coordinate relations make the signed Frobenius value at the $j$-th second-block pair equal to $u_j^{\epsilon_j}$, where $u_j$ is the $j$-th first-block root and $\epsilon_j$ is $1$ or $n-1$. The inversion occurs when the sign or the conjugate orientation inverts the character. Both exponents have nonzero residue in $k$.

**Proposition.** There are integral polynomials $f(X,Y)$ and $g(X)$ obtained from the coordinate determinants at the two supports, and

$$
 D(X)=\prod_{j=1}^R\det B_{P_{R+j}}\bigl((1+X_j)^{\epsilon_j}\bigr),
$$

such that $f-Dg$ belongs to the ideal generated by the $Y_j$ and the $(1+X_j)^n-1$. The reduction of $D$ is a monomial $\prod_j X_j^{a_j}$ times a polynomial with nonzero constant term, where $0\le a_j\le rd$. Moreover $v_O(g(0))=v_O(\det M_0)$, where $M_0$ has columns the coordinates of $x\theta_i$ in the basis $b_j$.

For an integral coefficient extension $O\to O'$ with normalized valuation $v_\ell(\ell)=1$, let $m$ be the specialized coordinate matrix for the full support, and let $T$ be any full $O'$-lattice containing all the projected specialized forms $w_i$. If $\det m\ne0$, then the signed index of $T$ relative to the base-changed $L$ is at most the untwisted index plus $v_\ell(\det m)-v_\ell(\det M_0)$. Here the signed index is the valuation of a basis change from the reference lattice to the comparison lattice. In particular, for an inclusion of the comparison lattice in the reference lattice it is the normalized length of the quotient.

**Proof.** The integral image bound gives coordinates of the cleared forms over the auxiliary group ring. For each group-ring basis element, represent its coefficient in the character evaluation by a product of powers of the cyclic generators in the chosen positions. Lift such a product to the same powers of $1+Z_j$. Missing positions have exponent zero. At a tuple of roots, evaluation at $Z_j=u_j-1$ recovers the corresponding specialized matrix. If a coordinate is trivial, evaluation sets its variable to zero, so the first-support matrix can use the first-block variables alone. Take $f,g$ to be the respective matrix determinants. At empty support the coordinates are $M_0$, and its determinant is nonzero because $x\ne0$ and the $\theta_i$ are independent.

Consider the comparison with a character inflated from the first block. When one removes a pair in the second block, the Hecke relation and commutation with the projection give the full coordinate matrix as $B_P(z)$ times the coordinate matrix for the smaller support. The coefficient extension is injective and flat over the valuation ring, so independence of the coordinate basis is preserved and the matrix equality follows from the equality of its columns. Apply this at each pair in turn. All the specialized character values at the second-block positions are trivial throughout, so the Frobenius value at each removed pair stays the signed value prescribed by the first block. Taking determinants makes the resulting factors scalar and gives $f(X,0)=D(X)g(X)$ at every root tuple. The [monic division argument on the root grid](removing-auxiliary-primes-gives-the-toric-polynomial-factor.md) proves the desired ideal membership.

At the trivial character, compare the first block to empty support by the same removal identity. The result is $g(0)=\det M_0\prod_{P\ {\rm in\ the\ first\ block}}\det B_P(1)$, with one prime per pair selected by the Hecke relation. Each factor is a unit. Indeed, if its reduction were singular, the nonzero kernel would be invariant under the commuting residual Hecke operators. They have a common eigenvector on this kernel. To see this even for a large family, choose a nonzero common invariant subspace of minimal dimension inside the kernel. Each operator has an eigenvalue there because the residue field is algebraically closed, and each corresponding eigenspace is invariant under the whole family. Minimality forces every operator to act by a scalar on the subspace. On a common eigenvector, $\bar B_P(1)$ acts by the nonzero scalar in the residual hypothesis, a contradiction. Thus the first-block factors do not change the constant-term valuation.

For a second-block prime set $p_P(z)=\det\bar B_P(z)\in k[z]$. Its value at zero is $1$, and its degree is at most $rd$ by the determinant expansion. Factor $p_P(z)=(z-1)^{a_j}U_P(z)$ with $U_P(1)\ne0$. The difference $(1+X_j)^{\epsilon_j}-1$ has linear coefficient $\epsilon_j\ne0$ in $k$. Substituting this power into $p_P$ therefore gives $X_j^{a_j}$ times a polynomial with nonzero constant coefficient. Multiplying yields the prescribed monomial factor.

Finally, the $w_i$ are a basis of their span $\langle w_i\rangle_{O'}$, since $m$ is nonsingular. Their coordinate determinant relative to $L\otimes O'$ is $x^{-d}\det m$. The same identity for the untwisted span gives $x^{-d}\det M_0$. Normalization by $\ell$ makes the valuation of the untwisted determinant unchanged by extension. Since the span of the $w_i$ lies in $T$, additivity of the signed index and the nonnegative quotient length give the asserted upper bound. This is the Hecke contribution to the [common twist](a-single-ray-character-satisfies-all-the-exported-twist-bounds.md).
