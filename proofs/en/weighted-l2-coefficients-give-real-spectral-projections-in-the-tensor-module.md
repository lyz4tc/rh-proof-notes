# Weighted L2 coefficients give real spectral projections in the tensor module

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $G=U(p,q)$ with $p,q\ge1$, $N=p+q$, and $K=U(p)\times U(q)$. Let $\sigma$ be an irreducible square-integrable unitary representation with a dense $(\mathfrak{gl}_N,K)$-module $M$ generated by a finite-dimensional compact type $V$. Assume $M$ is admissible, meaning each compact type has finite multiplicity. Assume the weighted integrability condition
$$
 \|g\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\langle y,\sigma(g)x\rangle\in L^2(G)
 \quad\text{for every }x,y\in M ,
\tag{1}
$$
where $g$ is the defining matrix and the norm is its Hilbert–Schmidt norm. This is the condition used on the source's model. Take $F=\mathbb C^N$ with either the defining action $F(g)=g$ or the contragredient action $F(g)=(g^{-1})^{\mathsf T}$, and write $M^N=F\otimes M$. The infinitesimal tensor action is $1\otimes d\sigma+dF\otimes1$. If $E_{ij}=d\sigma(e_{ij})$, define $A$ on $M^N$ by
$$
 (Af)_j=\sum_i E_{ij}f_i\quad\text{in the defining case},\qquad
 (Af)_i=-\sum_j E_{ij}f_j\quad\text{in the dual case}.
$$
Assume the Casimir acts by a scalar on $M$, as it does on the large-gap model. Then $A$ commutes with the tensor action and has a finite real spectral decomposition on $M^N$. This is an algebraic direct sum even when $M$ is infinite dimensional. ## Embed in a positive Hilbert space

Let $J$ be the diagonal sign matrix. Both defining actions preserve $J$, and satisfy $\|F(g)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\|g\|_{\mathrm{HS}}$, using $g^{-1}=Jg^*J$. For $y\ne0$ in $M$, put
$$
 (C_y f)(g)=J F(g)\bigl(\langle y,\sigma(g)f_j\rangle\bigr)_{j=0}^{N-1}.
$$
Condition (1) makes this a vector-valued $L^2$ function for every $f\in M^N$. The $J$ in front is a constant coordinate change. The signed form on $M^N$ is $B(f,f')=\sum_i J_{ii}\langle f_i,f'_i\rangle$. If the degree of $\sigma$ for the chosen Haar measure is $d>0$, then
$$
 \langle C_y f,J C_y f'\rangle_{L^2}
       =\frac{\|y\|^2}{d}B(f,f').
\tag{2}
$$
Indeed $F(g)^*JF(g)=J$ removes the finite-dimensional factor pointwise. Integrate the remaining sum with Schur orthogonality. Because $B$ is nondegenerate on $M^N$, (2) makes $C_y$ injective. Pulling back the ordinary $L^2$ inner product yields a positive inner product. The source builds such a positive form with a finite collection of test vectors from $V$. Formula (2) also allows one test vector after the square-integrable closure has been identified. [Signed Schur identity](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/ReciprocityTheory/ReductiveGroups/DiscreteSeries/TensorRepresentations/TraceFormulaD.lean#L30) · [Form from square-integrable coefficients](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/ReciprocityTheory/ReductiveGroups/DiscreteSeries/TensorRepresentations/Unitarize.lean#L47)

Right translation on these functions intertwines the tensor action. At the Lie algebra level this follows by differentiating $C_y f(g\exp tX)$. The derivative is the right translate of the function for $(1\otimes d\sigma(X)+dF(X)\otimes1)f$, which is again in $L^2$ by (1). Integrating along the one-parameter subgroup shows that this is also the derivative in $L^2$, since right translations are strongly continuous there. Thus the real infinitesimal actions are skew-adjoint as identities on the algebraic domain, and the complexified matrix units obey the signed adjoint rule for the positive form. Compact group covariance follows directly from the same multiplication law. ## Find a finite-dimensional seed for the projections

Expanding the quadratic Casimir of the tensor action gives the old Casimir acting on each coordinate, plus $N$ times the identity, plus $2A$. The first term is scalar on $M^N$. The tensor Casimir commutes with the tensor Lie and compact actions, so the same is true of $A$. It is symmetric for the positive form, since the signed adjoint rule reverses the two matrix units in each Casimir pairing. The Casimir expansion is the same for the dual action, with the sign already included in the definition of $A$. [Expand the tensor Casimir](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/ReciprocityTheory/ReductiveGroups/DiscreteSeries/TensorRepresentations/TensorV.lean#L76) · [Casimir identity in both cases](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/ReciprocityTheory/ReductiveGroups/DiscreteSeries/TensorRepresentations/DecompC.lean#L292)

The space $F\otimes V$ is a finite-dimensional compact submodule. Its $A$-iterates stay in the direct sum of its finitely many compact isotypic spaces. Each such isotypic space in $F\otimes M$ is finite dimensional. Indeed for a fixed compact type $S$, its multiplicity there is the dimension of $\operatorname{Hom}_K(F^*\otimes S,M)$, finite by admissibility and the finite decomposition of $F^*\otimes S$. Thus the span of the $A$-iterates is a finite-dimensional invariant seed. In Lean this finiteness comes from bounding the number of noncompact generators needed for its compact highest weights. [Finite-dimensional seed containing the lowest compact layer](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/ReciprocityTheory/ReductiveGroups/DiscreteSeries/TensorRepresentations/DecompC.lean#L207)

Diagonalize the symmetric operator on the seed. The polynomial $\prod_b(X-b)$ for its distinct real eigenvalues $b$ annihilates the seed. It annihilates all of $F\otimes M$, because the seed includes $F\otimes V$, this generates $F\otimes M$ under the diagonal Lie action, and the polynomial in $A$ commutes with that action. The generation assertion follows by applying $1\otimes E_{ij}=(1\otimes E_{ij}+dF(e_{ij})\otimes1)-dF(e_{ij})\otimes1$ inductively to all finite-factor basis vectors times a generator. The Lagrange polynomials now give projections
$$
 P_b=\prod_{a\ne b}\frac{A-a}{b-a}.
$$
Their sum is the identity and their images are the eigenspaces. This is the finite decomposition used before computing the partial trace.
