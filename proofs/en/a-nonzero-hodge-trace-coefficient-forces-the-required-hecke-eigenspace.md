# A nonzero Hodge trace coefficient forces the required Hecke eigenspace

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Consider the smooth projective fiber powers of the universal abelian scheme for a Harris–Taylor unitary datum as in the geometric plan. Use the project hypotheses `Sc`, including odd rank, a single distinguished embedding in the PEL datum and a single archimedean place with one positive direction on the unitary side. Fix the nonnegative dominant weights $\lambda$, a level prime to the PEL auxiliary prime, and a family of Hecke functions $\psi_j$ and a test function $f$ given by the geometric spectral input. Its precise conditions are that the $\psi_j$ commute on the Hecke span generated from $f$, and that for every finite polynomial in Hecke words $P$ with $P(\theta)\ne0$, some further polynomial $Q$ makes $Q(\psi)P(\psi)f$ an admissible regular test with nonzero trace on the stated definite-unitary automorphic weight space. For every geometric exponent list $h=(h_i)$ attached to $\lambda$ and the compatible embedding, the common eigensystem $\theta$ occurs in $H^b(Z,\Omega_Z^{h_i})$ for some $b$ and every $i$. The fiber power can be chosen uniformly over the finitely many coefficient-conjugate weight lists and exponent lists. This is the Hodge-occurrence part of the plan-to-tower proof. ## The trace identity

For each list $h$, a fixed finite linear combination $D_h$ of pullbacks by diagonal endomorphisms of the fiber power is chosen, with a sufficiently large fiber exponent. The group-side weight extraction gives this combination so that, for any admissible regular test $f_1$ at the chosen level,
$$
\sum_{a=0}^{d_Z}(-y)^a
    \sum_{b=0}^{d_Z}(-1)^b
      \operatorname{tr}\!\left(T(f_1) D_h\mid H^b(Z,\Omega_Z^a)\right)
=\operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{def}}(f_1)\sum_i y^{h_i},
$$
where $d_Z=\dim Z$ and $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{def}}$ is the definite-unitary trace for the chosen weight and fixed local factors. The geometric input consists of the finite étale holomorphic Lefschetz formula and the identification and derivatives of the isolated fixed points. The group-side input consists of the weighted fixed-point calculation and the trace identity for the definite unitary space. The Lean theorem uses these inputs to obtain the displayed identity as follows. [Group-side weight extraction](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/LefschetzSigmaSums/HeckeTraceSemi.lean#L122) · [Trace identity from the two sides](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/LefschetzSigmaSums/HodgeTraceOfLefschetzAndGroupSide.lean#L48)

First take one double coset of a regular test and one term of the fixed finite combination $D_h$. At a deeper level the Hecke correspondence is represented by two finite étale maps, composing the second map with the selected diagonal endomorphism. Its operator on Hodge cohomology is pullback along the second map and trace along the first, divided by the relevant finite positive level index. At each fixed point let $A$ denote the tangent operator of the second map relative to the first. The coefficient for $H^\bullet(Z,\Omega_Z^a)$ in holomorphic Lefschetz is the coefficient of $X^a$ in $\det(1+XA)$, divided by $\det(1-A)$. The fixed points are isolated and the denominators are nonzero by the regularity and the fixed-point input. Multiplying by $(-y)^a$ and summing over $a$ changes the numerator into $\det(1-yA)$. The calculation of the fixed-point sum identifies the result with the weighted group-side sum multiplied by the same level index. Dividing by the index cancels it. [Fixed-point and tangent input](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/LefschetzSigmaSums/HodgeTraceFromLefschetzAndGroupSide.lean#L125) · [Double-coset calculation](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/LefschetzSigmaSums/PGeoStep.lean#L16)

Linearity in the finitely many double cosets of $f_1$ and in the chosen diagonal combination gives the group-side functional on the entire test. Its value is the definite-unitary trace times $\sum_i y^{h_i}$ by the group-side identity. This proves the displayed formula. The distinctness of the $h_i$ follows from the dominant weight condition in the exponent formula. The fiber power can be chosen before the test: the trace input has a bound for each list, and the weight tuples obtained by coefficient conjugation are among finitely many permutations of the finitely many embeddings. Each has finitely many allowed exponent lists, so take the largest bound. ## Isolating the eigensystem

Fix $i$ and let $W_b=H^b(Z,\Omega_Z^{h_i})$ for $0\le b\le d_Z$. In each $W_b$, take the span of all vectors $T(\psi_{j_1})\cdots T(\psi_{j_s})T(f)x$. The Hecke operators preserve the span and commute there: the corresponding equality for adjacent factors in the Hecke words of the spectral hypothesis is carried to cohomology by the Hecke algebra action. These spans are finite-dimensional. Their direct sum has finitely many joint generalized eigensystems. One can see this by successively decomposing into generalized eigenspaces of commuting operators. Each nontrivial refinement increases the number of nonzero summands and thus there can be only finitely many refinements.

For each joint eigensystem $\alpha\ne\theta$, choose a coordinate $j$ with $\alpha_j\ne\theta_j$ and take a large power of the linear factor $(T(\psi_j)-\alpha_j I)/(\theta_j-\alpha_j)$. Choose the power at least the dimension of the direct sum. The product $P$ of these finitely many factors has $P(\theta)=1$ and kills every joint generalized eigenspace with eigenvalues different from $\theta$. Thus whenever $P(T)T(f)\ne0$ on a $W_b$, a nonzero vector in its image lies in the $\theta$-generalized eigenspace for the commuting operators. That space contains a common genuine eigenvector with eigenvalues $\theta_j$, by simultaneous triangularization. This is the linear-algebra assertion applied by the Lean proof. [Spectral isolation](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/LefschetzSigmaSums/PEigOf.lean#L55)

By the spectral hypothesis choose a further word polynomial $Q$ so that $f_1=Q(\psi)P(\psi)f$ is an admissible regular test and $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{def}}(f_1)\ne0$. Apply the trace identity to this $f_1$. Its coefficient at $y^{h_i}$ on the right is nonzero, because the exponents are distinct. Therefore the alternating sum on the left in Hodge degree $h_i$ is nonzero. In particular $h_i\le d_Z$ and some $b$ has $\operatorname{tr}(T(f_1)D_h\mid W_b)\ne0$. This forces $T(f_1)\ne0$ on $W_b$. Using $T(f_1)=Q(T)P(T)T(f)$, we get $P(T)T(f)\ne0$, and hence the required common eigenvector in $W_b$. [Taking the coefficient](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/LefschetzSigmaSums/PCoeffOcc.lean#L10) · [Applying the spectral witness](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/LefschetzSigmaSums/PHodgeEig.lean#L6)

Finally, base change along the allowed embedding identifies the complex fiber power and its Hecke correspondences with the ones used in this calculation. Conjugation of coefficients changes the weight and the eigenvalues by the same field automorphism, while the integral-valued Hecke functions remain fixed. Thus the occurrence of the stated conjugate eigensystem is exactly the Hodge test required by the plan.
