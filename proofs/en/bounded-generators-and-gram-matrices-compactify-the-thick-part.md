# Bounded generators and Gram matrices compactify the thick part

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E/F,W,h,G$ and $M=(P_w)$ be as in [the short-vector bound](a-shortest-vector-has-bounded-index-in-its-saturated-line.md). Fix a dual index bound $D$ and a lower length bound $t>0$. There exist finitely many lattices $\mathcal L$ in $W$ and a compact set $K\subseteq G$ such that for every full integral $\mathcal O_E$-lattice $\Lambda$ with $[\Lambda^\#:\Lambda]\le D$, and every $g\in G$ with $\ell_g(x)\ge t$ for nonzero $x\in\Lambda$, there is a rational isometry $\gamma$ of $h$ with $\gamma\Lambda\in\mathcal L$ and $\gamma g\in K$. The finite set and compact set are uniform over both $\Lambda$ and $g$. ## Bound the Euclidean covolumes

Put $d=[E:\mathbb Q]\dim_E W$, and use the nondegenerate rational symmetric form $T(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb Q}h(x,y)$. Its nondegeneracy follows from that of $h$ and of the field trace pairing, by testing along scalar multiples of a vector with nonzero pairing. Let $\Lambda^T=\{z:T(z,\Lambda)\subseteq\mathbb Z\}$. Since $\Lambda$ is stable under $\mathcal O_E$, $h(z,x)$ for $z\in\Lambda^T$ and $x\in\Lambda$ belongs to the trace-dual of $\mathcal O_E$. Choose one positive integer $q$ carrying that fractional ideal into $\mathcal O_E$. Then $q\Lambda^T\subseteq\Lambda^\#$. The dual-index bound gives $D!\Lambda^\#\subseteq\Lambda$, hence $k\Lambda^T\subseteq\Lambda$ for $k=qD!$.

If $B$ is the Gram matrix of $T$ in a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $\Lambda$, then the coordinates of $k$ times the $T$-dual basis form an integer matrix $C$ with $CB=kI_d$, up to transposing the choice of row or column coordinates. Thus $\lvert\det B\rvert\le k^d$ since $\det C$ is a nonzero integer. Relative to a fixed rational basis of $W$, a basis change with matrix $A$ multiplies $|\det T|$ by $(\det A)^2$ and the Euclidean lattice covolume by $|\det A|$. The determinant bound therefore gives a common upper bound for Euclidean lattice covolume in the archimedean coordinates. Applying $g^{-1}$ there has real absolute determinant $1$, since $g_w^*H_wg_w=H_w$ gives $|\det_{\mathbb C} g_w|=1$. Applying the fixed square root $P_w^{1/2}$ at each place multiplies the covolume by a fixed constant. The Euclidean lengths after these two maps are precisely $\ell_g$, so these transported lattices have uniformly bounded covolume and shortest length at least $t$. ## Get a bounded integral basis

In a real Euclidean lattice $L$ of dimension $d>0$, choose a shortest nonzero vector $v$. Then $L\cap\mathbb R v=\mathbb Z v$, so $v$ is primitive and extends to a lattice basis. Orthogonal projection onto $v^\perp$ gives a lattice $L'$ with $\operatorname{covol}(L)=|v|\operatorname{covol}(L')$, by taking determinants in that basis. For each nonzero $y\in L'$, choose a lift to $L$ and subtract an integer multiple of $v$ so its component along $v$ has length at most $|v|/2$. The lift has length at least $|v|$ by shortestness, so $|y|\ge\sqrt3|v|/2$, in particular $|y|\ge t/2$.

The injection of an open Euclidean cube into a lattice quotient, as in [the saturation-index proof](a-shortest-vector-has-bounded-index-in-its-saturated-line.md#control-the-index-using-the-shortest-vector), gives a positive lower bound for $\operatorname{covol}(L')$ from $t/2$ and $d-1$, with covolume $1$ in dimension zero. Hence the upper bound on $\operatorname{covol}(L)$ bounds $|v|$ from above. Also $\operatorname{covol}(L')\le\operatorname{covol}(L)/t$. Induct on $d$ to choose a bounded $\mathbb Z$-basis of $L'$, and lift each vector with component along $v$ of length at most $|v|/2$. The lifts together with $v$ generate $L$ because the kernel of projection is $\mathbb Z v$, and they are independent. This yields a bound depending only on dimension, $t$ and the upper covolume. Applying this proof to the transported lattices gives $\mathbb Z$-bases $e_1,\ldots,e_d$ of $\Lambda$ with $\ell_g(e_i)\le C_0$ for one fixed $C_0$. ## Classify the bounded bases and locate their translates

Every $h(e_i,e_j)$ is an algebraic integer. In a place $w$, the matrix $P_w^{-1/2}H_wP_w^{-1/2}$ is a Hermitian involution. Hence Cauchy–Schwarz bounds $|w(h(e_i,e_j))|$ by $\ell_g(e_i)\ell_g(e_j)\le C_0^2$. The conjugate embedding has the same bound. The image of $\mathcal O_E$ in its archimedean coordinates is a lattice, so there are only finitely many possibilities for each entry and hence for the whole Hermitian Gram matrix.

Two such ordered bases with the same Gram matrix are carried to one another by an $E$-linear isometry. Indeed each basis spans $W$ over $E$. Pairing a relation among the first family with all its vectors shows the same relation among the second family, by nondegeneracy and the common Gram matrix. This gives a well-defined surjective $E$-linear map, which preserves $h$. It also matches the two integral lattices. Choose one representative ordered basis for each Gram matrix and let $\mathcal L$ consist of their lattices.

For any representative $e^0$, the elements $g'\in G$ satisfying $\ell_{g'}(e^0_i)\le C_0$ form a compact set. At each place, the vectors $e^0_i$ span, so the bounds on their images under $g'^{-1}$ bound its matrix entries. The equations defining the unitary group cut out a closed set among all matrices, since $A^*H_w A=H_w$ forces invertibility. The bounded inverse matrices form a closed bounded subset of a finite-dimensional real space. Inversion is continuous there, so the $g'$ form a compact set. Take $K$ to be the finite union over representatives. The isometry $\gamma$ that takes the original basis to its representative satisfies $(\gamma g)^{-1}(\gamma e_i)=g^{-1}e_i$ place by place. Thus $\gamma g\in K$ and $\gamma\Lambda\in\mathcal L$, as required.
