# Tame inertia makes the first-block Euler factors units

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix a nonzero twist index of residual characteristic $\ell$. Let $O_0\to O$ be the local embedding of coefficient valuation rings, with the induced embedding of residue fields. Write $G$ for the Galois group used in the twist data over the CM field, and $c$ for the conjugation automorphism on $G$. The twist data give a character $\delta:G\to O_0^\times$ with $\delta(cgc^{-1})=\delta(g)^{-1}$ in the usual conjugation notation. Choose the designated tame inertia element $z$ at a twist prime, with $\delta(z)^2-1\in O_0^\times$. Thus both $\overline{\delta(z)}$ and its inverse differ from $1$.

**Toric assertion.** For each toric character $\lambda$, the residual character $\bar\lambda$ is nontrivial at $z$. Consequently there is an open normal subgroup $H_\lambda$ such that a Frobenius conjugate to $z$ modulo $H_\lambda$ has $\bar\lambda(\operatorname{Frob}_P)\ne1$.

Use the local toric comparison at the twist prime. For this comparison there is a choice $\delta_*=\delta(z)^{\pm1}$ and a nonnegative integer $b$ such that $(\lambda(z)/\delta_*)^{q^b}=1$, where $q$ is the norm of the twist prime. The tame element condition also says that some power $z^m$ with $\gcd(m,q)=1$ lies in $\ker\delta$, by applying its prime-to-$q$ order condition to that open normal subgroup. If $\bar\lambda(z)=1$, reducing the local comparison gives $\overline{\delta(z)}^{\,q^b}=1$. The other relation gives $\overline{\delta(z)}^{\,m}=1$. Since these exponents are coprime, Bézout's identity gives $\overline{\delta(z)}=1$, a contradiction. Set $H_\lambda=\ker\bar\lambda$, which is open by continuity and normal since it is a kernel. The character is constant on conjugacy classes in this quotient. This proves the assertion and supplies the first-block unit hypothesis in the [toric factorization](removing-auxiliary-primes-gives-the-toric-polynomial-factor.md).

**Hecke assertion.** For each member $s$ of the finite set of good signatures, assume the specified Galois representation $\rho_s$ has the auxiliary character $\eta_s$ supplied by the character twist comparison. Put $W_s=\eta_s\rho_s$. On inertia at each relevant twist place the local comparison says that $W_s$ is the scalar representation $\delta$ or $\delta^{-1}$. At an allowed split prime $P$, take the Frobenius convention of the source and write $\sigma$ for the designated Frobenius lift. The same comparison identifies the scalar Hecke expression at $1$ with

$$
1+\sum_{m=1}^{r}(-1)^m(\mathrm NP)^{m(m-1)/2}h_{s,m}(P)
=\det(1-W_s(\sigma^{-1})).
$$

There is a single open normal subgroup $H$ such that all these expressions are units for a first-block pair whose representative has Frobenius conjugate to $z$ modulo $H$. This holds for either orientation of the pair.

To prove this, let $H_0=\bigcap_s\ker\bar W_s$ and let $H=H_0\cap c^{-1}(H_0)$, where in the pullback $c$ denotes the conjugation automorphism. The kernels are open, since reduction of each continuous integral matrix representation takes values in the discrete group of residual matrices. There are finitely many signatures, so $H$ is open and normal. If $g\sigma g^{-1}z^{-1}\in H$, then $\bar W_s(g\sigma g^{-1})=\bar W_s(z)$. The latter is a scalar from the local inertia comparison. On taking inverses, conjugation by $\bar W_s(g)$ does not change the scalar, so $\bar W_s(\sigma^{-1})=\bar t I_r$, where $t$ is $\delta(z)$ or its inverse.

For the conjugate prime, a Frobenius is conjugate to the image of $\sigma$ under the conjugation automorphism. Pulling the congruence through that automorphism uses the second factor in the definition of $H$ and gives the corresponding congruence to the conjugate of $z$ modulo $H_0$. The conjugate of $z$ lies in the conjugate inertia group and its $\delta$-value is inverted. The same argument thus gives $\bar W_s(\sigma^{-1})=\bar t I_r$ with the same two possible scalars. In either orientation the displayed determinant reduces to $(1-\bar t)^r\ne0$. Hence it is a unit. The signature-to-eigensystem condition says that every simultaneous residual Hecke eigensystem on the selected lattice uses the values of some such signature. This gives the first-block unit hypothesis in the [Hecke factorization](removing-auxiliary-primes-factors-the-hecke-determinant.md).
