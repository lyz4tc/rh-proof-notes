# The finite Fourier transform exchanges a lattice and its annihilator

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $F$ be a number field, $\mathbb A_f$ its finite adeles and $\widehat{\mathcal O}_F$ the product of its finite integer rings. Let $\psi_f$ be the standard additive character, with $\psi_f(b)=\exp(-2\pi i\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb Q} b)$ on $F$. Fix an invertible symmetric $d\times d$ matrix $B$ over $F$. On $V=\mathbb A_f^d$ use $\chi(x,y)=\psi_f(x^{\mathsf T}By)$. Use additive Haar measure $d_f x$ with
$$
 d_f x(\widehat{\mathcal O}_F^d)=|\operatorname{disc}(F)|^{-d/2}
             |\mathrm N_{F/\mathbb Q}\det B|^{-1/2}.
\tag{1}
$$
For a Schwartz–Bruhat function $\phi$, meaning locally constant and compactly supported, define
$$
 (\mathcal F\phi)(y)=\int_V\phi(x)\chi(x,y)\,d_f x .
$$
Then $\mathcal F\phi$ is Schwartz–Bruhat, $\mathcal F^2\phi(x)=\phi(-x)$, and $\|\mathcal F\phi\|_2=\|\phi\|_2$. The transform of $1_{a+L}$ for any compact open additive subgroup $L\subset V$ is
$$
 d_f x(L)\,\chi(a,y)\,1_{L^\perp}(y),
\tag{2}
$$
where $L^\perp=\{y:\chi(x,y)=1\ \forall x\in L\}$. This is the finite Fourier normalization used in the modular transformation of theta. ## Determine the lattice measure

For $F$ viewed inside the finite adeles, the annihilator of $\widehat{\mathcal O}_F$ under $(x,y)\mapsto\psi_f(xy)$ is $\widehat{\mathfrak d_F^{-1}}$, the finite completion of the inverse different. For rational $y$, this follows by testing all elements of $\mathcal O_F$, dense in $\widehat{\mathcal O}_F$ by the Chinese remainder theorem. The condition on them is exactly $\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb Q}(y\mathcal O_F)\subseteq\mathbb Z$, the defining condition of the inverse different. For arbitrary finite $y$, work in $N^{-1}\widehat{\mathcal O}_F$ for some positive integer $N$ containing it, and also large enough to contain the inverse different there. The annihilator contains $\widehat{\mathcal O}_F$, since $\psi_f$ is trivial on it by density and integrality of the trace. Therefore membership in the annihilator on $N^{-1}\widehat{\mathcal O}_F$ is determined modulo $\widehat{\mathcal O}_F$, where each residue class has a rational representative $b\in N^{-1}\mathcal O_F$. The trace condition on these representatives proves the assertion. The rational classes also show that $\psi_f$ is uniquely specified by its rational values, since $F$ is dense in $\mathbb A_f$. [Trace criterion for the rational dual lattice](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/CompanionTheory/SpectralGeometry/LocalGlobalProofs/AdelicAnalysis/DualLatticeMembershipTrace.lean#L36)

It follows that the annihilator of $\widehat{\mathcal O}_F^d$ is $B^{-1}\widehat{\mathfrak d_F^{-1}}^{\,d}$. With the measure initially giving $\widehat{\mathcal O}_F^d$ volume one, the inverse different has relative volume $|\operatorname{disc}(F)|$ in one coordinate. This is the index of the integral basis in its trace-dual lattice. The change $B^{-1}$ on the finite adeles has modulus $|\mathrm N_{F/\mathbb Q}\det B|$, because the product of the finite absolute values of a rational scalar is the reciprocal of its archimedean absolute norm. Thus (1) is exactly the normalization making the product of the volumes of the standard integral group and its annihilator equal to one. For the groups $N\widehat{\mathcal O}_F^d$ and $N^{-1}\widehat{\mathcal O}_F^d$ the same property follows by reciprocal scaling.

Every compact open additive subgroup $L$ is between such two groups. The induced pairing between the finite quotients obtained by reversing the inclusions under annihilator is perfect. One justification is to use the trace dual bases on rational representatives of the finite quotients. The induced pairing has zero kernel on each side by the trace-dual criterion, and both sides have cardinalities given by the corresponding lattice indices. Equivalently, the finite group character sum for a subgroup shows that if its annihilator is taken in the opposite quotient, the two indices multiply to the size of the full quotient. This gives $(L^\perp)^\perp=L$ and $d_f x(L)d_f x(L^\perp)=1$ by comparing indices. In particular the annihilator is compact and open. ## Transform a finite partition of the support

For (2), translate $a+L$ to $L$. The resulting integral over $L$ is its volume if $y\in L^\perp$. If not, translation by some $l\in L$ multiplies the integral by $\chi(l,y)\ne1$, so it vanishes. Every Schwartz–Bruhat function is a finite linear combination of indicators of cosets of some $N\widehat{\mathcal O}_F^d$. Indeed local constancy and compact support give a common open subgroup of translations under which it is invariant, and compactness makes the quotient of the support by that subgroup finite. The groups $N\widehat{\mathcal O}_F^d$ form a neighborhood base at zero. Therefore (2) gives the Schwartz–Bruhat property after transformation. Transforming (2) once more produces $1_{-a+L}$ since the two measures multiply to one and the two characters combine into the character at $a+x$. This proves inversion by linearity. For Plancherel, use a single $L$ and a finite combination of distinct coset indicators. The transformed characters on $L^\perp$ are mutually orthogonal: if the two cosets differ, their difference is outside $(L^\perp)^\perp=L$. The squared norm of each transformed indicator is $d_f x(L)^2d_f x(L^\perp)=d_f x(L)$, the original squared norm. This proves the equality for the combination.
